# Part 0: The Master Course Web — How Chapters 1–8 Interconnect

Every EMS call is a single, unbroken chain where system architecture, scene safety, law, communication, biomechanics, anatomy, and cellular physiology operate simultaneously. Use these four core bridges to see how every chapter feeds into the others:

## Bridge 1: The Clinical Perfusion Engine (Ch. 7 ↔ Ch. 8 ↔ Ch. 6 ↔ Ch. 1)
- **Structure Dictates Failure:** Upper/lower airway anatomy, pleural linings, heart valves, and blood vessels (**Ch. 7**) physically govern **airway resistance**, **compliance**, **Boyle’s law**, **dead air space**, **preload (Frank-Starling)**, **afterload**, and **SVR** (**Ch. 8**).
- **Cellular Survival Drives Priorities:** Every EMT primary assessment priority (**Ch. 1**) exists to protect the **11 components of perfusion** (**Ch. 8**) so cells stay in **aerobic metabolism** (high ATP) rather than sliding into **anaerobic metabolism** (lactic acid buildup → **Na+/K+ pump failure** → cell swelling, rupture, and organ failure).
- **Positioning Directly Modifies Physiology:** Placing a pregnant patient (`>20 weeks`) on her left side (**Ch. 6**) lifts the uterus off the **inferior vena cava** (**Ch. 7**), restoring **venous return (64% reservoir)**, **preload**, and **cardiac output** (**Ch. 8**). Elevating a dyspneic patient in **Fowler’s position (`45°–60°`)** (**Ch. 6 & 7**) lowers abdominal pressure on the **diaphragm (`60–70%` of breathing effort)** (**Ch. 7 & 8**). Avoiding **Trendelenburg** (**Ch. 7**) prevents abdominal organs from impairing diaphragmatic descent and raising ICP.
- **Lifespan Anatomy Alters Decompensation:** A child's **4–5 mm** pliable trachea, **cricoid narrowing (`<6–8 yrs`)**, **2–3×** metabolic rate, and inability to raise stroke volume (**Ch. 7**) mean minor airway swelling or blood loss (`>30%` sudden crash) triggers rapid hypoxia and **anaerobic collapse** (**Ch. 8**). Conversely, an older adult's **cardiac hypertrophy**, **arteriosclerosis**, blunted **baroreceptors/chemoreceptors**, and **kyphosis (`2 of 3`)** (**Ch. 7**) impair hemodynamic compensation (**Ch. 8**) and require extra void padding during packaging (**Ch. 6**).

## Bridge 2: The Authority, Consent & Legal Defense Pipeline (Ch. 1 ↔ Ch. 3 ↔ Ch. 4 ↔ Ch. 5)
- **Chain of Practice:** The **State Lead EMS Agency** licenses you (**Ch. 1**), the physician **Medical Director** delegates clinical authority via **off-line protocols/standing orders** and **on-line direction** (**Ch. 1 & 3**), **scope of practice** defines what is legal while **standard of care** defines expected quality (**Ch. 3**).
- **Consent Requires Communication & Documentation:** Valid **expressed, implied, minor, or involuntary consent**—or honoring a valid **DNR / POLST** (**Ch. 3**)—relies on plain-language **therapeutic communication** (**Ch. 5**) and airtight **PCR documentation** (**Ch. 4**).
- **Refusals & Handoffs Are Peak Lawsuit Traps:** Preventing **negligence** (all 4 elements) and **intentional torts** (**abandonment, assault, battery, false imprisonment, defamation**) (**Ch. 3**) requires verifying medical **capacity**, consulting **on-line medical direction** using **closed-loop ("echo") communication** (**Ch. 1 & 5**), executing the **8-step refusal protocol** (**Ch. 3 & 4**), delivering **both verbal (SBAR/oral) and written reports** (**Ch. 1, 4, 5**), and obeying **HIPAA** and **COBRA/EMTALA** (**Ch. 3 & 4**).
- **Clinical Precision Drives Reimbursement & Science:** Using accurate **anatomical and medical terminology** (**Ch. 7**) to document objective findings, **pertinent negatives**, and **primary working impressions** (**Ch. 4**) proves **medical necessity** (**Prudent Layperson**, **Medicare 42 CFR § 410.40(d)**, **Medicaid**, **ET3**) while feeding **NEMSIS** and **CARES** to power **Evidence-Based Guidelines (EBGs)** and **CQI** (**Ch. 1 & 4**).

## Bridge 3: The Safety, Biomechanics & Longevity Shield (Ch. 1 ↔ Ch. 2 ↔ Ch. 6)
- **Hierarchy of Safety (`Self → Crew → Patient → Bystanders`):** Personal safety (**Ch. 1**) is operationalized through **OSHA Standard Precautions**, **PPE**, **6-step CDC hand hygiene**, **7-step safe glove removal**, **ANSI/ISEA roadway vests**, **DOT ERG hazmat scouting**, and **staging for law enforcement** (**Ch. 2**).
- **Physical & Mental Longevity:** Protecting your spine with the **4 principles of body mechanics**, **power lift/grip**, and **powered/wheeled devices** (**Ch. 6**) pairs directly with the **5 components of physical fitness** (`70%` target HR), **8–10 hours of sleep** (`<24-hr` NASEMSO shifts), **CISM (defusing/CISD)**, and **resilience** against burnout and PTSD (**Ch. 2**).

## Bridge 4: End-to-End Integrated Call Architecture
1. **Dispatch & En Route:** PSAP/E-911/EMD/AACN (**Ch. 1 & 5**) → acknowledge via radio/MDT using synchronous military time (**Ch. 4 & 5**).
2. **Scene Size-Up & Safety:** Stage for police/hazmat if unsafe (**Ch. 1, 2, 3**); don **Standard Precautions PPE** (**Ch. 2**); preserve crime-scene evidence if suspected (**Ch. 2 & 3**).
3. **Patient Contact & Consent:** Project the **Three Cs**, use open posture/personal distance (`1½–4 ft`), obtain **informed consent** or apply **implied/minor/involuntary consent** or verify physical **DNR/POLST** (**Ch. 3 & 5**).
4. **Assessment & Pathophysiology:** Primary assessment resolves immediate threats to the **11 perfusion components** (**Ch. 1 & 8**); use **open/closed questions** without leading (**Ch. 5**); interpret vital signs, skin (**α1** vasoconstriction/sweat), pulse pressure (`<25%`), and lifespan differences (**Ch. 7 & 8**).
5. **Treatment, Packaging & Moving:** Follow **standing orders** or request **on-line medical direction** via **SBAR** + **echo repeat-back** (**Ch. 1, 3, 5**); select **emergency, urgent, or nonurgent move** and matched device using **body mechanics** and clinical positioning (**Ch. 6 & 7**).
6. **Transport, Handoff & Documentation:** Give pre-arrival radio report (no patient names, **30-sec pause**, **1-sec PTT**); reassess en route (**>=2 complete vitals sets**); deliver **oral handoff** + **NEMSIS/DOT-compliant PCR** (**Ch. 1, 4, 5**).
7. **Post-Call Recovery & System Improvement:** Safe **7-step glove removal**, **20-sec hand wash**, **cleaning/disinfecting/sterilization** (**Ch. 2**); **CISM defusing/CISD** if critical incident (**Ch. 2**); **CQI** audit and **EBM/EBG** feedback loop (**Ch. 1 & 4**).

# Chapter 1: Emergency Medical Service Systems, Research, and Public Health

## Master Key Terms (Chapter 1)
- **Provider Levels:** Emergency Medical Responder (EMR), Emergency Medical Technician (EMT), Advanced Emergency Medical Technician (AEMT), Paramedic
- **System & Delivery Models:** EMS system, prehospital care, Mobile Integrated Healthcare (MIH), Community Paramedicine (CP), quality improvement (QI)
- **Medical Oversight & Direction:** medical oversight, direct medical oversight, indirect medical oversight, medical director, medical direction, off-line medical direction, on-line medical direction, on-scene medical direction, protocols, standing orders
- **Research & Legal:** evidence-based medicine, evidence-based guidelines (EBG), Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)

## 1.1: The Emergency Medical Services (EMS) System
- **Health Care Continuum & Dual Foundation:** Positions prehospital emergency care within the broader continuum from sudden illness/injury onset through hospital stabilization, rehabilitation, or discharge—combining immediate out-of-hospital clinical care with system organization, evidence-based guidelines, quality improvement, and population-level public health integration. Catastrophic accidents and sudden illnesses cause thousands of preventable deaths and permanent disabilities annually when EMS is inadequate.
- **Historical Evolution:**
  - Early ambulances operated out of local funeral homes, prioritizing rapid transport over active field care.
  - *Korean & Vietnam Conflicts:* Proved immediate field stabilization before transport dramatically improved survival.
  - *Iraq & Afghanistan Conflicts:* Advanced civilian trauma care via battlefield resuscitation and hemorrhage-control lessons.
  - *Legislative & Policy Milestones:* **1960s–1970s** CPR developed & AHA public BLS training; **1966** *Accidental Death and Disability* ("White Paper") & **Highway Safety Act** (DOT/NHTSA); **1973** **EMS System Act** (regional planning/funding); **1993** NREMT Education and Practice Blueprint; **1996 & 2000** NHTSA *EMS Agendas for the Future*; **2005–2006** *National EMS Core Content*, *National EMS Scope of Practice Model* (4 levels), and IOM *EMS at the Crossroads* (reciprocity, paramedic accreditation, national certification); **2014–2018** *EMS Agenda 2050* (30-year people-centered vision).
- **State Governance (NASEMSO) & NHTSA's 10 Technical Assistance Program Assessment Standards:**
  - Individual states regulate EMS independently via a designated **lead state EMS agency** responsible for statewide (1) **planning**, (2) **coordination**, (3) **regulation**, and (4) **licensing** of local EMS agencies and individual personnel (distinct from national NREMT certification).
  - *NHTSA 10 Standards:* (1) Regulation and policy; (2) resource management; (3) human resources and training (at least EMT level); (4) transportation (ground/air); (5) facilities; (6) communications; (7) public information and education; (8) medical direction; (9) trauma systems; (10) evaluation (QI).
  - *Gunderson (2015):* **6 System Design Goals** (clinical quality, service quality, economic efficiency, accountability, improvement, resilience) and **12 Modern Service Lines** (prevention/public education, triage, medical first response, ambulance response/transport, medical transportation, event coverage, disaster services, critical care transport, air medical transport, hazmat medical support, tactical medical support, community paramedicine).
- **Accessing EMS (911, E-911, Wireless & VoIP):**
  - 911 enables universal simultaneous police/fire/EMS dispatch through a **Public Service Answering Point (PSAP)**.
  - Landline **E-911** automatically displays **ANI** (phone number) and **ALI** (physical address)—vital if caller hangs up, is a child, or is incapacitated. **EMDs** give pre-arrival care instructions (CPR, bleeding control).
  - **Wireless (~70% of 911 calls, rising) & VoIP:** *Basic 911* (transmits regardless of carrier plan), *Phase I E-911* (callback + cell tower), *Phase II E-911* (latitude/longitude within **50 to 300 meters**), and *VoIP* (callback + registered physical address).
- **Six EMS Service Delivery Models:** **Fire Department EMS** (paid/part-paid/volunteer; transport or first response), **Municipal EMS ("Third Service")** (standalone local government agency), **Private EMS** (municipal contract; 911 + inter-facility), **Hospital-Based EMS** (for-profit/non-profit), **Law Enforcement EMS** (rare; small jurisdictions), and **Public Safety Officer** (cross-trained fire/police/EMS).

### Provider Levels (*National EMS Scope of Practice Model*)

| Provider Level | Core Scope of Practice & Capabilities |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Emergency Medical Responder (EMR)** | Immediate lifesaving care prior to ambulance arrival: basic airway/ventilation/oxygen devices, vital signs, spinal/extremity stabilization, eye irrigation, bleeding control, emergency moves, CPR, AED, and emergency childbirth. |
| **Emergency Medical Technician (EMT)** | Basic emergency care and ambulance transport: builds on EMR skills with advanced oxygen/ventilation equipment, pulse oximetry, automated BP monitors, and limited medication administration/assistance. |
| **Advanced EMT (AEMT)** | Basic plus limited advanced care and transport: adds advanced airway devices, blood glucose monitoring, IV and intraosseous (IO) fluid therapy, and select medications. |
| **Paramedic** | Highest prehospital tier: builds on EMT/AEMT care with advanced assessments, field impressions, invasive procedures, and expanded pharmacology to reduce morbidity and mortality. |

- **Care Settings, Specialty Centers & MIH-CP:** *Prehospital care* (before hospital arrival) vs. *out-of-hospital care* (scene care with/without transport + inter-facility transfers). **10 Specialty Referral Centers:** Trauma, Burn, Obstetrical, Pediatric, Poison, Stroke, Cardiac, Hyperbaric (diving, CO/toxic exposures), Spine Injury, Psychiatric. **Mobile Integrated Healthcare (MIH)** (mobile tech/multi-practitioner out-of-hospital care) & **Community Paramedicine (CP)** (EMS agency clinicians delivering underserved access, telephone triage, chronic-disease/post-discharge follow-ups).

## 1.2: EMT Roles, Medical Oversight, QI & Research
- **Safety Priority #1 (`Self → Crew → Patient → Bystanders`):** Drive defensively with due regard; wear seat belts unless actively treating in back; avoid traffic, downed wires, spills, fires, radiation; **never enter volatile scenes** (riots, active crime, hostage) until law enforcement clears area; wear night high-vis reflective gear, trauma/extrication gear, and infectious PPE.
- **Core Duties, Physical Standards & ADA:** Maintain vehicle/gear readiness; triage → **primary assessment** (fix immediate life threats first) → **secondary assessment** & reassessment; safe lifting/moving; select appropriate facility + early pre-arrival notification; deliver **both verbal and written reports (PCR)** and never leave until formal transfer of care; safeguard valuables, protect privacy, relay scene context. Physical demands: lift/carry up to **125 pounds**, good visual acuity + **good color vision** (detecting cyanosis, pallor, flushing), adequate hearing. **ADA (1990)** prohibits disability employment discrimination and requires reasonable accommodations. Track your own CE, skills, and license renewals.
- **Medical Oversight & Direction:** EMT practices strictly as designated agent of the physician **Medical Director**:
  - *Indirect (Off-Line) Oversight:* Training, QI audits, written **Protocols** (triage, treatment, transport, destination), and **Standing Orders** (preauthorized treatments without calling a physician first—e.g., **Chest Pain Standing Orders**: O2 via NRB at 15 L/min, comfort position, 1 patient-prescribed nitroglycerin if SBP > 90 mmHg, 160–325 mg aspirin, request ALS).
  - *Direct Oversight:* **On-Line Medical Direction** (radio, phone, video telemedicine—e.g., requesting authorization for repeat nitroglycerin) and **On-Scene Medical Direction** (physically on scene).
- **CQI, Error Prevention, EBM & EBGs:**
  - *CQI:* Non-punitive system-level audits across **6 EMT roles** (thorough PCRs, peer reviews/audits, patient/ED feedback, CE, preventive maintenance, regular skill practice). High-risk activities: hand-offs, miscommunication, lifting/drops, ambulance crashes, improper spinal restriction. Root causes: skill failure, protocol violation, knowledge gap. Prevent via clear protocols, lighting, minimal interruptions, med labeling, decision aids, and **closed-loop communication**.
  - *Research & EBGs:* **4-step EBM** (formulate question → search literature → appraise validity → update protocols); **8-step Scientific Method** (question → literature review → hypothesis → study → analyze data → support/reject → limitations → peer-reviewed publication); **NEMSIS** (national standardized EMS data), **CARES** (largest U.S. OHCA registry), **NIGMS**, NHTSA *Safety in Numbers*; **8-Step Cyclical National EBG Model** (1. System inputs → 2. Evidence accumulation → 3. Priorities → 4. Guideline development → 5. Protocol development → 6. Dissemination → 7. Implementation → 8. Outcome evaluation/QI *loops back to Step 2*).

Monitor **5 SDOH Domains** (economic stability, education access/quality, health care access/quality, neighborhood/built environment, social/community context), log EHR surveillance, and refer vulnerable patients. **Primary Prevention** (before onset: DUI/fall/fire/seatbelt/helmet campaigns, flu/COVID-19 vaccines); **Secondary Prevention** (early detection: BP screenings, reporting abuse/unsafe homes, public AEDs, disaster/pandemic planning); **Tertiary Prevention** (acute emergency care, stabilization, transport).

# Chapter 2: Workforce Safety and Wellness of the EMT

## Master Key Terms (Chapter 2)
- **Stress & Mental Health:** burnout, critical incident, critical incident stress debriefing (CISD), defusing
- **Infection Control & PPE:** cleaning, disinfecting, pathogens, personal protective equipment (PPE), purified protein derivative (PPD) tuberculin test, Standard Precautions, sterilization

## 2.1: Emotional Aspects, Grief & Stress Management
- **Death, Dying & Kübler-Ross's Five Emotional Stages** *(non-linear; applies to major nonfatal loss like amputation)*: (1) **Denial** (*"Not me."*), (2) **Anger** (*"Why me?"*), (3) **Bargaining** (*"Okay, but first let me..."*), (4) **Depression** (*"Okay, but I haven't..."*), (5) **Acceptance** (*"Okay, I am not afraid."*).
- **Nine Principles for Dying Patients, Families & Bystanders:** Prioritize care with dignity/respect/privacy/control; speak to unresponsive patients as if alert; show sincerity and allow family presence during resuscitation when appropriate; communicate facts clearly without guessing; permit safe emotional venting without taking anger personally; listen empathetically and stay until ME/coroner arrives; avoid false assurances; use a gentle tone; **clean postmortem blood/secretions and elevate head briefly if allowed—EXCEPT at possible crime scenes** (do not clean body, remove blood, or allow family access until cleared by police/coroner/ME).
- **Stressors, Reactions & CISM:**
  - *High-Stress Calls:* MCIs, abuse/neglect (any age), pediatric emergencies, coworker injury/death, caring for relatives/bystanders, severe trauma (amputations).
  - *3 Reactions:* **Acute** (immediate; evaluate chest pain/SOB medically), **Delayed** (days–years; **PTSD** → mental health care), **Cumulative** (ongoing buildup → **burnout**).
  - *10 Warning Signs & 5 Domains:* Irritability, poor concentration, sleep/nightmares, anxiety, indecisiveness, guilt, appetite loss, low sex drive, isolation, lost work interest across **Thinking, Psychological, Physical, Behavioral, and Social** domains.
  - *Lifestyle & Work:* Cut sugar/caffeine/alcohol, eat small frequent lean-protein meals, exercise, deep breathe/meditate, never self-medicate, use coworker "buddy" system, rotate from high-violence areas, seek mental health care.
  - *CISM:* **Defusing** (**<=8 hours** post-incident, **30–45 minutes**, informal venting) vs. **CISD** (**24–72 hours**, **7 phases** [facts, feelings, signs/symptoms, sorting feelings, coping strategies, return-to-work plan, follow-up], confidential/non-punitive) + **10 CISM Components** (preincident education, on-scene peer support, one-on-one, disaster support, defusing, CISD, follow-up, spouse/family support, community outreach, health/welfare).

## 2.2: Scene Safety, Infection Control & Injury Prevention
- **Golden Boundary Rule:** **Never** act outside your medical responder role unless specifically trained and authorized.
- **Five Pathogen Categories:** **Bacteria** (single-celled, usually antibiotic-treatable), **Viruses** (require host cell, **not** antibiotic-treatable; EIDs = COVID-19, Ebola [EVD], Zika [ZVD]), **Fungi** (immunocompromised), **Protozoa** (e.g., mosquito-borne malaria—infectious but not person-to-person communicable), **Helminths** (parasitic worms like hookworm). Spread via direct contact, indirect contact (needles), or airborne droplets.

- **OSHA Standard Precautions, Hand Hygiene, PPE & Decontamination:**
  - *Exposure Reporting:* Airborne (TB) facility notifies DO within **24 hours**; bloodborne (HIV/HBV) facility reports to DO within **48 hours**.
  - *6-Step CDC Hand Wash (Single Most Important Measure):* Remove jewelry → wet with clean running water, turn off tap, apply soap → lather backs/between fingers/under nails → scrub **>=20 seconds** (`10–15 sec` USPHS) → rinse → dry. Use **>=60%** alcohol sanitizer if water unavailable, then wash ASAP.
  - *PPE & Decontamination:* Treat all used PPE as contaminated; eye shields/side shields; disposable gloves (never reuse; utility gloves for cleaning); gowns for splashes (wash contaminated uniforms separately in hot soapy water **>=25 minutes** + hot shower); surgical masks vs. **HEPA/N-95** (clean-shaven, annual fit test). **Cleaning** (soap/water + friction) → **Disinfecting** (germicide/bleach on intact-skin gear) → **Sterilization** (autoclave killing **all** microbes on invasive/mucous-membrane gear like laryngoscope blades). Double-bag HIV/HBV waste; never recap sharps.
  - *Screening & Immunizations:* Initial-hire PPD TB screen (annual only if risk/exposure); COVID-19, tetanus (Tdap once, then Td/Tdap q**10 years**; Tdap each pregnancy), Hepatitis B (2-dose Heplisav-B or 3-dose Recombivax/Engerix; offered free within **10 days** + titer), annual flu, MMR (born `>=1957`), varicella.
- **Hazmat, Rescue & Violence:** Scout hazmat from afar with binoculars + U.S. DOT *Emergency Response Guidebook*; stay clear until hazmat teams control/decontaminate. Wear **ANSI/ISEA 107-2004 Class 2/3** or **207-2006 PSV** breakaway vests on roadways (`Class 1 <25 mph`, `Class 2 >=25 mph`, `Class 3` high risk). Stage for law enforcement on violent/crime scenes (domestic disputes, hostile intoxication, fights, suicide, clandestine drug labs, "chemical suicides"); preserve crime-scene evidence.

## 2.3: Wellness Principles & Safe Glove Removal
- **6 Wellness Dimensions** (physical, intellectual, emotional, social, environmental, spiritual) & **5 Fitness Components:**
  1. *Cardiovascular Endurance:* **30–40 min, >=3 days/week** at 70% Target HR: `Target HR = [((220 - Age) - RHR) × 0.70] + RHR` (e.g., age 28, RHR 62 = **153 bpm**).
  2. *Muscle Strength*, (3) *Muscle Endurance*, (4) *Muscle Flexibility*, (5) *Body Composition* (lower fat lowers heart disease, HTN, diabetes).
- **Sleep, Mental Health & 7-Step Glove Removal:** Need **8–10 hours** sleep/day (half of EMS workers get `<6 hours`); **5 NASEMSO Fatigue Guidelines** (validated surveys, shifts **<24 hours**, caffeine access, on-duty napping, fatigue education). Avoid smoking and alcohol/drug coping (**EAP**). **>30%** of first responders have a mental health diagnosis; **resilience** (NAEMT) counters rising EMS suicide rates (untreated **PTSD** = **6×** suicide risk; hotline **1-800-273-8255**). **7-Step Glove Removal:** Contaminated touches contaminated only; clean touches clean only (hook outer cuff → pull inside out → crumple in gloved hand → bare fingers under clean inner cuff → invert off → drop in biohazard).

# Chapter 3: Medical, Legal, and Ethical Issues

## Master Key Terms (Chapter 3)
- **Foundations & Duties:** scope of practice, standard of care, duty to act, Good Samaritan law
- **Consent & Refusal:** consent, informed consent, expressed consent, implied consent, minor consent, involuntary consent
- **Advance Directives:** advance directive, living will, durable power of attorney, do not resuscitate (DNR) order, physician orders for life-sustaining treatment (POLST), medical orders for life-sustaining treatment (MOLST)
- **Liability & Torts:** tort, negligence, proximate cause, intentional tort, abandonment, assault, battery, false imprisonment, defamation, slander, libel
- **Privacy & Federal Regulations:** Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (COBRA), Emergency Medical Treatment and Active Labor Act (EMTALA)

## 3.1: Scope, Standards, Duties, Protections & Ethics
- **Scope of Practice vs. Standard of Care:** **Scope of practice** (state law, NHTSA model/standards) defines what is *legally allowed* (oxygen = legal; suturing = illegal). **Standard of care** ("reasonable person standard") evaluates (1) right assessment/care and (2) proper performance—falling below = **negligence**.
- **Duty to Act (5 Dimensions):** On-duty obligation; off-duty assumed duty if publicly responding or stopping at a scene (cannot leave until equal/higher training takes over; responsible for picked-up patient property); duties to **patient** (advocate/privacy/do no harm), **self** (credentials/skills/fitness), **partner** (report alcohol/drug impairment to supervisor), and **equipment** (malfunction/absence = negligence).
- **Legal Protections & Ethics:** **Good Samaritan laws** (CA **1959**) protect unpaid good-faith care unless **gross negligence** (does *not* prevent being sued). **Sovereign immunity** protects government EMS only (not private EMS). **Statute of limitations** (state filing window); **Contributory negligence** (patient contributed to own harm). **Ethics** (study of morality) vs. **Morals** (right/wrong) vs. **NAEMT Code of Ethics (1978)**; resolve legal/ethical/moral conflicts via protocols, laws, and medical direction.

## 3.2: Patient Consent, Advance Directives & Refusal of Care
- **5-Step Consent Approach:** Introduce yourself → state intent to assess/treat/transport → ask if patient understands/has questions → request explicit permission (even if they called 911) → listen without assuming blanket consent. Treating/touching without consent = **battery**.
- **Four Consent Types:**
  1. *Expressed Consent:* Conscious legal-age adult with rational capacity after understandable risk disclosure (verbal preferred; nodding acceptable).
  2. *Implied Consent (Emergency Doctrine):* True emergency when patient is unresponsive, irrational, or becomes incapacitated after refusal.
  3. *Minor Consent:* Parent/legal guardian/authorized caregiver (`<18` or `<21` per state). **6 Emergent Minor Implied Consent Conditions:** Emergent threat, guardian unavailable, cannot safely delay, treat immediate threats only, act in minor's best interest when in doubt, contact medical direction. **Emancipated minor** (married, pregnant, parent, armed forces, financially independent living away, court decree) needs no parent consent.
  4. *Involuntary Consent:* Mentally incompetent adult or in law enforcement custody/incarcerated → third party with legal authority (guardian, police, court officer).
- **End-of-Life Advance Directives (*Patient Self-Determination Act*):**
  - *Living Will:* Terminal illness or permanent coma; requires **2 physicians** to confirm incapacity and terminal/comatose state; may still allow insulin/antibiotics/food/pain meds.
  - *Durable Power of Attorney (Health Care Proxy):* Names proxy; requires **1 physician** certification of incapacity; ends if capacity returns.
  - *DNR / DNRCC:* Standardized physician + patient form prohibiting resuscitation once pulseless and apneic (DNRCC allows O2, bleeding control, pain meds). Cannot act until cardiac arrest; **must verify physical document** (form, card, bracelet)—never rely solely on family/staff word; if missing/questionable, resuscitate and call medical direction.
  - *POLST (MOLST/MOST/POST/TPOPP):* Life expectancy **<=1 year**; specifies pre-arrest treatment limits; signed by physician (or PA/NP in some states).
  - *Field Ambiguity:* Reject vague phrases (*"no heroics"*); do not waste resuscitative time reading living wills; when in doubt, start care and contact medical direction.
- **Refusals:** Medical **capacity** vs. legal **competence** (*de facto incompetent* without court ruling). **7-Step Protection:** (1) Thorough assessment (or document declined exam) → (2) re-persuade in plain language + patient read-back → (3) confirm capacity (not suicidal/impaired) → (4) consult medical direction → (5) stay safe (call police if needed) → (6) advise specific warning symptoms → (7) thorough documentation + signed release & witness (or witnessed refusal to sign).

## 3.3: Liability, Privacy, Federal Laws & Special Field Situations
- **Criminal vs. Civil Liability & Negligence:** Criminal (government prosecutes → fine/jail) vs. Civil (**plaintiff** sues **defendants** for a **tort**). **Negligence requires ALL 4 elements:** (1) **Duty to act** + (2) **Breach of duty** (*simple* vs. *gross* negligence) + (3) **Damages** + (4) **Proximate cause** (*res ipsa loquitur* = "thing speaks for itself"; *negligence per se* = violating law/protocol).
- **Five EMS Intentional Torts:** **Abandonment** (stopping care without equal/higher handoff; leaving with ED clerk only; inappropriate ALS-to-BLS downgrade), **Assault** (willful threat without contact), **Battery** (unlawful touching/care without consent), **False Imprisonment** (transporting/detaining rational adult against wishes), **Defamation** (**Slander** = spoken; **Libel** = written/mass media).
- **HIPAA (1996) & COBRA/EMTALA:**
  - *HIPAA 5 Legal Disclosures Without Consent:* (1) Continuing care provider, (2) mandatory public health/government reporting (abuse, neglect, rape, domestic violence, communicable diseases, GSW, stabbings, animal bites, suspicious burns), (3) police criminal investigation, (4) third-party billing, (5) court subpoena. Consult agency **Privacy Officer** for breaches.
  - *COBRA/EMTALA ("Anti-Patient-Dumping"):* Guarantee emergency care regardless of ability to pay; stabilize before transfer; ensure EMS crew scope matches patient needs (EMTs cannot run continuous ECG or IV drug infusions); no hospital bypass for inability to pay. **5 Interfacility Transfer Safeguards:** Full report, confirm scope/capability, signed informed transfer consent, written transfer certification, quickest route.
- **Organ Donors, Death Recognition, Crime Scenes & Safe-Haven Laws:**
  - *Organ Donors & Medical IDs:* Treat **patient first, organ donor last**; requires signed legal document; identify, notify medical direction/ED, support organs (CPR) if harvesting attempted. Check medical ID jewelry/bands and non-standardized medical alert tattoos.
  - *Death & 2022 NASEMSO Protocol:* Resuscitate warm patients without obvious death signs, **except accidental hypothermia** (not dead until rewarmed). **5 Presumptive Signs** (absent pulse/breathing/breath sounds, unresponsive, no eye/pupil response, absent BP, no reflexes); **4 Obvious Signs** (decapitation, rigor mortis **2–12 hrs**, decomposition, dependent lividity). **NASEMSO Exclusions (excludes lightning, drowning, hypothermia):** **8 Biological Conditions** (decapitation, decomposition/putrefaction `>=24 hrs`, torso transection, incineration `90% BSA`, injuries incompatible with life, futile/inhumane per protocol, blunt/penetrating trauma apneic/pulseless/no signs of life, nontraumatic arrest with lividity/rigor) **OR** valid, intact state DNR/POLST. When unclear, start CPR and call medical direction.
  - *Crime Scenes & Reporting:* Stage for police; single path in/out; touch only necessary items and report to police; no crime-scene phone use without permission; avoid cutting bullet/stab holes; cut away from knots; do not cover body with sheet; in rape cases, advise against washing, changing clothes, bathroom, or eating/drinking. Report abuse/neglect (good-faith defamation immunity), crime/drug injuries (addiction is an illness per Supreme Court), exposures, restraints, suicide attempts, dog bites. **Baby Safe-Haven Laws** (TX **1999**, nationwide): safe relinquishment of unharmed infants within state age limits (**3 days** up to **1 year** in ND).

## 4.1: PCR Functions, DOT Minimum Data Set & 2022 NASEMSO / NEMSIS v3.4

# Chapter 5: Communication

## Master Key Terms (Chapter 5)

## 5.1: Hardware, FCC Regulation & System Radio Protocols
- **Milestones & Hardware:** **EMS Act of 1973 (PL 93-154)** (1 of 15 components; VHF/UHF funding) & **FirstNet (2012, PL 112-96)** (public safety broadband). **Base Station** (**80–150 W**, high terrain near command hospital; antenna offsets FCC limits/noise), **LMRS** (UHF, VHF, **700–800 MHz**), **Mobile Radios** (**20–50 W**, **10–15 mi**), **Portable Radios** (**1–5 W**, boosted via repeaters), **Repeaters** (rebroadcast at higher power/new frequency), **Encoder/Decoder** (digital codes allowing shared frequencies), **Mobile Data Terminal (MDT)** (displays/prints dispatch + status buttons), **Cellular Phones** (**2–40 sq mi** cells; high quality/privacy/data, but can overload in MCIs), **Telemetry** (automated vitals/12-lead ECG), **Telemedicine** (MIH/ET3), **Land Mobile Satellite** (remote rural areas).
- **FCC & Maintenance:** FCC licenses base stations, assigns call signs/frequencies, approves gear, sets power limits, prevents interference, bans profanity/obscenity. Clean with damp cloth/mild detergent, check frequency drift, test batteries daily + keep spares, maintain generator/cellular backups.
- **11-Step Transmission Progression & 16 Radio Ground Rules:** Dispatch → acknowledge → en route → other agencies → on scene → medical direction → leaving scene → hospital report → at hospital → leaving hospital → back at station. **Ground rules:** Close windows; listen first; press PTT and wait **1 second**; hold mic **2–3 inches** from lips; calm monotone; call target unit first; wait for *"go ahead"* (or *"stand by"*); pause every **30 seconds**; plain English (no slang, *"Be advised"*, excessive *"Please/Thank you"*); confusing numbers as word + digits (*"thirteen, one-three"*); objective/pertinent subjective facts only (no diagnoses); **never broadcast patient names**; no profanity; **closed-loop ("echo")** word-for-word order repeat-back; write down addresses/orders; say *"we"* not *"I"*; *"affirmative/negative"* not *"yes/no"*; end with *"Over"*.
- **Dispatch, AACN, Military Time & Handoffs:** **AACN** transmits crash location, velocity change, force direction, airbag deployment, rollover, and multiple impacts. Communicate at **9 dispatch points** using **24-hour military time** (`0100–1200` A.M., `1300–2400` P.M.). Radio terms: **Break, Clear, Come in, Copy/10-4, ETA, Go ahead, Landline, Over, Repeat/say again, Spell out, Stand by**.
  - *Radio Report & SBAR:* Unit ID/level, age/sex, chief complaint, special alerts (trauma/stroke/STEMI), HPI/scene/MOI, major PMH, mental status, baseline vitals, exam findings, care given, response, current condition, requests/orders, ETA. **SBAR** = **S**ituation, **B**ackground, **A**ssessment, **R**ecommendation. Repeat orders word for word (including *"do not"* orders) and question inappropriate orders.
  - *Oral Handoff & Scene Authority:* In-person oral report adds en-route vitals, en-route treatments/responses, omitted history, + PCR copy. Take `<=1 min` EMR update on alert non-critical patients; go straight to critical patients. Authority transfer phrases: *"We’re Emergency Medical Technicians. Is there anything we need to know before we provide emergency care?"* (police/rescue) and *"We’re certified EMTs, Doctor. How can we help?"* (physician).

## 5.2: Therapeutic Communication, Nonverbal Cues & Special Populations
- **Loop & Nine Therapeutic Responses:** **Sender** (encoding) → **Receiver** (decoding) → **Feedback** loop. **9 Responses:** **Facilitation**, **Clarification**, **Summary**, **Explanation**, **Silence** (caution in depression), **Reflection**, **Empathy**, **Confrontation** (assertive, non-aggressive), **Facilitated Communication** (physical support for letterboard/keyboard without influencing content).
- **Patient Contact, Nonverbal Cues & Interviewing:** Convey **Competence, Confidence, and Compassion**; formal titles (*Mr./Mrs.*) for older adults; ask permission; expect denial from **defense mechanisms**; protect privacy; control light/noise. Nonverbal dominates when cues conflict: open posture, match eye level (above only for hostile safety; below for children/elderly), **Distance Zones** (**Intimate `<1½ ft`**, **Personal `1½–4 ft`**, **Social `4–12 ft`**, **Public `>=12 ft`**), **gestures**, cultural **eye contact**, **haptics** (ask before touch if reluctant). Use **open-ended** and **closed-ended** questions; avoid **leading questions**, interrupting, false assurances, diagnosing, or *"why"* blame questions.
- **Transcultural & Special Populations:** Overcome **ethnocentrism**; respect folk medicine/space/eye contact; use interpreter lines and document interpreter. Speak slowly/distinctly without talking down to elderly (keep hearing aids in/on, do not shout—links to **Ch. 7 presbycusis**); keep parents calm, crouch to child's eye level, always tell truth; **Family-Centered Care (1987, Surgeon General Koop / EMSC)**.

# Chapter 6: Lifting and Moving Patients

## Master Key Terms (Chapter 6)
- **Body Mechanics & Posture:** body mechanics, kyphosis, lordosis, power grip, power lift
- **Patient Move Categories:** emergency move, urgent move, nonurgent move

## 6.1: Body Mechanics, Posture, Lifting & Move Categories
- **Four Principles of Body Mechanics & Posture:** (1) Keep weight close to body; (2) engage legs, hips, glutes, and contracted abs (never back); (3) "stack" shoulders over hips over feet (never lift & twist); (4) reduce lift height/distance. Avoid **excessive lordosis** (swayback) and **excessive kyphosis** (slouch); vertically align ears, shoulders, hips; match partners of comparable strength/height; explain plan to alert patients beforehand.
- **Lifting Techniques & Limits:**
  - *Power Lift (9 steps) & Power Grip:* Full palm/finger contact with all fingers bent equally; feet shoulder-width apart/slightly outward, sit down with knees bent, tighten abs/back, neutral head, straddle object, hands **>=10 inches** apart, drive through heels/arches raising upper body before hips.
  - *Squat Lift* (weak leg slightly forward, foot flat, push up with stronger leg) & *One-Handed Carry* (staggered feet, bend at hips, trunk tilt **<=45°**).
  - *Limits:* Heavy 2-rescuer carry **<=1 minute**; 100% effort **6 seconds**, 50% effort **1 minute**; forward reach **15–20 inches** (move closer if `>20 in`); push rather than pull (waist-to-shoulder height; kneel for low loads; never push/pull overhead).
- **Three Move Categories:**
  - *Emergency Moves (Immediate Scene Danger or Lifesaving Access):* Fire, explosives/hazmat, traffic, unstable structures, extreme weather, hostile crowds, or CPR/defibrillation/hemorrhage access. Pull along **long axis** (nose aligned with umbilicus): **Armpit-Forearm Drag**, **Shirt Drag** (never T-shirt alone), **Blanket Drag**.
  - *Urgent Moves (Immediate Life Threat):* **Rapid Extrication** (MVC with ABC/shock/AMS/torso/bilateral femur trauma: manual in-line stabilization + C-collar + coordinated rotation onto long backboard → move off board per protocol) & **Self-Extrication** (non-critical blocking patient exits with C-collar and neutral alignment).
  - *Nonurgent Moves:* **Walking assist** (never if dizzy/sweaty, chest pain, dyspnea, lower-extremity or spinal injury), **Direct Ground Lift** (not for heavy patients), **Extremity Lift** (no spinal/extremity injury), **Direct Carry** (stretcher perpendicular at foot of bed), **Draw Sheet Method** (stretcher alongside bed).

## 7.1: Anatomical Terms, Positions, Planes & Quadrants

## 7.3: Pediatric vs. Geriatric Lifespan Differences & Medical Terminology
- **Pediatric:** Larger tongue; **4–5 mm** pliable trachea (vs. **20 mm** adult); **obligate nose breathers**; narrowest at **cricoid** (`<6–8 yrs`); higher epiglottis; large occiput → shoulder padding until **8–9 yrs** (adult proportions at **10**); infants `<6–7 mo` cannot support head; **posterior fontanelle** closes **3 mo**, **anterior** **12–18 mo**; pliable horizontal ribs, **abdominal breathing**, retractions, **grunting**; rate **>=60/min** = inadequate; children `<5 yrs` breathe **2–3× faster**; compensate via **tachycardia/vasoconstriction**, sudden hypotension after **>30%** blood loss; bradycardia = **late** in children, **initial** in **newborns**; **greenstick fractures**; O2/glucose consumed **2–3× faster** (rapid **hypoglycemia** & **hypothermia**).
- **Geriatric (`65+`: 16% in 2019 → 22% in 2040; >50% EMS calls by 2030):** Normal aging starts **~age 30**. Valve **stenosis**/regurgitation (mitral > tricuspid → pulmonary edema), **cardiac hypertrophy**, fewer pacemakers, **dysrhythmias**, **arteriosclerosis**, blunted **baroreceptors** (**orthostatic hypotension**); no new alveoli after **age 20**, blunted **chemoreceptors**, weaker cough/cilia; neuron loss (**mid-20s**), **cataracts/glaucoma/macular degeneration/retinal detachment**, **presbycusis** (keep hearing aids in/on, do not shout), **peripheral neuropathy** (silent MI); reduced liver drug metabolism; type 2 diabetes risk; **osteoporosis** & **kyphosis** (**2 of 3**, needs void padding); **1/3** nephron loss (**drug toxicity**); thin fragile skin.

# Chapter 8: Pathophysiology

## 8.1: Cellular Metabolism & Sodium–Potassium Pump Failure
- **Aerobic vs. Anaerobic Metabolism:**
  - *Glycolysis (Cytosol, No O2):* Glucose → **pyruvic acid** + **small ATP**.
  - *Aerobic (With O2, Mitochondria):* Pyruvic acid → **large ATP** + **heat**, **CO2** (exhaled), and **water** (reabsorbed/excreted).
  - *Anaerobic (Without O2):* Pyruvic acid → **lactic acid** + minimal **ATP** → acid inactivates enzymes, disrupts membranes, causes **cell death**.
- **Sodium–Potassium Pump (6-Step Cycle) & Failure Cascade:** **Na+** = primary extracellular ion; **K+** = primary intracellular ion. **ATP** actively pumps **Na+ out** against its gradient so **K+ moves in**. Poor perfusion → ↓ ATP → pump **fails** → **Na+ accumulates inside** → **water follows Na+** → cell **swells** → **membrane ruptures & cell dies** → **organ failure**.

## 8.2: The 11 Components Necessary for Adequate Perfusion
1. **Composition of Ambient Air:** Sea-level air = **78.08% N2** (`593.4 mmHg`), **20.95% O2** (`159.2 mmHg`), **0.93% Ar** (`7.1 mmHg`), **0.03% CO2** (`0.2 mmHg`); ensure `>=21%` O2. **FiO2** (spontaneous: room air `0.21`, NRB `~0.95`) vs. **FDO2** (assisted: BVM at 15 L/min `~0.95–0.98`). Toxic gases: O2 displacement (fire), **CO** (blocks RBC O2 binding), **cyanide** (blocks cellular O2 use).
2. **Patent Airway:** Clear nasopharynx (infants = obligate nose breathers), oropharynx, epiglottis (jaw-thrust/chin-lift), larynx (narrowest at **vocal cords** in adults vs. **cricoid ring** in children **<8 yrs**; **laryngeal spasm**), **carina** (2nd ICS / `T4`), bronchi/bronchioles (**bronchitis** vs. **asthma** [inflammation + bronchoconstriction → **wheezing**, **prolonged expiration**, **hypercarbia**]).
3. **Mechanics of Ventilation:** **Boyle’s Law** (volume inversely proportional to pressure). Active inhalation (diaphragm **60–70%** + external intercostals: `760 → 758 mmHg`); passive exhalation (`762 mmHg`); negative pleural pressure (`754 mmHg`, "water glass" effect; breach collapses lung). Accessory muscles: inhalation (**sternocleidomastoid, scalene, pectoralis minor**); active exhalation (**abdominals, internal intercostals**). **Compliance** (↓ in pneumonia, pulmonary edema, flail chest) vs. **Airway Resistance** (↑ primarily by **edema**, mucus, bronchoconstriction). `Minute Ventilation = VT × f` (`500 × 12 = 6 L/min`); `Alveolar Ventilation = (VT − VD) × f` (`[500 − 150] × 12 = 4.2 L/min`; `VD ≈ 150 mL`, exhaled O2 = `17–18%`). Low VT fills dead space first → needs **PPV**. **4 Causes of Inadequate Ventilation:** Low VT, rate too slow, rate too fast (**>=40/min** adult, **>60/min** peds), ↑ dead space.
4. **Regulation of Ventilation:** **Central Chemoreceptors** (medulla: CO2/CSF pH = *hypercapnic drive*); **Peripheral Chemoreceptors** (aortic arch/carotid bodies: arterial O2 = *hypoxic drive* in chronic CO2-retaining **COPD**); **Irritant** (cough/bronchoconstriction), **Stretch** (prevents overinflation), **J-Receptors** (↑ capillary pressure → rapid, shallow breathing). **Brainstem Centers:** **VRG** (anterior medulla: motor output, **2 sec** on / **3 sec** off = `~12/min`, forced breathing), **DRG** (posterior medulla: sensory relay), **Pontine/Pneumotaxic** (inhibits `VRG-I`).
5. **Ventilation/Perfusion (V/Q) Ratio:** Overall normal V/Q **<1** (apexes = wasted ventilation; bases = wasted perfusion). Alveolar pressure > capillary pressure halts blood flow. **Ventilatory mismatch** (asthma = wasted perfusion → O2 + bronchodilators) vs. **Perfusion mismatch** (bleeding = wasted ventilation → stop bleeding + restore volume).
6. **Gas Transport:** **O2 (~1,000 mL/min):** **97–98.5%** on **hemoglobin** (**4 iron sites**; **oxyhemoglobin** vs. **deoxyhemoglobin**) + **1.5–3%** plasma *(anemic patients read normal SpO2 while hypoxic—give O2)*. **CO2:** **70%** **bicarbonate**, **23%** **hemoglobin**, **7%** **plasma**.
7. **Blood Volume & Fluid Pressures:** **~70 mL/kg** (`~4.9 L`); **55% Plasma** (91% water + **albumin**, antibodies, **fibrinogen**) + **45% Formed Elements** (RBCs [48% men, 42% women], WBCs, platelets). **Distribution:** **Venous 64%** (drops first in bleeding), **Arterial 13%**, **Pulmonary 9%**, **Capillaries 7%**, **Heart 7%**. **Hydrostatic Pressure ("Push")** (↑ → **edema / pulmonary edema**) vs. **Plasma Oncotic Pressure ("Pull")** (**albumin** retains fluid).
8. **Pump Function of the Myocardium:** `CO = HR × SV` (resting **5 L/min**, up to **6×**). **HR >160 bpm** cuts filling time and **decreases CO**. **3 SV Determinants:** **Preload** (**Frank-Starling law**), **Myocardial Contractility** (↓ in CHF, MI, β1-blockers), **Afterload** (aortic resistance; chronic high diastolic BP → LV failure).
9. **Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR) & Pulse Pressure:** **Vasoconstriction** (↑ SVR/BP, cool skin) vs. **Vasodilation** (↓ SVR/BP). **Narrow Pulse Pressure** (`Systolic − Diastolic < 25% of Systolic BP`) = early shock (`↓ CO` + compensatory `↑ SVR` → **thready pulse**).
10. **Microcirculation:** Arterioles → metarterioles → true capillaries → venules; **precapillary sphincters** regulated by **local** (temp, hypoxia, acidosis, histamine), **neural** (SNS/PNS), and **hormonal** (α1 epinephrine) factors.
11. **Blood Pressure (`BP = CO × SVR`) & Reflexes:** **Baroreceptors** (aortic arch & carotid sinuses) signal medulla → ↑ BP triggers cardioinhibitory (`↑ PNS/↓ SNS`) & vasodilation; ↓ BP triggers cardioexcitatory (`↑ SNS/↓ PNS`) & vasoconstriction; severe drop activates **adrenal medulla** (**epinephrine** α1/α2/β1 & **norepinephrine** α1/α2). **Chemoreceptor CV Reflexes** (O2 drop → **pale, cool skin** & **tachycardia**).

## 8.3: Master Summary — 10 Requirements for Aerobic Metabolism
1. Adequate oxygen content in ambient air
2. Patent airway
3. Adequate **minute ventilation** (rate + tidal volume)
4. Adequate **alveolar ventilation** (rate + tidal volume minus dead air space)
5. Adequate **pulmonary capillary perfusion** (venous volume + RV pump function)
6. Adequate **alveolar/capillary gas exchange**
7. Adequate **blood content** (RBCs, hemoglobin, plasma)
8. Adequate **cardiac output** (HR, preload, SV, contractility, afterload)
9. Adequate **systemic vascular resistance** (SNS + PNS balance)
10. Adequate **capillary/cell gas exchange**
