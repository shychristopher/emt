# Part I: Chapter 7 — Anatomy, Physiology, and Medical Terminology

## 7.1: Anatomical Terms, Positions, Planes & Quadrants

### Core Concepts & Reference Standard
- **Anatomy:** The structure of the body and the relationship of its parts to each other (how the body is made).
- **Physiology:** The function of the living body and its parts (how the body works).
- **Clinical Importance:** Using accurate terms for position, direction, and location minimizes confusion and clearly communicates assessment findings—especially when requesting online medical direction.
- **Anatomical Position:** The default reference posture for all directional and location terms:
  - Patient standing erect
  - Facing forward
  - Arms down at the sides
  - Palms facing forward

### Patient Positions

| Position | Description & Clinical Considerations |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Supine** | Lying face up on the back. |
| **Prone** | Lying face down on the stomach. |
| **Lateral Recumbent / Recovery Position** | Lying on the left or right side. Position so the airway can be easily monitored and avoid excessive chest pressure that could impair breathing. If kept in the recovery position for **greater than 30 minutes**, turn the patient to the opposite side to avoid injury from impaired blood flow to the lower arm. |
| **Fowler’s Position** | Lying on the back with the upper body elevated at a **45° to 60°** angle. |
| **Semi-Fowler’s Position** | Lying on the back with the upper body elevated at an angle **less than 45°**. |
| **Trendelenburg Position** | Lying on the back on an inclined plane with legs elevated higher than the head and body (head down, legs up). **No longer recommended** for shock management: gravity can push abdominal organs against the diaphragm (making breathing harder) and may increase intracranial pressure in head-injured patients. |
| **Shock Position** | Alternative to Trendelenburg where only the feet and legs are elevated approximately **12 inches**. **No longer recommended** for treating shock (no evidence of effectiveness) and **contraindicated** with suspected spine injury; may still be considered for a simple faint (syncopal episode) or vasodilation-related hypotension. |

### Anatomical Planes & Reference Lines

| Plane / Reference Line | Definition & Anatomical Division |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Anatomical Planes** | Imaginary divisions of the body (paired with reference lines) that clarify internal organ structure and relationships. |
| **Sagittal Plane / Median Plane** | Vertical lengthwise plane dividing the body into right and left segments (not necessarily equal). |
| **Midsagittal Plane** | Vertical plane dividing the body into **equal** right and left halves. |
| **Frontal Plane / Coronal Plane** | Vertical side-to-side plane dividing the body into front (**anterior plane**) and back (**posterior plane**) halves. |
| **Transverse Plane / Horizontal Plane** *(Axial Plane)* | Plane parallel to the ground dividing the body into upper (**superior plane**) and lower (**inferior plane**) halves. |
| **Midline** | Imaginary vertical line from the top of the head through the nose and navel down between the legs; aligns with the midsagittal plane by dividing the body into equal halves. |
| **Midaxillary Line** | Imaginary vertical line from the middle of the armpit (axilla) down to the ankle; connecting both midaxillary lines forms the frontal plane. |
| **Transverse Line** | Imaginary horizontal line through the waist; forms the transverse plane dividing the superior plane (above waist) from the inferior plane (below waist). |

### Directional & Regional Terms

| Term / Pair | Definition & Clinical Reference |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Anterior vs. Posterior** | Toward the front (*anterior*) vs. toward the back (*posterior*). |
| **Superior vs. Inferior** | Toward the head or above a reference point (*superior*) vs. toward the feet or below a reference point (*inferior*). |
| **Dorsal vs. Ventral** | Toward the back/backbone/spine, including dorsum of hand/foot (*dorsal*) vs. toward the front/belly/abdomen (*ventral*). |
| **Medial vs. Lateral** | Toward the midline/center (*medial*) vs. toward the left/right or away from the midline (*lateral*). |
| **Laterality Terms** | **Bilateral** = both left and right sides; **unilateral** = one side; **ipsilateral** = same side; **contralateral** = opposite side. |
| **Proximal vs. Distal** | Near the point of reference (*proximal*) vs. distant/far from the point of reference (*distal*). |
| **Right vs. Left** | Always refers to the **patient's** right and left (opposite the viewer's left/right when facing the patient). |
| **Midclavicular vs. Midaxillary** | **Midclavicular:** Center of each collarbone (clavicle); the **midclavicular line** runs down the anterior thorax from the center of either clavicle. **Midaxillary:** Center of the armpit (axilla), extending from mid-armpit to ankle. |
| **Plantar vs. Palmar** | Sole of the foot (*plantar*) vs. palm of the hand (*palmar*). |

## 7.2: Body Systems — Structure and Function

### I. The Musculoskeletal System
- **Structural Overview:** Composed of a bony framework (skeleton) held together by **ligaments** (bone to bone), muscle layers, **tendons** (muscle to bone), and connective tissues.
- **Four Core Skeletal Functions:** (1) Giving the body shape, (2) protecting vital internal organs, (3) allowing movement, and (4) storing minerals and producing blood cells.
- **Bone Classifications:** Long, short, flat, or irregular.

#### A. Six Basic Skeletal Components
1. **Skull:** Divided into **cranium** and **face**:
   - *Cranium:* Interlocking flat bones (**occipital**, two **parietal**, two **temporal**, and **frontal**). Thick/tough outer layer and thinner/brittle inner layer provide strength and elasticity, yet fractures and anterior/inferior bony ridges can lacerate, bruise, bleed, or swell brain tissue within a non-expanding space (risking increased intracranial pressure, unresponsiveness, or death).
   - *Face:* **14 bones** between brow and chin—**13 immovable/interlocking** (**orbits**, **nasal bones**, **maxillae**, **zygomatic bones**) and **1 freely movable** lower jaw (**mandible**) on hinge joints.
2. **Spinal Column (Vertebral Column):** **33** irregularly shaped **vertebrae** bound by strong ligaments and separated by fluid-filled elastic cartilage *intervertebral disks* (shock absorbers vulnerable to twisting/improper lifting); houses the spinal cord. *(Assessment pearl: Spinal cord damage causes distal sensation/motor loss; vertebral column injury usually causes pain alone.)*

| Spinal Division | Vertebrae | Anatomical Region & Key Clinical Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cervical Spine** | `C1–C7` (7) | Neck; most prone to injury |
| **Thoracic Spine** | `T1–T12` (12) | Upper back; 12 pairs of thoracic ribs attach posteriorly |
| **Lumbar Spine** | `L1–L5` (5) | Lower back; least mobile (most lower-back injuries involve muscles rather than vertebrae) |
| **Sacral Spine** *(Sacrum)* | `S1–S5` (5 fused) | Fused together to form the rigid posterior wall of the pelvis |
| **Coccyx** | 4 fused | Most distal end of spine (tailbone); lacks typical vertebral protrusions |

3. **Thorax:** Formed by **24 ribs** (12 pairs attached posteriorly to `T1–T12`), **sternum**, and thoracic spine:
   - *Ribs:* Pairs **1–7** (*true ribs*, attach directly to sternum by cartilage); pairs **8–10** (*false ribs*, attach via cartilage to ribs above); pairs **11–12** (*floating ribs* / also *false ribs*, no anterior sternal attachment).
   - *Sternum:* Superior **manubrium** (articulates with clavicle and first ribs), middle **body** (attaches ribs), and inferior **xiphoid process**.
4. **Pelvis:** Doughnut-shaped abdominal cavity floor supporting intestines and housing bladder, rectum, and internal reproductive organs; includes sacrum, coccyx, lateral **iliac crests** ("wings"), anterior/inferior **pubis**, and posterior/inferior **ischium**.
5. **Lower Extremities (Hip to Toes):**
   - *Hip & knee:* Pelvic **acetabulum** socket + rounded head of **femur** (thighbone); distal femur + proximal **tibia** form hinged knee protected anteriorly by triangular **patella**.
   - *Lower leg & foot:* Weight-bearing anterior/medial **tibia** (distal end = **medial malleolus**) and lateral **fibula** (distal end = **lateral malleolus**); proximal **tarsals** including **calcaneus** (heel), **5 metatarsals** (arch), and **14 toe phalanges** per foot (**2** big toe, **3** others).
6. **Upper Extremities (Shoulder to Fingers):**
   - *Shoulder girdle:* **Clavicle** + **scapula** with tip **acromion**; ball-and-socket shoulder joint offers widest mobility and easiest dislocation.
   - *Arm, forearm & hand:* **Humerus**; hinged elbow joint with lateral/thumb-side **radius** (upper two-thirds muscle-sheathed, distal third palpable) and medial/pinky-side **ulna** (posterior **olecranon**); **8 carpals**, **metacarpals**, and **phalanges** (**3** per finger, **2** per thumb).

#### B. Joints, Movements, Bone Hemorrhage & Muscle Types
- **Joint Mobility:** Immovable (skull), slightly movable (spine), or freely movable (synovial cavity, synovial membrane, articular cartilage, wrapped in ligaments).
- **Joint Movements:** **Flexion** (bending/decreasing angle), **extension** (straightening/increasing angle), **abduction** (away from midline), **adduction** (toward midline), **circumduction** (360° conical motion), **pronation** (forearm turned so palm faces back), and **supination** (forearm turned so palm faces front).

| Joint Type | Permitted Motion | Anatomical Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ball-and-Socket Joint** | Widest range of motion (flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation) | Shoulder, hip |
| **Hinged Joint** | Flexion and extension in one plane | Elbow (forward), knee (backward), finger |
| **Pivot Joint** | Turning / rotational motion | `C1–C2` (head/neck), wrist |
| **Gliding Joint** | Flat sliding of one bone across another | Small bones in hands and feet |
| **Saddle Joint** | Limited combinations of movement along perpendicular planes | Ankle (foot turns inward slightly while moving up/down) |
| **Condyloid Joint** | Modified ball-and-socket permitting limited two-direction motion without full rotation | Wrist (up/down and side-to-side) |

- **Bone Injury & Hemorrhage Risk:** Fractures rupture intraosseous blood vessels in compact bone, marrow cavity, and spongy bone:
  - *Femur fracture:* **1,000 to 2,000 mL** blood loss
  - *Pelvic fracture:* Up to the entire **~5 L** (`70–80 mL/kg`) blood volume, risking shock

| Muscle Type | Control & Structure | Location & Key Physiological Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skeletal Muscle** *(Voluntary)* | Consciously controlled; mostly tendon-attached to bone (broad/flat in trunk, spindle-like in limbs) | Locomotion, chewing, swallowing, talking, eye movement |
| **Smooth Muscle** *(Involuntary)* | Automatic rhythmic wavelike contractions without conscious control | Hollow organs, ducts, respiratory tract, intestines, urinary tract, blood vessels (*vasoconstriction* vs. *vasodilation*) |
| **Cardiac Muscle** | Involuntary striated meshwork with **automaticity** (generates impulses independent of CNS) | Found exclusively in the walls of the heart; cannot tolerate even brief interruption of coronary blood supply |

### II. The Respiratory System
- **Four Basic Functions:** (1) **Respiration** (O2/CO2 exchange across membranes), (2) **Ventilation** (mechanical air movement via pressure changes), (3) **Oxygenation** (O2 moving down concentration gradient into blood/cells + CO2 removal), and (4) **Acid–Base Buffering** (exhaling excess CO2 alongside kidneys and bicarbonate buffer). *(Brain cells typically begin dying within **~5 minutes** without oxygen.)*
- **Upper Airway (Ends at Cricoid Cartilage):**
  - *Nares & Nasal Cavity:* Warms, moistens, and filters inhaled air over mucous membranes and three bony scroll-like **conchae** ridges.
  - *Pharynx (Throat):* **Nasopharynx** (posterior nasal cavity to soft palate), **oropharynx** (soft palate to epiglottis), and **laryngopharynx (hypopharynx)** (hyoid bone to **esophagus** and larynx).
  - *Larynx (Voice Box):* **Thyroid cartilage** (*Adam’s Apple*), **cricoid cartilage** (only full cartilaginous ring of upper airway), *false* and *true* **vocal cords** surrounding the **glottis**, and **epiglottis** (covers larynx during swallowing; loss of reflexes risks **aspiration**).
- **Lower Airway, Lungs & Pleura:**
  - *Trachea:* Larynx to ~`T5` (**carina**), reinforced by **15–20** C-shaped cartilage rings bridged posteriorly by smooth **trachealis muscle**.
  - *Bronchi & Bronchioles:* Mainstem **bronchi** branch into smooth-muscle **bronchioles** (*bronchoconstriction* vs. *bronchodilation*).
  - *Alveoli & Respiratory Membrane:* Surrounded by capillaries (**respiratory membrane**), lined with **surfactant** to lower surface tension and prevent alveolar collapse.
  - *Lungs & Pleura:* Covered by **visceral pleura** and **parietal pleura** separated by negative-pressure **pleural cavity**; dome-shaped **diaphragm** separates thorax and abdomen.
- **Ventilation Mechanics & Breathing Rates:**
  - *Inhalation (Active):* **Diaphragm** (**~60–70%** of effort via phrenic nerve `C3–C5`) and **intercostal muscles** (**~30–40%**) contract (`C3–C5` injury can paralyze diaphragm). *Exhalation (Passive):* Muscles relax.

| Age Group | Normal Respiratory Rate Range | Average / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Adults** | **8–24 breaths/min** | Average **12–20 breaths/min**; ~1-inch effortless chest rise |
| **Older Adults** | — | Average **20–22 breaths/min** |
| **Children** | **18–37 breaths/min** | Varies by age; faster than adults |
| **Infants** | **30–60 breaths/min** | Rate **>=60/min** indicates inadequate breathing |

- **Inadequate Breathing Red Flags:** Abnormal rate/rhythm, diminished/absent breath sounds, unequal/shallow chest expansion, pale/cyanotic/cool/clammy skin, retractions/neck muscle use, nasal flaring, infant "seesaw" breathing or head bobbing, newborn grunting, or **agonal respirations** (requiring immediate ventilation).

### III. The Circulatory System
- **Heart Anatomy & Blood Flow:** Fist-sized muscular organ inside *pericardium* (two-thirds left of midline):
  1. **Right Atrium** (receives O2-poor blood via superior/inferior **venae cavae**) → **Tricuspid Valve** → **Right Ventricle** → **Pulmonary Valve** → **Pulmonary Arteries** (only arteries carrying O2-depleted blood) → lungs.
  2. **Pulmonary Veins** (only veins carrying O2-rich blood) → **Left Atrium** → **Bicuspid (Mitral) Valve** → **Left Ventricle** → **Aortic Valve** → **Aorta** (divides into iliac arteries near navel).
  3. *Conduction & Coronary Supply:* **SA node** → **AV node** → **bundle of His** → bundle branches → **Purkinje fibers**; **coronary arteries** supply myocardium directly.
- **Vessels, Blood Components & Hemodynamics:**
  - *Vessels:* **Arteries** (**carotid**, **femoral**, **dorsalis pedis**, **posterior tibial**, **brachial** [BP/infant pulse], **radial**) → **arterioles** → **capillaries** → **venules** → **veins** (one-way valves).
  - *Blood Components:* **RBCs** (hemoglobin carries O2/CO2), **WBCs** (infection defense), **platelets** (clotting), and **plasma** (liquid + *albumin*).
  - *Pulse & BP:* Central (carotid, femoral), peripheral (radial, brachial, posterior tibial, dorsalis pedis), and apical pulses; **systolic BP** (left ventricular contraction) vs. **diastolic BP** (arterial resistance at rest); elevated **hydrostatic pressure** causes **edema**.
  - *Gas Transport & Metabolism:* O2 = **~97%** hemoglobin / **~3%** plasma; CO2 = **~70%** bicarbonate / **~23%** hemoglobin / **~7%** plasma. **Aerobic** (with O2, high energy) vs. **anaerobic** (without O2, minimal energy + acid in **hypoperfusion/shock**).

### IV. Nervous, Endocrine, Integumentary, Digestive, Urinary & Reproductive Systems
- **Nervous System:**
  - *CNS:* Brain (~3 lb, continuous O2/glucose via blood–brain barrier) + ~18-inch spinal cord + meninges + CSF: **Cerebrum** (thought, sensation, speech, memory, emotion, conscious motor), **Cerebellum** (balance/coordination/tone), and **Brainstem** (midbrain, pons, *medulla oblongata* housing respiratory, cardiac, and vasomotor centers).
  - *PNS & Consciousness:* *Afferent* (sensory) & *efferent* (motor) nerves; **Voluntary (somatic)** vs. **Autonomic** (*sympathetic* fight-or-flight vs. *parasympathetic*). Consciousness requires intact **reticular activating system (RAS)** + at least **one cerebral hemisphere**.
- **Endocrine System & Adrenergic Receptors:**

| Endocrine Gland | Location & Core Hormonal Functions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pineal Gland** | Brain; secretes melatonin (inhibits reproductive functions; circadian regulation) |
| **Pituitary Gland** | Base of brain; "master gland" regulating growth, thyroid/parathyroid, pancreas, gonads, fat/protein/glucose reactions, and urinary excretion |
| **Thyroid Gland** | Anterior neck; regulates metabolism, growth/development, and nervous system activity |
| **Parathyroid Glands** | Posterior to thyroid; regulate calcium and phosphorus bone metabolism |
| **Adrenal Glands** | Superior to kidneys; secrete epinephrine (adrenaline) and norepinephrine, delay muscle fatigue, increase sugar storage, and regulate kidney function and salt/water balance |
| **Islets of Langerhans** *(Pancreas)* | Pancreas; secrete *insulin* (facilitates cellular glucose entry) and *glucagon* (raises blood glucose) |
| **Gonads** *(Ovaries & Testes)* | Pelvis / scrotum; reproductive hormones and secondary sex characteristics |
| **Thymus Gland** | Superior neck/chest; secretes *thymosin* for immune system development/maturation |

  - *Adrenergic Receptors:* **Alpha1** (vasoconstriction → cool/pale skin + sweat glands → clammy), **Alpha2** (regulates alpha1 release), **Beta1** (increases HR, contractility, conduction), **Beta2** (bronchodilation/vasodilation). **Epinephrine** hits α1/α2/β1/β2; **norepinephrine** acts mainly on α1/α2.
- **Integumentary System (Skin):** Barrier, thermoregulation, sensation, water/electrolyte (`Na+`, `Cl-`) balance. Layers: **Epidermis** (4 cell layers, *melanin*), **Dermis** (vessels, follicles, sweat/oil glands, sensory nerves), **Subcutaneous layer** (variable fat) + nails, hair, sweat/oil glands.
- **Digestive System:** Mouth, **esophagus**, stomach (gastric juices), **pancreas** (digestive enzymes + insulin), **liver** (RUQ; bile, sugar storage, clotting/plasma/immune proteins, detox), **spleen** (LUQ; blood filter/reservoir, immunity, no digestion), **gallbladder** (stores bile), **small intestine** (**duodenum**, **jejunum**, **ileum**; digestion/absorption), **large intestine** (water absorption → stool). Mechanical (*peristalsis*) & chemical (*enzymes*: carbs → glucose, fats → fatty acids, proteins → amino acids).
- **Urinary & Reproductive Systems:** Urinary = 2 kidneys, 2 ureters, bladder, urethra. Male = 2 testes (sperm, testosterone), epididymis, vas deferens, prostate/accessory glands, penis. Female = 2 ovaries (ova, estrogen/progesterone), 2 fallopian tubes, uterus, vagina, vulva, breasts.

## 7.3: Pediatric vs. Geriatric Lifespan Differences

### Pediatric Anatomy & Physiology (Infants & Children)

| System / Region | Key Pediatric Differences & Clinical Implications |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Airway** *(Primary Difference)* | Proportionally larger tongue (occlusion risk in AMS); newborn trachea is **4–5 mm** wide (vs. **20 mm** adult) and pliable (occludes with neck hyperextension/flexion); pressing under chin pushes tongue into pharynx; **obligate nose breathers**; narrowest point at **cricoid cartilage** until **~6 years** (vs. vocal cords in adults); higher **epiglottis** increases aspiration risk |
| **Head** | Large occiput flexes neck forward supine → shoulder padding until **8–9 years** (adult proportions at **age 10**); infants **<6–7 months** cannot support head; **posterior fontanelle** closes by **3 months**, **anterior** at **12–18 months** (do not press; use mental status for ICP and tachycardia/mottling/tears/wet diapers for dehydration) |
| **Chest & Lungs** | Pliable horizontal ribs (less organ protection, minimal AP expansion); fragile lung tissue (contusion/pneumothorax risk); **abdominal breathing**; early retractions (intercostal → suprasternal/supraclavicular/sternal); **grunting** ("laryngeal braking") maintains airway pressure but causes rapid exhaustion |
| **Respiratory Rate & Fatigue** | Rate **>=60/min** = inadequate breathing (subsequent drop = impending failure); stress-induced apnea; children **<5 years** breathe **2–3× faster**; diaphragmatic fatigue causes lethargy and lost "attitude" |
| **Cardiovascular** | Small blood volume; cannot raise stroke volume meaningfully—compensate via **tachycardia** and **vasoconstriction** (mottled/cool limbs); hold BP until **>30%** blood loss, then crash suddenly; bradycardia is a **late** hypoxia sign in children, but the **initial** sign in **newborns** |
| **Abdomen & Extremities** | Thin abdominal muscles + exposed liver/spleen until puberty; **greenstick fractures**; cephalocaudal motor development |
| **Metabolism & Skin** | Consume O2 and glucose **2–3× faster** (rapid CNS hypoxia, earlier CO toxicity, rapid **hypoglycemia**); high surface-area-to-mass ratio, thin skin, minimal fat, and immature hypothalamus cause rapid **hypothermia** |

### Geriatric Anatomy, Physiology & Effects of Aging
- **Demographics:** **65+ years** = fastest-growing group (**54.1 million / 16%** in 2019 → **81 million / 22%** projected by 2040; **>50%** of EMS calls by **2030**). Normal aging starts around **age 30**; comorbidities and polypharmacy mask acute emergencies.

| Body System | Age-Related Changes & Clinical Impact |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Valve calcification (**stenosis** or regurgitation, mitral > tricuspid → pulmonary edema); **cardiac hypertrophy** lowers SV/CO; fewer pacemaker cells and blunted catecholamine response limit HR rise; **dysrhythmias**; **arteriosclerosis** raises afterload/systolic BP while blunted **baroreceptors** cause **orthostatic hypotension** |
| **Respiratory** | Weaker muscles and calcified rib-sternum joints stiffen chest; new alveoli stop forming around **age 20**; blunted **chemoreceptors** reduce hypoxia/hypercapnia drive; weaker bronchiolar smooth muscle causes air trapping (airflow declines after **age 30**); weaker cough/cilia + dehydration raise pneumonia risk |
| **Neurologic & Sensory** | Decreased brain mass increases CSF volume; plaques/tangles and neuron loss (starting **mid-20s**) slow reflexes and impair balance/proprioception; blunted hunger/thirst/thermoregulation; **cataracts, glaucoma, macular degeneration, retinal detachment**; **presbycusis** (keep hearing aids in/on, do not shout); **peripheral neuropathy** masks MI pain in diabetics |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Reduced taste/smell, tooth loss, lower saliva; weaker esophageal sphincter (reflux) and lower stomach acid; smaller liver impairs drug metabolism; slower peristalsis/intestinal degeneration cause malnutrition, constipation, impaction, or incontinence |
| **Endocrine** | Reduced insulin sensitivity (type 2 diabetes risk); altered norepinephrine/vasopressin/renin/aldosterone/ANH and lower β-receptor sensitivity drive HTN, orthostatic hypotension, and fluid imbalance; lower thyroxine conversion affects temperature and HR |
| **Musculoskeletal** | **Osteoporosis** (brittle bones, slow healing); narrowing disks cause **kyphosis** in **2 of 3** elderly patients (mostly thoracic—needs extra void padding for SMR); thinner cartilage, thicker synovial fluid, and muscle loss raise fall risk |
| **Renal** | Loss of **nephrons** (kidney weight drops **~one-third**) and reduced renal blood flow make **drug toxicity** common alongside polypharmacy |
| **Integumentary** | Thinning skin, weaker dermal–epidermal bond, slower cell turnover, less sweat, and dulled touch cause skin tears, slow healing, infection, and both hypothermia and hyperthermia |

# Part II: Chapter 8 — Pathophysiology

## 8.1: Cellular Metabolism & Sodium–Potassium Pump Failure

### Aerobic vs. Anaerobic Metabolism
- **Cellular Metabolism (Cellular Respiration):** Cells break down **glucose** (primary fuel) using **oxygen** (primary catalyst) to produce **adenosine triphosphate (ATP)**.

| Feature | Aerobic Metabolism ("With Oxygen") | Anaerobic Metabolism ("Without Oxygen") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1: Glycolysis** | Glucose enters **cytosol** and breaks down into **pyruvic acid** + **small amount of ATP** (no O2 required) | Same initial **glycolysis** produces **pyruvic acid** + **small amount of ATP** |
| **Step 2: Fate of Pyruvic Acid** | Enters **mitochondria** with O2, releasing a **large amount of ATP** | Cannot enter mitochondria without O2; converts into **lactic acid** |
| **By-Products** | **Heat** (core temp), **CO2** (exhaled), and **water** (reabsorbed/excreted) | **Lactic acid** + minimal **ATP** |
| **Cellular Outcome** | High energy output with no harmful by-products | Acid buildup inactivates enzymes, disrupts cell membranes, causes low energy and **cell death** |

### Sodium–Potassium Pump & Pump Failure
- **Ions & Active Transport:** **Na+** = primary extracellular ion; **K+** = primary intracellular ion. Muscle contraction and nerve impulses require continuous **Na+ out** (pumped actively against its gradient using **ATP**) and **K+ in** (moves with its gradient).
- **Six-Step Pump Cycle:** (1) Pump attracts intracellular Na+ → (2) phosphate from **ATP** attaches (leaving **ADP**) → (3) intracellular side closes, extracellular side opens, releasing Na+ → (4) extracellular K+ enters pump and phosphate detaches → (5) extracellular side closes, intracellular side opens → (6) K+ enters cell.
- **Pump Failure Cascade:** Poor perfusion (anaerobic metabolism or lack of glucose) → insufficient **ATP** → pump **fails** → **Na+ accumulates inside cell** → **water follows Na+** into cell → cell **swells** → **cell membrane ruptures and cell dies** → **organ failure**.

## 8.2: The 11 Components Necessary for Adequate Perfusion

### Component 1: Composition of Ambient Air

| Gas | Sea-Level Percentage | Partial Pressure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nitrogen** | **78.08%** | `593.4 mmHg` |
| **Oxygen** | **20.95%** | `159.2 mmHg` |
| **Argon** | **0.93%** | `7.1 mmHg` |
| **Carbon Dioxide** | **0.03%** | `0.2 mmHg` |
| **Total** *(+ trace gases)* | **99.99%** | `759.9 mmHg` |

- **FiO2 vs. FDO2:** Ensure at least **21%** O2. **FiO2** (spontaneous: room air `0.21`, NRB `~0.95`, 100% O2 `1.0`) vs. **FDO2** (assisted: BVM with reservoir at `15 L/min` `~0.95–0.98`).
- **Toxic Gases:** O2 displacement (enclosed fire); **carbon monoxide (CO)** blocks RBC O2 binding; **cyanide** prevents cellular O2 utilization even when blood O2 is adequate.

### Component 2: Patency of the Airway
- **Obstruction Sites:** **Nasopharynx** (drains blood/vomitus; must stay clear in obligate-nose-breathing infants); **oropharynx** (tongue, foreign bodies, swelling, blood, vomitus); **epiglottis** (swelling/flaccid tongue lifted by jaw-thrust/chin-lift); **larynx** (narrowest at **vocal cords** in adults vs. **cricoid ring** in children **<8 years**; **laryngeal spasm** & laryngeal fracture); **trachea** (splits at **carina** [2nd ICS / `T4`]), **bronchi**, and smooth-muscle **bronchioles** (**bronchitis** = mucous inflammation; **asthma** = inflammation + bronchoconstriction → **wheezing**, **prolonged expiratory phase**, hypoxia, **hypercarbia**).

### Component 3: Mechanics of Ventilation
- **Boyle’s Law & Pleural Pressure:** Volume is inversely proportional to pressure. Serous fluid between **visceral** and **parietal pleura** creates a **"water glass" effect** maintaining negative intrapleural pressure (`754 mmHg`). A pleural break draws air in and collapses the lung (occlude open chest wounds early).
  - *Inhalation (Active):* Diaphragm (**60–70%**) + external intercostals drop pressure `760 → 758 mmHg` → air enters.
  - *Exhalation (Passive):* Muscles relax + elastic recoil raise pressure to `762 mmHg` → air exits.
- **Accessory Muscles:**

| Phase | Accessory Muscles | Mechanical Action & Clinical Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inhalation** *(Increase negative pressure)* | **Sternocleidomastoid** (lifts sternum), **Scalene** (elevates ribs 1–2), **Pectoralis minor** (elevates ribs 3–5) | Consumes extra energy; inadequate O2 causes fatigue and respiratory failure |
| **Exhalation** *(Active, e.g., COPD)* | **Abdominal muscles** (push diaphragm up), **Internal intercostals** (pull sternum/ribs down) | Chest trauma/compression disrupts "bellows action" and reduces air intake |

- **Compliance & Airway Resistance:** **Compliance** (lung/chest stretch) decreases in pneumonia, pulmonary edema, flail chest, or neuromuscular disease; **airway resistance** increases primarily from **edema**, mucus, and bronchiolar constriction.
- **Ventilation Volumes & Dead Air Space (VD ≈ 150 mL; Exhaled Air = 17–18% O2):**
  - *Minute Ventilation:* `VT × f/min` → Normal adult: `500 mL × 12/min = 6,000 mL/min (6 L/min)`.
  - *Alveolar Ventilation:* `(VT − VD) × f/min` → Normal adult: `(500 mL − 150 mL) × 12/min = 4,200 mL/min (4.2 L/min)`.
  - *Low VT Clinical Trap:* `VT = 200 mL` at `f = 28/min` gives `5.6 L/min` minute ventilation but only `1.4 L/min` alveolar ventilation because dead space fills first → requires **positive-pressure ventilation**.
- **Four Causes of Inadequate Ventilation:** (1) Low VT, (2) rate too slow, (3) rate too fast (**>=40/min** adults, **>60/min** pediatrics), or (4) increased dead air space.

### Component 4: Regulation of Ventilation

| Regulatory Structure | Location | Primary Stimulus & Physiological Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Central Chemoreceptors** | Medulla | Most sensitive to **CO2** and CSF **pH** (`CO2 + H2O → carbonic acid → H+`); ↑ CO2 → ↑ rate & depth (*hypercapnic drive*) |
| **Peripheral Chemoreceptors** | Aortic arch & carotid bodies | Most sensitive to significant **arterial O2** drops; drives **hypoxic drive** in chronic CO2-retaining **COPD** patients |
| **Irritant Receptors** | Airways | Gases, aerosols, particles → cough, bronchoconstriction, ↑ ventilatory rate |
| **Stretch Receptors** | Airway smooth muscle | High tidal volumes → decrease rate/volume to prevent overinflation |
| **J-Receptors** | Alveoli near capillaries | ↑ capillary pressure → rapid, shallow ventilation |
| **Ventral Respiratory Group (VRG)** | Anterior medulla | `VRG-I` & `VRG-E` motor impulses to diaphragm/intercostals; **2 sec** on / **3 sec** off = **~12/min**; drives forced breathing |
| **Dorsal Respiratory Group (DRG)** | Posterior medulla | Relays sensory rate/depth input to VRG |
| **Pontine Respiratory Center** *(Pneumotaxic)* | Pons | Inhibits `VRG-I` to smooth transition to exhalation |

### Component 5: Ventilation/Perfusion (V/Q) Ratio
- **Normal V/Q:** Overall **<1** (gravity pulls blood to compliant bases: bases = *wasted perfusion*, apexes = *wasted ventilation*).
- **Mismatches:** Alveolar pressure > capillary pressure halts capillary flow (apexes or hemorrhagic hypotension); **ventilatory disturbances** (asthma = *wasted perfusion* → O2 + bronchodilators); **perfusion disturbances** (bleeding = *wasted ventilation* → stop bleeding + restore volume).

### Component 6: Transport of O2 and CO2 by the Blood
- **Oxygen (~1,000 mL/min):** **97–98.5%** on **hemoglobin** (**4 iron sites**: 25/50/75/100%; **oxyhemoglobin** vs. **deoxyhemoglobin**) + **1.5–3%** in plasma *(anemic patients can read normal SpO2 while hypoxic—give O2)*.
- **Carbon Dioxide:** **70%** as **bicarbonate**, **23%** on **hemoglobin**, **7%** in **plasma**.
- **Diffusion:** Moves from high to low concentration at alveolar/capillary and capillary/cell membranes.

### Component 7: Blood Volume & Fluid Pressures
- **Normal Adult Volume:** **~70 mL/kg** (`~4.9 L` in 70-kg adult). **Plasma (55%)** (91% water + **albumin**, antibodies, **fibrinogen**) + **Formed Elements (45%)** (RBCs [48% men, 42% women], WBCs, platelets).

| Compartment | Percentage of Blood Volume | Clinical Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Venous System** | **64%** | Capacitance reservoir—volume drops here first regardless of bleeding source, reducing right-heart filling |
| **Arterial System** | **13%** | High-pressure distribution vessels |
| **Pulmonary Vessels** | **9%** | Pulmonary gas-exchange circulation |
| **Capillaries** | **7%** | Microcirculation exchange sites |
| **Heart** | **7%** | Pumping chambers |

- **Hydrostatic Pressure ("Push")** (↑ → **edema** / **pulmonary edema** in LV failure) vs. **Plasma Oncotic Pressure ("Pull")** (**albumin** retains fluid; low oncotic pressure → vascular volume loss and edema).

### Components 8–11: Myocardial Pump, SVR, Microcirculation & BP Reflexes
- **Cardiac Output (`CO = HR × SV`; Resting = 5 L/min, Up to 6×):**
  - *HR:* SA node automaticity + brainstem cardioexcitatory/cardioinhibitory centers + β1 epinephrine; rates **>160 bpm** shorten filling time and **decrease CO**.
  - *Stroke Volume (3 Determinants):* **Preload** (**Frank-Starling law**), **Myocardial Contractility** (↓ in CHF, MI, β1-blockers), and **Afterload** (aortic resistance; chronic high diastolic BP → LV failure).
- **SVR & Pulse Pressure:** **Vasoconstriction** (↑ SVR, ↑ BP, cool skin) vs. **Vasodilation** (↓ SVR, ↓ BP). **Narrow Pulse Pressure** (`Systolic − Diastolic < 25% of Systolic BP`, e.g., `108/88 = 20 mmHg`) signals dropping CO + compensatory ↑ SVR (**thready pulse**).
- **Microcirculation:** Arterioles → metarterioles → true capillaries → venules; **precapillary sphincters** regulated by **local** (temp, hypoxia, acidosis, histamine), **neural** (SNS/PNS), and **hormonal** (α1 epinephrine) factors.
- **Blood Pressure (`BP = CO × SVR`) & Reflexes:**
  - *Baroreceptors (Aortic Arch & Carotid Sinuses):* ↑ BP → ↑ PNS/↓ SNS → ↓ HR/contractility & vasodilation → ↓ BP; ↓ BP → ↑ SNS/↓ PNS → ↑ HR/contractility & vasoconstriction → ↑ BP; severe drop triggers **adrenal medulla** (**epinephrine** α1/α2/β1 & **norepinephrine** α1/α2).
  - *Chemoreceptor CV Reflexes:* Triggered by significant **O2 drops** → SNS stimulation (`↑ HR` + vasoconstriction → **pale, cool skin** & **tachycardia**).

## 8.3: Master Summary — 10 Requirements for Aerobic Metabolism
1. Adequate oxygen content in ambient air
2. Patent airway
3. Adequate **minute ventilation** (rate + tidal volume)
4. Adequate **alveolar ventilation** (rate + tidal volume minus dead air space)
5. Adequate **pulmonary capillary perfusion** (venous volume + RV pump function)
6. Adequate **alveolar/capillary gas exchange**
7. Adequate **blood content** (RBCs, hemoglobin, plasma)
8. Adequate **cardiac output** (HR, preload, SV, contractility, afterload)
9. Adequate **systemic vascular resistance** (SNS + PNS balance)
10. Adequate **capillary/cell gas exchange**
