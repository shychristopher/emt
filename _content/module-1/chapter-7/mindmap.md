# Chapter 7: Anatomy, Physiology, and Medical Terminology
- **1. Anatomical Terms, Positions, Planes & Quadrants**
  - **Core Concepts & Anatomical Position:**
    - **Anatomy:** Structure of the body and relationship of its parts (how the body is made)
    - **Physiology:** Function of the living body and its parts (how the body works)
    - **Anatomical Position:** Standing erect, facing forward, arms at sides, palms forward
  - **Patient Positions:**
    - **Supine** (face up) • **Prone** (face down)
    - **Lateral Recumbent / Recovery:** Side-lying; turn to opposite side if **>30 minutes** to protect lower arm circulation
    - **Fowler’s (`45°–60°`)** vs. **Semi-Fowler’s (`<45°`)**
    - **Trendelenburg** (head down, legs up) & **Shock Position** (legs elevated **~12 inches**): **No longer recommended** for shock (impairs breathing, raises ICP; shock position contraindicated in spine injury, considered only for simple faint)
  - **Anatomical Planes & Reference Lines:**
    - **Sagittal / Median Plane:** Vertical lengthwise plane dividing right and left segments
    - **Midsagittal Plane:** Vertical plane dividing **equal** right and left halves (aligns with **Midline**)
    - **Frontal / Coronal Plane:** Vertical side-to-side plane dividing **anterior** and **posterior** planes (connects **midaxillary lines**)
    - **Transverse / Horizontal / Axial Plane:** Plane parallel to ground dividing **superior** and **inferior** planes (aligns with **transverse line** at waist)
  - **Directional & Regional Terms:**
    - **Anterior / Ventral** (front) vs. **Posterior / Dorsal** (back) • **Superior** (above) vs. **Inferior** (below)
    - **Medial** (toward midline) vs. **Lateral** (away from midline) • **Proximal** (near reference) vs. **Distal** (far from reference)
    - **Right / Left** = always **patient's** right and left • **Bilateral** (both), **Unilateral** (one), **Ipsilateral** (same), **Contralateral** (opposite)
    - **Midclavicular line** (center of clavicle) • **Midaxillary line** (center of armpit) • **Plantar** (sole of foot) • **Palmar** (palm of hand)
  - **Abdominal Quadrants (Intersect at Umbilicus):**
    - **RUQ:** Majority of liver, gallbladder, right kidney, colon, small portion of pancreas, small intestine
    - **LUQ:** Spleen, stomach, left kidney, small portion of liver, majority of pancreas, colon, small intestine
    - **RLQ:** Appendix, colon, small intestine, right ureter, right ovary/fallopian tube
    - **LLQ:** Colon, small intestine, left ureter, left ovary/fallopian tube
    - **Midline:** Bladder, uterus, prostate

- **2. Musculoskeletal System**
  - **Connective Tissues & Skeletal Functions:**
    - **Ligaments** (bone to bone) • **Tendons** (muscle to bone)
    - **4 Functions:** Shape, protect vital organs, allow movement, store minerals & produce blood cells
    - **Bone Shapes:** Long, short, flat, irregular
  - **Axial Skeleton:**
    - **Skull:** **Cranium** (occipital, 2 parietal, 2 temporal, frontal) + **Face** (**14 bones**: 13 immovable [orbits, nasal, maxillae, zygomatic] + 1 movable **mandible**)
    - **Spinal Column (33 Vertebrae + Intervertebral Disks):** **Cervical (`C1–C7`)** • **Thoracic (`T1–T12`)** • **Lumbar (`L1–L5`)** • **Sacral (`S1–S5` fused)** • **Coccyx (`4 fused`)** *(cord injury = distal motor/sensory loss; column injury = usually pain alone)*
    - **Thorax:** **24 ribs** (12 pairs: **1–7** true, **8–10** false cartilage-attached, **11–12** floating) + **Sternum** (**manubrium**, body, **xiphoid process**)
  - **Appendicular Skeleton:**
    - **Pelvis:** Sacrum, coccyx, **iliac crests**, **pubis** (anterior/inferior), **ischium** (posterior/inferior)
    - **Lower Extremities:** Pelvic **acetabulum** + **femur** → knee (**patella**) → **tibia** (**medial malleolus**) & **fibula** (**lateral malleolus**) → tarsals (**calcaneus**), **5 metatarsals**, **14 toe phalanges** (2 big toe, 3 others)
    - **Upper Extremities:** **Clavicle** + **scapula** (**acromion**) → **humerus** → elbow (**ulna** with **olecranon**, **radius** thumb-side) → **8 carpals**, **metacarpals**, **phalanges** (2 thumb, 3 fingers)
  - **Joints, Movements, Fracture Blood Loss & Muscle Types:**
    - **6 Joint Types:** **Ball-and-socket** (shoulder, hip), **Hinged** (elbow, knee, finger), **Pivot** (`C1–C2`, wrist), **Gliding** (hand/foot), **Saddle** (ankle), **Condyloid** (wrist)
    - **Movements:** **Flexion / Extension**, **Abduction / Adduction**, **Circumduction** (360°), **Pronation / Supination**
    - **Fracture Blood Loss:** Femur = **1,000–2,000 mL**; Pelvis = up to entire **~5 L** (`70–80 mL/kg`) blood volume
    - **3 Muscle Types:** **Skeletal (Voluntary)** • **Smooth (Involuntary)** (vasoconstriction/vasodilation) • **Cardiac** (**automaticity** independent of CNS)

- **3. Respiratory, Circulatory & Nervous Systems**
  - **Respiratory System:**
    - **4 Functions:** **Respiration**, **Ventilation**, **Oxygenation** & CO2 removal, **Acid–base buffering** (brain cells begin dying in **~5 min** without O2)
    - **Upper Airway (Ends at Cricoid Cartilage):** Nasal cavity (**conchae**), **pharynx** (**nasopharynx, oropharynx, laryngopharynx/hypopharynx**), **larynx** (**thyroid cartilage**, **cricoid cartilage**, **vocal cords / glottis**, **epiglottis** preventing **aspiration**)
    - **Lower Airway & Lungs:** **Trachea** (**15–20** C-rings + **trachealis muscle**), **bronchi**, **bronchioles**, **alveoli** + **respiratory membrane** + **surfactant**; **visceral** & **parietal pleura** (**pleural cavity**)
    - **Mechanics & Breathing Rates:** **Diaphragm** (**60–70%**, phrenic nerve `C3–C5`) + **intercostals** (**30–40%**); **Adults:** `8–24/min` (avg `12–20`), **Older adults:** avg `20–22`, **Children:** `18–37`, **Infants:** `30–60`; **agonal respirations** = immediate ventilation
  - **Circulatory System:**
    - **Heart & Valves:** **Right atrium** (from **venae cavae**) → **tricuspid valve** → **right ventricle** → **pulmonary valve** → **pulmonary arteries** (O2-poor) → lungs → **pulmonary veins** (O2-rich) → **left atrium** → **mitral (bicuspid) valve** → **left ventricle** → **aortic valve** → **aorta**; conduction: SA → AV → bundle of His → Purkinje fibers; **coronary arteries**
    - **Vessels & Blood:** **Arteries** (*carotid, femoral, dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial, brachial, radial*) → **arterioles** → **capillaries** → **venules** → **veins**; **RBCs**, **WBCs**, **platelets**, **plasma** (albumin)
    - **Hemodynamics & Metabolism:** **Systolic / Diastolic BP**; **hydrostatic pressure** → **edema**; O2 (**97%** Hgb, **3%** plasma) & CO2 (**70%** bicarbonate, **23%** Hgb, **7%** plasma); **Aerobic** vs. **Anaerobic metabolism** (**hypoperfusion / shock**)
  - **Nervous System:**
    - **CNS:** **Cerebrum** (thought/sensory/motor/emotion), **Cerebellum** (balance/coordination), **Brainstem** (midbrain, pons, medulla: respiratory, cardiac, vasomotor centers) + spinal cord (~18 in) + CSF
    - **PNS:** Afferent (sensory) & efferent (motor); **Voluntary** vs. **Autonomic** (**sympathetic** fight-or-flight vs. **parasympathetic**)
    - **Consciousness:** Requires intact **reticular activating system (RAS)** + at least **1 cerebral hemisphere**

- **4. Endocrine, Integumentary, Digestive, Urinary & Reproductive Systems**
  - **Endocrine System & Adrenergic Receptors:**
    - **Glands:** Pineal (melatonin), Pituitary ("master"), Thyroid (metabolism), Parathyroid (Ca/P), Adrenals (epinephrine/norepinephrine), Pancreas Islets of Langerhans (insulin/glucagon), Gonads, Thymus (thymosin)
    - **Receptors:** **Alpha1** (vasoconstriction → cool/pale skin + sweat glands → clammy), **Alpha2** (regulates alpha1), **Beta1** (increases HR, contractility, conduction), **Beta2** (bronchodilation/vasodilation)
  - **Integumentary, Digestive, Urinary & Reproductive:**
    - **Skin:** **Epidermis** (4 cell layers, melanin), **Dermis** (vessels, follicles, sweat/oil glands, nerves), **Subcutaneous layer**
    - **Digestive:** Mouth, **esophagus**, stomach, **pancreas**, **liver** (RUQ; bile, sugar storage, clotting factors, detox), **spleen** (LUQ; blood filter/reservoir, no digestion), **gallbladder**, **small intestine** (duodenum, jejunum, ileum), **large intestine**
    - **Urinary & Reproductive:** 2 kidneys, 2 ureters, bladder, urethra; Male (testes, epididymis, vas deferens, prostate, penis) & Female (ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, vagina, vulva)

- **5. Pediatric & Geriatric Lifespan Differences**
  - **Pediatric Differences:**
    - **Airway:** Larger tongue; newborn trachea **4–5 mm** (vs. **20 mm** adult) & pliable; **obligate nose breathers**; narrowest at **cricoid cartilage** until **~6 years**; higher epiglottis
    - **Head & Chest:** Large occiput requires shoulder padding until **8–9 years** (adult proportions at **age 10**); infants **<6–7 months** cannot support head; posterior fontanelle closes by **3 mo**, anterior at **12–18 mo**; pliable horizontal ribs, **abdominal breathing**, retractions, and **grunting**; rate **>=60/min** = inadequate breathing; children **<5 yrs** breathe **2–3x faster**
    - **Cardiovascular, Metabolism & Skin:** Compensate via **tachycardia & vasoconstriction**; sudden hypotension after **>30%** blood loss; bradycardia = **late** hypoxia sign in children, **initial** in **newborns**; O2/glucose consumed **2–3x faster** (rapid **hypoglycemia**); high surface-area-to-mass ratio → rapid **hypothermia**
  - **Geriatric Changes (65+ Years: 16% in 2019 → 22% in 2040; >50% of EMS Calls by 2030):**
    - **Normal Aging Onset:** Around **age 30**; comorbidities and polypharmacy mask acute emergencies
    - **Cardiovascular & Respiratory:** Valve **stenosis**/regurgitation, **cardiac hypertrophy**, fewer pacemaker cells, **dysrhythmias**, **arteriosclerosis**, blunted **baroreceptors** (**orthostatic hypotension**); no new alveoli after **age 20**, blunted **chemoreceptors**, weaker cough/cilia
    - **Neuro, GI, Endocrine, MSK, Renal & Skin:** Neuron loss (starting **mid-20s**), **cataracts/glaucoma/macular degeneration/retinal detachment**, **presbycusis** (keep hearing aids in/on, do not shout), **peripheral neuropathy** (silent MI); reduced liver drug metabolism; type 2 diabetes risk; **osteoporosis** & **kyphosis** (**2 of 3** elderly patients); **1/3** loss of nephrons → **drug toxicity**; thin fragile skin

- **6. Medical Terminology**
  - **Three Word Parts:** **Combining form** (root + combining vowel, usually `o`, e.g., `cardi/o-`) + **Prefix** (beginning: direction, time, size) + **Suffix** (end: condition, procedure, *"pertaining to"* `-ac, -al, -ary, -eal, -ic, -ous`)
  - **Joining & Breakdown Examples:** `cardi/o-` + `-logy` = cardiology; `peri-` + `cardi/o-` + `-um` = pericardium; `my/o-` + `cardi/o-` + `-al` = myocardial; `bronch/o-` + `-itis` = bronchitis; `hepat/o-` + `-ic` = hepatic; `laryng/o-` + `trache/o-` + `bronch/o-` + `-itis` = laryngotracheobronchitis; `endo-` + `trache/o-` + `-al` = endotracheal; `hypo-` + `vol/o-` + `-emic` = hypovolemic; `hyper-` + `glyc/o-` + `-emia` = hyperglycemia; `a-` + `pne/o-` + `-a` = apnea; `tachy-` + `cardi/o-` + `-ia` = tachycardia; `brady-` + `pne/o-` + `-a` = bradypnea
