# EMS Operations, Legal, Safety & Communication

## 1. EMS Systems, Levels & Oversight

- **Practitioner Levels (Ascending Scope)**
  - **Emergency Medical Responder (EMR)**: First on scene with emergency care training.
  - **Emergency Medical Technician (EMT)**: Basic care and transport (oxygen therapy, ventilation equipment, pulse oximetry, auto BP, limited meds).
  - **Advanced Emergency Medical Technician (AEMT)**: EMT skills + advanced airways, blood glucose monitoring, IV/IO infusions, and select medications.
  - **Paramedic**: Highest prehospital care (advanced assessment, field impression, invasive and drug interventions).
- **System Concepts & Healthcare Models**
  - **Prehospital Care**: Emergency medical treatment given before transport to a hospital or facility (out-of-hospital care).
  - **Community Paramedicine (CP)**: Services provided by the EMS agency and personnel that are administratively and clinically integrated with other healthcare entities.
  - **Mobile Integrated Healthcare (MIH)**: Patient-centered healthcare in the out-of-hospital environment using mobile technology and resources integrated with EMS.
  - **Evidence-Based Medicine**: Medical practice based on scientific evidence proving procedures, medications, and equipment improve patient outcomes.
  - **Evidence-Based Guidelines (EBG)**: Development of medical guidelines based on scientific evidence and research studies.
  - **Quality Improvement (QI)**: System of internal and external reviews and audits of an EMS system to ensure high-quality care.
- **Medical Direction & Oversight**
  - **Medical Director**: Physician legally responsible for the clinical and patient care aspects of an EMS system.
  - **Medical Oversight**: Medical director's broad clinical and administrative responsibilities for individual personnel and the entire EMS system.
  - **Medical Direction**: Medical policies, procedures, and practices available to EMS providers off-line or on-line.
    - **Indirect Medical Oversight**: Routine duties of the EMS medical director, including creating protocols and standing orders.
      - **Off-Line Medical Direction**: Medical policies, procedures, and practices established in written guidelines.
      - **Protocols**: Policies and procedures for all components of an EMS system (orders or standing orders).
      - **Standing Orders**: Preauthorized treatment procedures; a type of treatment protocol.
    - **Direct Medical Oversight**: Real-time oversight or medical direction provided by a physician seeking immediate feedback or direction.
      - **On-Line Medical Direction**: Direct orders from a physician to a prehospital care provider given by radio or telephone.
      - **On-Scene Medical Direction**: Medical direction provided by an EMS medical director physician who is on scene with the crew.

## 2. Medical-Legal & Consent

- **Types of Consent**
  - **Consent**: Permission that must be obtained before care is rendered.
  - **Expressed Consent**: Permission obtained from every conscious adult who has the capacity to understand and make a reasoned judgment before emergency treatment.
  - **Informed Consent**: Consent for treatment given by a patient with capacity based on full disclosure of possible risks and consequences.
  - **Implied Consent**: Assumption in a true emergency that an unresponsive/irrational patient at significant risk of death, disability, or deterioration would agree to treatment (emergency doctrine).
  - **Involuntary Consent**: Consent assumed when the patient either lacks capacity to understand/reason or is legally not permitted to make their own medical decisions.
  - **Minor Consent**: Permission obtained from a parent or legal guardian for emergency treatment of a minor or an adult legally ruled mentally incompetent.
- **Advance Directives & End-of-Life**
  - **Advance Directive**: Instructions written in advance (DNR order, living will, or durable power of attorney).
  - **Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) Order**: Legal document signed by patient and physician indicating which life-sustaining measures should be taken when heart and respiratory functions cease.
  - **Living Will**: Legal document delineating the signer's wishes about general healthcare issues such as long-term life support measures.
  - **Durable Power Of Attorney**: Legal document designating a person legally empowered to make healthcare decisions if the signer cannot do so.
  - **Physician Orders For Life-Sustaining Treatment (POLST)**: Orders identifying the desired level of life-sustaining treatment in patients with terminal or life-threatening illness (MOLST).
- **Duties, Standards & Federal Laws**
  - **Scope Of Practice**: Actions and care an EMT is legally allowed to perform, typically defined by state laws.
  - **Standard Of Care**: Emergency care expected to be given to a patient by any trained EMT under similar circumstances.
  - **Duty To Act**: Obligation to care for a patient who requires it.
  - **Good Samaritan Law**: Law providing immunity from liability for acts performed in good faith at a medical emergency unless constituting gross negligence.
  - **Health Insurance Portability And Accountability Act (HIPAA)**: Federal law (1996) protecting patient health information privacy and giving patients control over its distribution/use.
  - **Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (COBRA)**: Federal regulation ensuring public access to emergency healthcare regardless of ability to pay.
  - **Emergency Medical Treatment And Active Labor Act (EMTALA)**: Federal regulation ensuring emergency healthcare access regardless of ability to pay ("anti-patient-dumping statute").
  - **Americans With Disabilities Act (ADA)**: Federal law (1990) protecting individuals with documented disabilities from employment discrimination.
- **Negligence & Torts**
  - **Abandonment**: Discontinuing emergency care without ensuring another healthcare professional with equivalent or better training takes over.
  - **Negligence**: Deviating from an accepted standard of care through carelessness, inattention, disregard, inadvertence, or oversight, resulting in further patient injury.
  - **Proximate Cause**: Deviating from an accepted standard of care through carelessness, inattention, disregard, inadvertence, or oversight, resulting in further patient injury.
  - **Tort**: A wrongful act, injury, or damage.
  - **Intentional Tort**: A wrongful act, injury, or damage committed knowingly.
    - **Assault**: Willful threat to inflict harm on a person.
    - **Battery**: Act of touching a person unlawfully without their consent.
    - **False Imprisonment**: Intentional and unjustifiable detention of a person without consent or legal authority.
    - **Defamation**: Intentional false communication that injures another person's reputation or good name.
      - **Libel**: Injuring reputation/good name in writing or mass media with malicious intent or reckless disregard for falsity.
      - **Slander**: Injuring reputation/good name through spoken statements with malicious intent or reckless disregard for falsity.

## 3. Infection Control & Provider Wellness

- **Infection Control & Decontamination**
  - **Pathogens**: Microorganisms such as bacteria and viruses that cause disease.
  - **Standard Precautions**: Method of preventing infection based on the premise that all blood and body fluids are infectious (formerly body substance isolation).
  - **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)**: Equipment worn to protect against injury and disease.
  - **Purified Protein Derivative (PPD) Tuberculin Test**: Test to determine tuberculosis infection based on a positive reaction to tuberculin.
  - **Decontamination Levels**
    - **Cleaning**: Process of washing a soiled object with soap and water.
    - **Disinfecting**: Using a disinfectant (alcohol or bleach) to kill many microorganisms on an object's surface.
    - **Sterilization**: Subjecting an object to chemical/physical substances (typically superheated steam in an autoclave) to kill all surface microorganisms.
- **Stress & Mental Health**
  - **Critical Incident**: Any situation causing unusually strong emotions that interfere with the ability to function.
  - **Defusing**: Session held prior to CISD for directly involved personnel to vent emotions and get information before the CISD.
  - **Critical Incident Stress Debriefing (CISD)**: Session held 24–72 hours after a critical incident where peer counselors and mental health professionals help personnel work through emotions.
  - **Defense Mechanisms**: Psychological coping strategies used to protect against unwanted feelings or thoughts.
  - **Burnout**: Condition resulting from chronic job stress, characterized by irritability and fatigue that markedly decrease effectiveness.

## 4. Lifting, Moving & Body Mechanics

- **Body Mechanics & Spinal Alignment**
  - **Body Mechanics**: Application of kinesiology (study of muscles and body movement) to posture and lifting.
  - **Kyphosis**: Abnormal curvature of the spine with convexity backward (slouch).
  - **Lordosis**: Abnormal anterior convexity of the spine (swayback).
  - **Power Lift**: Feet apart, knees bent, back/abdominal muscles tightened, back straight, drive force through heels/arches, upper body rises before hips.
  - **Power Grip**: Palm and fingers in complete contact with the object and all fingers bent at the same angle.
- **Patient Moves**
  - **Emergency Move**: Patient move performed when there is immediate danger to the patient or rescuer.
  - **Urgent Move**: Patient move made because there is an immediate threat to life due to the patient's condition, requiring quick transport.
  - **Nonurgent Move**: Patient move made when no immediate threat to life exists.

## 5. Communication, Documentation & Culture

- **Interpersonal Communication & Questioning**
  - **Communication**: Dynamic process incorporating verbal and nonverbal expressions into meaningful messages received and interpreted by others.
  - **Encoding**: Process of converting information into a message.
  - **Decoding**: Process by which a received message is translated and interpreted.
  - **Feedback**: Any information an individual receives about their behavior.
  - **Gestures**: Nonverbal body movements that convey meaning to others.
  - **Haptics**: The study of touching.
  - **Intimate Zone**: In American culture, the space within less than 12 feet of an individual.
  - **Questioning Techniques**
    - **Open-Ended Questions**: Questions enabling the patient to give a detailed response in their own words.
    - **Closed-Ended Questions**: Questions requiring only a "yes" or "no" answer.
    - **Leading Questions**: Questions suggesting an answer guided by the individual asking.
- **Culture**
  - **Culture**: Thoughts, communications, actions, and values of a racial, ethnic, religious, or social group.
  - **Ethnocentrism**: Tendency to evaluate other cultures/groups by one's own culture's standards, often viewing one's own way of life as superior.
- **Radio & Telemetry Systems**
  - **Base Station**: Central dispatch and coordination area of an EMS communications system in contact with all other elements.
  - **Repeaters**: Devices receiving transmissions from low-powered sources (mobile/portable radios) and rebroadcasting at another frequency and higher power.
  - **Encoder**: Device breaking down sound waves into unique digital codes for radio transmission.
  - **Decoder**: Device recognizing and responding only to certain codes imposed on radio broadcasts.
  - **Mobile Data Terminal**: Cab-mounted device receiving digital radio signals, displaying info on screen, and sometimes printing hard copies.
  - **Telemetry**: Process of transmitting data through an automated communications process, usually wireless data transfer.
- **Patient Reporting & Documentation**
  - **SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, And Recommendation)**: Method of organizing communications about a patient.
  - **Pertinent Negatives**: Expected signs/symptoms based on chief complaint or physical exam that are denied by the patient or absent on exam.
  - **Prehospital Care Report (PCR)**: Documentation of an EMT's contact with a patient.
  - **Minimum Data Set**: Minimum information the U.S. DOT has determined should be included on all PCRs.
  - **Triage Tag**: Tag containing key information attached to a patient during a multiple-casualty incident.
