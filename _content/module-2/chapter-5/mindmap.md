# Chapter 5: Communication
- **1. Key Terms, History & EMS Hardware Systems**
  - **Key Terms:**
    - *Hardware & Data:* base station, mobile data terminal, repeaters, encoder, decoder, telemetry
    - *Process & Nonverbal:* communication, encoding, decoding, feedback, gestures, haptics, intimate zone, defense mechanisms
    - *Reporting & Interviewing:* SBAR (situation, background, assessment, and recommendation), open-ended questions, closed-ended questions, leading questions
    - *Culture:* culture, ethnocentrism
  - **Historical Milestones:**
    - **EMS Act of 1973 (Public Law 93-154):** Made communications 1 of **15 essential EMS components** and funded VHF/UHF radios
    - **FirstNet (2012, Public Law 112-96):** Nationwide public safety broadband network reserved for public safety including EMS
    - **Modern Capabilities:** NEMSIS ePCR longitudinal data, 12-lead ECG transmission, MIH/community paramedicine links
  - **Core System Components:**
    - **Base Station:** Central dispatch/coordination hub on high terrain near command hospital; **80–150 watts**; antenna efficiency offsets FCC power limits and man-made noise
    - **Land Mobile Radio Systems (LMRS):** UHF, VHF, or **700- to 800-MHz** networks linking base, vehicles, and hospitals
    - **Mobile Radios:** Vehicle-mounted transmitter/receivers; **20–50 watts**; **10- to 15-mile** range on average terrain
    - **Portable Radios:** Handheld transmitter/receivers; **1–5 watts**; limited range boosted via repeaters
    - **Repeaters:** Vehicle-mounted or fixed sites receiving low-power signals and rebroadcasting at another frequency and higher power
    - **Digital Equipment:** **Encoder** (converts sound to digital code) + **Decoder** (recognizes specific codes so multiple units share frequencies) + **Mobile Data Terminal (MDT)** (displays/prints dispatch data and status buttons)
    - **Cellular Phones:** Overlapping **2 to 40 sq mi** cells; high audio quality, privacy, and image/data transfer, but vulnerable to overload in MCIs
    - **Telemetry & Telemedicine:** Automated wireless data transfer (vitals, 12-lead ECGs) + remote telehealth consults (e.g., MIH and **ET3** alternate destinations)
    - **Land Mobile Satellite:** Radio alternative for sparse, remote rural areas
  - **FCC Regulation & System Maintenance:**
    - **FCC Duties:** Licenses base stations, assigns call signs/frequencies, approves gear, sets power limits, prevents interference, prohibits profanity/obscenity
    - **Maintenance:** Daily battery checks + spare charged batteries, clean with damp cloth/mild detergent, check frequency drift, maintain generator/cellular backups

- **2. Communicating Within the EMS System**
  - **11-Step Radio Transmission Progression:**
    - Dispatch sends call → unit acknowledges → unit en route → notify other agencies if needed → unit on scene → contact medical direction for orders → unit leaving scene → radio report to receiving hospital → arrival at hospital → leaving hospital → back at station
  - **16 Radio Ground Rules:**
    - Close windows to cut noise; listen before transmitting; press PTT and wait **1 second** before speaking; hold mic **2–3 inches** from lips; speak calmly/slowly in monotone
    - Call target unit first, then ID yourself; wait for *"go ahead"* (or pause on *"stand by"*); pause every **30 seconds** on long transmissions
    - Use plain English (no slang, *"Be advised"*, excessive *"Please/Thank you"*, or unapproved codes); state confusing numbers as word + digits (*"thirteen, one-three"*)
    - State objective/pertinent subjective facts only (no diagnoses); never broadcast patient names (HIPAA/public scanners); never use profanity
    - Use **closed-loop ("echo") communication** repeating orders word for word; write down addresses/orders; use *"we"* instead of *"I"*; use *"affirmative/negative"* instead of *"yes/no"*; end with *"Over"*
  - **Dispatch & AACN:**
    - **AACN (Advanced Automatic Collision Notification):** Transmits crash location, velocity change, force direction, airbag deployment, rollover, and multiple impacts (2009 CDC triage criteria)
    - **9 Key Dispatch Contacts:** (1) Acknowledge call, (2) en route, (3) ETA/delays, (4) on scene/resource requests, (5) departing scene + destination/patient count/ETA, (6) arrival at hospital/rendezvous, (7) clear/available, (8) leaving hospital, (9) back at station
  - **Medical Direction, Hospital Reports & Handoffs:**
    - **Medical Direction & Pre-Transport Report:** Unit ID/level, age/sex, chief complaint, special alerts (trauma, stroke, STEMI), pertinent HPI/scene/MOI, major PMH, mental status, baseline vitals, exam findings, care given, patient response, current condition, requests/orders, ETA
    - **Order Safety:** Repeat orders word for word (including *"do not"* orders); clarify unclear orders; question orders that seem inappropriate
    - **SBAR Format:** **S**ituation (reason/chief complaint) • **B**ackground (PMH/response so far) • **A**ssessment (mental status, vitals, neuro, BGL, GCS) • **R**ecommendation (specific order/action requested)
    - **In-Person Oral Handoff Report:** Chief complaint, en-route vitals, en-route treatment + response, pertinent history not yet reported, plus written PCR copy

- **3. Team Dynamics, Radio Codes & Terminology**
  - **Taking Charge on Scene:**
    - Project confidence through appearance and demeanor; take a `<=1 minute` update from EMRs if patient is alert without immediate life threats; go straight to unresponsive/critical patients
    - **Transfer of Authority:** Ask police/rescue *"We’re Emergency Medical Technicians. Is there anything we need to know before we provide emergency care?"*; ask physicians *"We’re certified EMTs, Doctor. How can we help?"*
  - **Radio Codes & Military Time:**
    - **Radio Codes (APCO Ten-Codes like `10-4`):** Shorten airtime and shield info from bystanders, but fail if not universally understood; most EMS systems favor plain English
    - **Military Time:** `0100–1200` (`1:00 A.M.–Noon`), `1300–2400` (`1:00 P.M.–Midnight`); e.g., `1427 hours` = `2:27 P.M.`, `0030 hours` = `12:30 A.M.`
  - **Key Radio Terms:**
    - **Break** (pause for hospital interrupt) • **Clear** (end of transmission) • **Come in** (request acknowledgment) • **Copy / 10-4** (received & understood) • **Go ahead** (proceed) • **Landline** (telephone) • **Over** (end of message, awaiting reply) • **Repeat/say again** (did not understand) • **Spell out** (phonetic spelling) • **Stand by** (wait)

- **4. Therapeutic Communication, Nonverbal Cues & Special Populations**
  - **Communication Loop:**
    - **Sender** encodes message → **Receiver** decodes message (filtered by values/experience) → **Feedback** encoded and returned in a continuous loop
  - **Nine Therapeutic Responses:**
    - **Facilitation** (*"go on"*) • **Clarification** (ask to repeat/clarify) • **Summary** (rephrase & verify) • **Explanation** (plain-language preview before procedures) • **Silence** (time to think; use carefully in depression) • **Reflection** (mirror feelings/statements) • **Empathy** ("in patient's shoes") • **Confrontation** (assertive, non-aggressive reality check) • **Facilitated Communication** (physical support for letterboard/keyboard without influencing content)
  - **Patient Contact & The Three Cs:**
    - Convey **Competence, Confidence, and Compassion**; dress professionally; use formal titles (*Mr./Mrs.*) for older adults; ask explicit permission before helping
    - Expect initial denial/refusal from **defense mechanisms** (use calm distraction/focus shift); protect privacy from crowds; control lighting and noise (ask before turning off TV)
  - **Nonverbal Communication (Dominates When Verbal Cues Conflict):**
    - **Posture:** Open arms/hands/relaxed shoulders; match eye level (stand above only for hostile safety; crouch below for children/elderly)
    - **Distance Zones:** **Intimate (`<1½ ft`)** → **Personal (`1½–4 ft`, verbal care)** → **Social (`4–12 ft`)** → **Public (`>=12 ft`)**
    - **Gestures, Eye Contact & Haptics:** Watch facial expressions; adjust eye contact for cultural norms; ask permission before comforting touch (**haptics**) if patient seems reluctant
  - **Interviewing & Questioning:**
    - Use **open-ended questions** (*"How are you feeling?"*) for rich detail and **closed-ended questions** (*"When did pain start?"*) for specific facts or overly talkative patients
    - **Pitfalls to Avoid:** **Leading questions** (*"It really hurts, doesn't it?"*), interrupting, false assurances/lying, diagnosing, blame-implying *"why"* questions
    - **Hostile/Unmotivated Patients:** Stage for police on unsafe scenes; set clear behavioral boundaries with safe alternatives; use phone interpreter lines for language barriers
  - **Transcultural & Special Populations:**
    - **Culture & Ethnocentrism:** Avoid stereotyping or viewing your culture as superior (**ethnocentrism**); respect folk medicine, space, and eye-contact norms; document interpreter used
    - **Elderly & Children:** Speak slowly/distinctly without talking down to older adults (assist with glasses/hearing aids); keep parents calm, crouch to child's eye level, use simple words, and always tell children the truth
    - **Family-Centered Care (1987, Surgeon General Koop / EMSC):** Partners with families who know their child's baseline needs and communication style
