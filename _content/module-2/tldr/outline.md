# Module 1: Preparatory (Chapters 1–6) — 5-Mile-High TL;DR

## The Big Picture: How Module 1 Fits Together
Every EMS call runs on the same operational loop:
1. **Systems & Public Health (Ch. 1):** You practice under a state license and a physician **Medical Director** inside a broader health care and public health network.
2. **Safety & Wellness (Ch. 2):** You protect yourself first (**Self → Crew → Patient → Bystanders**) through scene control, **Standard Precautions/PPE**, stress management, and fitness.
3. **Law & Ethics (Ch. 3):** You stay inside your **scope of practice**, meet the **standard of care**, obtain valid **consent** (or verify **advance directives**), and avoid negligence and intentional torts.
4. **Documentation (Ch. 4):** You prove what happened—clinically, legally, and financially—through an objective, **NEMSIS/DOT-compliant PCR** (*"If it wasn’t written down, it wasn’t done"*).
5. **Communication (Ch. 5):** You link dispatch, medical direction, receiving hospitals, and patients via disciplined radio protocols (**SBAR**, closed-loop echo) and empathetic, culturally aware interviewing.
6. **Lifting & Moving (Ch. 6):** You move patients safely from scene to stretcher to ambulance/aircraft using **body mechanics**, matched equipment, and proper clinical positioning.

## Chapter 1: EMS Systems, Research, and Public Health
- **Evolution & Governance:** Evolved from funeral-home transport via wartime trauma lessons, the **1966 White Paper & Highway Safety Act**, the **1973 EMS System Act**, the **2006 National EMS Scope of Practice Model**, and **EMS Agenda 2050**. States independently regulate EMS via a **lead state EMS agency** (planning, coordination, regulation, licensing) benchmarked by NHTSA's **10 Assessment Standards**.
- **911 Access & Models:** Calls route to a **PSAP** using landline **E-911** (ANI phone #, ALI address), **EMDs** (pre-arrival instructions), **Wireless Phase I** (cell tower) / **Phase II** (lat/long within **50–300 m**), and **VoIP** (registered address). Six delivery models: **Fire-based, Municipal ("Third Service"), Private, Hospital-based, Law Enforcement, and Public Safety Officer**.
- **4 Provider Tiers & Specialty Centers:**
  - **EMR** (first-on-scene basic care) → **EMT** (basic care/transport, pulse ox, auto BP, limited meds) → **AEMT** (advanced airways, BGL, IV/IO, select meds) → **Paramedic** (highest prehospital care).
  - **10 Specialty Centers:** Trauma, Burn, Obstetrical, Pediatric, Poison, Stroke, Cardiac, Hyperbaric, Spine Injury, Psychiatric.
  - **MIH-CP:** Mobile Integrated Healthcare & Community Paramedicine expand EMS into chronic disease/post-discharge care, telephone triage, and underserved access.
- **EMT Duties, Medical Oversight, QI & Research:**
  - Lift/carry up to **125 lbs**; good visual acuity and **color vision**; protected by **ADA (1990)** reasonable accommodations. Deliver **both verbal and written reports (PCR)** before leaving the ED.
  - Practice as the designated agent of the **Medical Director**: **Indirect (Off-Line)** = written **Protocols** & **Standing Orders** vs. **Direct** = **On-Line** (radio/phone) & **On-Scene**.
  - **CQI** fixes *system* flaws (non-punitive) using **closed-loop communication**, **Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM)**, **NEMSIS**, **CARES**, and the **8-step cyclical EBG model**.
- **Public Health Integration:** Targets populations *before* onset (CDC **3 Core Functions**: Assessment, Policy Development, Assurance), boosting U.S. life expectancy from **47.3 yrs (1900)** to **78.4 yrs (2018)** (**77.0 yrs** in 2020/2022). EMTs track **5 SDOH domains** and practice **Primary** (vaccines, seatbelts/helmets/DUI), **Secondary** (BP checks, reporting abuse, public AEDs), and **Tertiary** (acute emergency care) prevention.

## Chapter 2: Workforce Safety and Wellness
- **Emotional Care & Grief:** Support patients/families through **Kübler-Ross's 5 stages** (*Denial, Anger, Bargaining, Depression, Acceptance*) with dignity and honesty (no false assurances). Clean postmortem blood/secretions and elevate head briefly if allowed—**except at possible crime scenes** (preserve evidence until cleared by police/ME).
- **Stress & CISM:** Manage **acute**, **delayed (PTSD)**, and **cumulative (burnout)** stress across 5 domains (Thinking, Psychological, Physical, Behavioral, Social). **CISM** pairs **Defusing** (`<=8 hrs`, `30–45 min`, informal venting) with **CISD** (**24–72 hrs**, **7 phases**, confidential/non-punitive).
- **Infection Control & Scene Safety:**
  - Never act outside your medical responder role unless trained and authorized.
  - Know the **5 pathogen groups** (bacteria, viruses [COVID-19, Ebola, Zika], fungi, protozoa [malaria], helminths) and **OSHA Standard Precautions** (airborne exposure reported to DO in **24 hrs**; bloodborne in **48 hrs**).
  - **6-step CDC hand wash** (`>=20 sec` scrub) or `>=60%` alcohol sanitizer; treat all used PPE as contaminated; wear **HEPA/N-95** for TB/COVID-19 (clean-shaven, annual fit test); wash soiled uniforms `>=25 min` in hot soapy water; **Cleaning** → **Disinfecting** → **Sterilization** (autoclave); never recap sharps; maintain PPD TB screening and vaccines (COVID-19, Tdap q10y, Hep B offered free within **10 days**, flu, MMR, varicella).
  - Scout hazmat with binoculars + DOT *ERG*; wear **ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2/3** or **207 PSV** vests on roadways (`Class 1 <25 mph`, `Class 2 >=25 mph`); stage for police on violent/crime scenes.
- **Wellness & Glove Removal:** Cardio **30–40 min, >=3 days/wk** (`Target HR = [((220 - Age) - RHR) × 0.70] + RHR`); sleep **8–10 hrs/day** (NASEMSO shifts **<24 hrs**); `>30%` first responders have mental health diagnosis and untreated PTSD = **6x** suicide risk (**1-800-273-8255**). Remove gloves **contaminated-to-contaminated, clean-to-clean** (7 steps).

## Chapter 3: Medical, Legal, and Ethical Issues
- **Scope, Standards & Duties:** **Scope of practice** (state law: what is legal) vs. **Standard of care** ("reasonable person standard": expected quality). **Duty to act** applies on duty and whenever stopping/responding off duty (cannot leave until equal/higher training takes over). **Good Samaritan laws** (CA **1959**) protect unpaid good-faith care unless **gross negligence**; **sovereign immunity** covers government EMS only.
- **Consent & Advance Directives:**
  - **4 Consent Types:** **Expressed** (rational adult), **Implied** (unresponsive/irrational emergency doctrine), **Minor** (parent/guardian `<18` or `<21`; 6 emergent conditions; emancipated minor rules), **Involuntary** (mentally incompetent or law enforcement custody).
  - **Advance Directives:** **Living Will** (2 physicians), **Durable Power of Attorney** (1 physician), **DNR / DNRCC** (valid physical document required; act only in cardiac arrest), **POLST/MOLST** (`<=1 yr` prognosis, pre-arrest treatment limits).
  - **Refusals:** Verify medical **capacity** (vs. legal **competence**), re-persuade, consult medical direction, document thoroughly, and obtain signed release + witness.
- **Liability, Privacy & Special Rules:**
  - **Negligence** requires all **4 elements**: **Duty to act + Breach + Damages + Proximate cause** (*res ipsa loquitur*; *negligence per se*).
  - **5 Intentional Torts:** **Abandonment**, **Assault**, **Battery**, **False Imprisonment**, **Defamation** (*Slander* = spoken; *Libel* = written).
  - **HIPAA (1996):** 5 legal disclosures without consent (continuing care, mandatory reporting, police investigation, billing, subpoena). **COBRA/EMTALA** ("anti-patient-dumping") requires stabilization and scope-matched transfer crews.
  - **Death, Crime Scenes & Safe-Haven:** Treat organ donors as patient first, donor last; **accidental hypothermia** patients are not dead until rewarmed; know **5 presumptive** vs. **4 obvious** signs of death and **2022 NASEMSO** 8 biological exclusions; preserve crime-scene evidence; follow mandatory abuse/crime reporting and **Baby Safe-Haven laws** (TX **1999**, 3 days to 1 yr).

## Chapter 4: Documentation
- **Goals & Golden Rules:** Serves **continuity of care, legal protection, and reimbursement**. *"If it wasn’t written down, it wasn’t done"* & *"If it wasn’t done, don’t write it down."*
- **U.S. DOT Minimum Data Set & PCR Structure:**
  - **8 Patient Elements** (including BP `>3 yrs`, capillary refill `<6 yrs`) + **6 Administrative Elements** with **synchronous clocks**.
  - Record **>=2 complete sets of vitals** (with position and times), chief complaint in quotes, objective signs vs. pertinent subjective symptoms, **pertinent negatives**, and chronological treatments/responses.
- **2022 NASEMSO Guideline & NEMSIS v3.4:** Mandatory timestamps (**unit notified** & **back in service**); **Primary impression** = most acute problem; **Tiered vitals** (Routine → Initial → Full); aspirin **324 mg** PTA = *"medication already taken"*; procedure attempts = **"1"**; **Mandatory** vs. **Required** fields (`d` and `e` datasets).
- **Narratives, Abbreviations & Legal/Billing Rules:**
  - Organize narratives with **SOAP/SOAPIE**, **CHART/DCHART**, or **CHEATED**. Never invent abbreviations; writing terms out is preferred (`PE` = physical exam or pulmonary embolism).
  - **Zero Falsification** (**False Claims Act**); correct paper errors before submission with a **single horizontal line + initials** (after submission: add note + date/time + notify recipients; ePCR: timestamped amendment). Use **drop reports**, **MCI triage tags**, and objective **special reports**.
  - **Medical Necessity:** Commercial (**prudent layperson**), Medicare (**42 CFR § 410.40(d)**), Medicaid (state criteria), and **ET3** (CMS/HHS 2019 / Jan 5, 2020: alternate destinations & telehealth/on-scene non-transport).

## Chapter 5: Communication
- **Hardware & FCC Regulation:** **EMS Act of 1973** (1 of 15 components) & **FirstNet (2012)**. **Base Station** (80–150 W), **LMRS** (700–800 MHz), **Mobile** (20–50 W, 10–15 mi), **Portable** (1–5 W), **Repeaters**, **Encoder/Decoder**, **MDT**, **Cellular** (2–40 sq mi; can overload in MCIs), **Telemetry**, **Telemedicine**, **Satellite**. **FCC** licenses base stations, assigns frequencies, sets power limits, and bans profanity.
- **Radio Ground Rules & Handoffs:**
  - Wait **1 sec** after PTT, hold mic **2–3 in** from lips, pause at **30 sec**, speak plain English, never broadcast patient names, use **closed-loop ("echo")** repeat-back, say *"we"*, *"affirmative/negative"*, and *"Over"*.
  - Report at **9 dispatch points** using **24-hr military time** (`0100–2400`); structure medical direction/handoff reports with **SBAR** (**S**ituation, **B**ackground, **A**ssessment, **R**ecommendation); project confidence on scene and execute clean authority transfers with police/rescue or on-scene physicians.
- **Therapeutic & Nonverbal Communication:**
  - **Sender → Encoding → Receiver → Decoding → Feedback** loop + **9 therapeutic responses** (Facilitation, Clarification, Summary, Explanation, Silence, Reflection, Empathy, Confrontation, Facilitated Communication).
  - Project **Competence, Confidence, and Compassion**; respect nonverbal cues and distance zones (**Intimate `<1½ ft`**, **Personal `1½–4 ft`**, **Social `4–12 ft`**, **Public `>=12 ft`**); favor **open-ended** and **closed-ended** questions while avoiding **leading questions**; overcome **ethnocentrism**; adapt for elderly, pediatric (truth + eye level), hostile, and **Family-Centered Care (1987)** calls.

## Chapter 6: Lifting and Moving Patients
- **Body Mechanics & Lifting:**
  - **4 Principles:** Keep weight close; engage legs/hips/glutes/abs; stack shoulders/hips/feet (never lift & twist); reduce lift height/distance. Avoid **lordosis** (swayback) and **kyphosis** (slouch).
  - **Power Lift & Power Grip** (hands `>=10 in` apart, drive through heels/arches, upper body before hips); **Squat Lift** (weak leg forward); **One-Handed Carry** (trunk tilt `<=45°`). Heavy 2-rescuer carries `<=1 min`; 100% effort `6 sec`, 50% effort `1 min`; forward reach `15–20 in`; push rather than pull.
- **Move Categories:**
  - **Emergency Moves** (immediate danger or lifesaving access; pull along long axis): Armpit-Forearm Drag, Shirt Drag, Blanket Drag.
  - **Urgent Moves** (immediate life threat): **Rapid Extrication** (MVC) & **Self-Extrication**.
  - **Nonurgent Moves:** Walking assist (no dizziness, chest pain, dyspnea, leg/spinal injury), Direct Ground Lift, Extremity Lift (no spinal/limb injury), Direct Carry, Draw Sheet.
- **Packaging, Devices, Stairs & Air Transport:**
  - **Weight Ratings:** Wheeled stretcher (**650 lbs**), Bariatric (**850–1,600 lbs**), Portable (**350 lbs**), Stair Chair (**500 lbs** powered; contraindicated in AMS, spinal, or lower-extremity injury), Scoop (**300 lbs**; not for spinal injury), Backboard/Vacuum Mattress, Stokes Basket, Reeves.
  - **Carrying & Stairs:** `>=3` straps; secure hands inside; **feet first down / head first up**; stronger EMT at head; **spotter** behind foot EMT downstairs / ahead of head EMT upstairs; Diamond vs. Rectangular 4-person carry.
  - **Positioning & Air Medical:** Unresponsive non-trauma = **left lateral recumbent**; shock = supine; **pregnancy `>20 wks`** = left side or right hip elevated (**supine hypotensive syndrome**); prolapsed cord = hips elevated; **Neonatal Isolette**; follow all **13 air-medical packaging rules** (hazmat decon first, avoid tail rotor, IV bag on chest).
