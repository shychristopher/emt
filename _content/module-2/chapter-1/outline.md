## Part I: EMS Systems, Clinical Roles, and Evidence-Based Practice

### 1.1: Introduction
* **Health Care Continuum:** Positions prehospital emergency care within the broader continuum from sudden illness or injury onset through hospital stabilization, rehabilitation, or discharge.
* **Dual Foundation of Modern EMS:** Combines immediate out-of-hospital clinical care with system organization, evidence-based guidelines, quality improvement, and population-level public health integration.
* **Public Health Burden:** Catastrophic accidents and sudden illnesses cause thousands of preventable deaths and permanent disabilities annually when EMS is inadequate or unavailable.

---

### 1.2: The Emergency Medical Services (EMS) System

#### A. Historical Evolution & Milestones
* **Early Origins:** Early ambulances frequently operated out of local funeral homes, prioritizing rapid transport over active medical treatment prior to hospital arrival.
* **Military Trauma Influence:**
  * *Korean & Vietnam Conflicts:* Demonstrated that immediate field stabilization before transport significantly improved survival, driving civilian EMS toward structured prehospital medical care.
  * *Iraq & Afghanistan Conflicts:* Continued to advance modern civilian trauma care through battlefield resuscitation and hemorrhage-control lessons.
* **Key Legislative, Policy & Educational Timeline:**

| Year | Milestone / Document | Significance to EMS Development |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1960s–1970s** | **CPR & Public BLS Training** | CPR techniques developed in the 1960s; AHA began teaching CPR/BLS to the public in the 1970s (now a standard EMT course prerequisite). |
| **1966** | ***Accidental Death and Disability: The Neglected Disease of Modern Society*** | National Academy of Sciences / National Research Council "White Paper" exposing severe deficiencies in prehospital care and spurring ambulance, training, and clinical reforms. |
| **1966** | **Highway Safety Act** | Required states to meet federal highway safety standards (including emergency services), placing DOT and NHTSA in charge of system and national curriculum development. |
| **1973** | **Emergency Medical Services System Act** | Provided major federal funding for regional EMS system planning, implementation, staffing, and training. |
| **1993** | **NREMT Education and Practice Blueprint** | Standardized training and education frameworks to guide national curriculum development. |
| **1996 & 2000** | **NHTSA *EMS Agendas for the Future*** | The 1996 Agenda pushed for full integration of EMS into the broader U.S. health care system; the 2000 *Education Agenda* promoted national consistency across education, certification, and licensure. |
| **2005–2006** | **Core Content, Scope of Practice & IOM Report** | NHTSA/HRSA published *National EMS Core Content* (2005) and *National EMS Scope of Practice Model* (2006, defining 4 provider levels). IOM published *EMS at the Crossroads* (2006), advocating state reciprocity, paramedic program accreditation, and national certification for licensure. |
| **2014–2018** | ***EMS Agenda 2050*** | Initiated via NEMSAC recommendation and developed through national stakeholder meetings to establish a 30-year, people-centered vision for EMS. |

#### B. Technical Assistance Program Assessment Standards & System Design
* **State Authority:** Individual states design and regulate their own EMS systems independently of the federal government, using NHTSA's **10 Technical Assistance Program Assessment Standards** as benchmarks:
  1. **Regulation and policy:** State laws, regulations, policies, procedures, and a lead state EMS agency.
  2. **Resource management:** Central control ensuring equitable community and patient access to emergency care.
  3. **Human resources and training:** Ambulance and transport personnel trained to at least the EMT level.
  4. **Transportation:** Safe, reliable ground or air ambulance transport.
  5. **Facilities:** Timely delivery of seriously ill or injured patients to an appropriate medical facility.
  6. **Communications:** Public access plus reliable communication among dispatch, EMS units, and hospitals.
  7. **Public information and education:** Injury prevention outreach and education on appropriate EMS activation.
  8. **Medical direction:** Physician medical director oversight, patient care supervision, and delegation of medical practice.
  9. **Trauma systems:** Specialized trauma networks encompassing trauma centers, rehabilitation, and triage/transport protocols.
  10. **Evaluation:** Quality improvement (QI) programs for continuous system evaluation and upgrades.
* **Six System Design Goals (Gunderson, 2015):** Clinical quality, service quality, economic efficiency, accountability, improvement, and resilience.
* **Modern EMS Service Lines (Gunderson, 2015):** Prevention and public education; triage; medical first response; ambulance response and transport; medical transportation; event coverage; disaster services; critical care transport; air medical transport; hazmat medical support; tactical medical support; and community paramedicine.

#### C. Access to the EMS System (911 vs. Non-911)
* **911 vs. 10-Digit Non-911 Numbers:** 911 provides universal, immediate access to police, fire, and EMS without requiring callers to look up local 10-digit numbers, enabling rapid simultaneous multi-agency dispatch.
* **Enhanced 911 (E-911) & PSAPs:**
  * **Public Service Answering Point (PSAP):** Receives, verifies, and records calls, selects appropriate agencies, and dispatches responders.
  * **E-911 Features:** Automatically displays **Automatic Number Identification (ANI)** and **Automatic Location Identification (ALI)** on landlines—crucial if a caller hangs up, is a young child, or is incapacitated (e.g., stroke or confusion).
  * **Emergency Medical Dispatchers (EMDs):** Trained dispatchers who provide pre-arrival care instructions (such as bleeding control or CPR) while units respond.
* **Wireless & VoIP Considerations:**
  * **Cellular Calls (~70% of 911 volume, rising):** Lack a fixed street address, may route to a boundary PSAP, and initially connect via cell towers.
  * **FCC Wireless Tiers:**
    * *Basic 911:* Transmits all 911 calls regardless of carrier subscription.
    * *Phase I E-911:* Provides callback number and cell tower/base station location.
    * *Phase II E-911:* Provides caller latitude/longitude within **50 to 300 meters** (many systems incorporate GPS tracking).
  * **VoIP:** FCC requires VoIP providers to supply 911 as a standard feature and transmit the subscriber's callback number and registered physical address to the PSAP.

#### D. EMS Service Delivery Models
* **Fire Department EMS:** Integrated into fire suppression services (paid, part-paid, or volunteer); may provide transport or non-transport first response, primarily focused on 911 emergencies.
* **Municipal EMS ("Third Service"):** Distinct from fire and police, operated directly by local government (e.g., public safety or public health department); primarily 911 response with occasional inter-facility/non-emergency transport.
* **Private EMS:** Operated by private companies under municipal contracts/regulations; provides 911 response, inter-facility transfers, and non-emergency transport.
* **Hospital-Based EMS:** Operated by for-profit or not-for-profit hospitals; functions similarly to private EMS across 911 and inter-facility transport.
* **Law Enforcement EMS:** Rare model seen in smaller, lower-volume jurisdictions leveraging existing patrol readiness.
* **Public Safety Officer:** Personnel cross-trained to perform fire, police, and EMS duties within a single unified agency.

#### E. Levels of Training (*National EMS Scope of Practice Model*)
Established to promote national consistency, patient safety, state reciprocity, and public clarity across four certification tiers:

| Provider Level | Core Scope of Practice & Capabilities |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Emergency Medical Responder (EMR)** | Immediate lifesaving care prior to ambulance arrival: basic airway/ventilation/oxygen devices, vital signs, spinal/extremity stabilization, eye irrigation, bleeding control, emergency moves, CPR, AED, and emergency childbirth. |
| **Emergency Medical Technician (EMT)** | Basic emergency care and ambulance transport: builds on EMR skills with advanced oxygen/ventilation equipment, pulse oximetry, automated BP monitors, and limited medication administration/assistance. |
| **Advanced EMT (AEMT)** | Basic plus limited advanced care and transport: adds advanced airway devices, blood glucose monitoring, IV and intraosseous (IO) fluid therapy, and select medications. |
| **Paramedic** | Highest prehospital tier: builds on EMT/AEMT care with advanced assessments, field impressions, invasive procedures, and expanded pharmacology to reduce morbidity and mortality. |

#### F. Health Care System & Specialty Referral Centers
* **Prehospital vs. Out-of-Hospital Care:** *Prehospital care* refers to emergency care prior to hospital arrival; *out-of-hospital care* more broadly includes scene care (with or without transport) and inter-facility transfers.
* **Specialty Referral Facilities:** Beyond standard hospital emergency departments, EMTs must recognize local destination protocols for specialized centers:
  * **Trauma center** (rapid surgical intervention exceeding standard ED capability)
  * **Burn center** (acute specialized burn management and long-term rehabilitation)
  * **Obstetrical center** (high-risk obstetric care)
  * **Pediatric center** (specialized infant and child care)
  * **Poison center** (specialized toxicology/poisoning care)
  * **Stroke center** (time-critical acute stroke interventions)
  * **Cardiac center** (rapid, advanced cardiac emergency management)
  * **Hyperbaric center** (diving emergencies, carbon monoxide/toxic exposures)
  * **Spine injury center** (severe spinal cord/column trauma)
  * **Psychiatric center** (acute behavioral emergencies)
* **Interagency Coordination:** EMTs operate as part of a team alongside partners, fire, rescue, and law enforcement, serving as the medical liaison on multi-agency scenes.

### 1.3: The EMT — Roles, Responsibilities, and Professional Attributes

#### A. Core Roles & Responsibilities
* **Primary Domains:** Personal safety and safety of others; patient assessment and emergency care; safe lifting and moving; patient transport and transfer of care; record keeping and data collection; and patient advocacy.
* **Specific Duties:**
  * Maintain vehicle and equipment readiness
  * Ensure safety of the EMS crew, patient, and bystanders
  * Operate the emergency vehicle safely
  * Assess the patient and provide emergency care
  * Safely lift, move, transport, and transfer patient care
  * Prepare oral and written reports; perform record keeping and data collection
  * Serve as the patient’s advocate and provide emotional support to patients, relatives, and bystanders
  * Integrate EMS with other emergency/nonemergency services to resolve incidents
  * Maintain medical/legal standards, provide administrative support, enhance professional development, and maintain community relations
* **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) of 1990:** Prohibits employment discrimination against individuals with documented disabilities and requires employers to provide reasonable accommodations.

#### B. Personal Safety & Scene Control (Top Priority)
* **Hierarchy of Safety:** Personal safety is the **#1 priority**—an injured EMT cannot help patients and forces other rescuers to divert resources to rescue you. Once scene safety is assured, patient needs take priority.
* **Driving & Vehicle Safety:** Drive defensively with due regard; wear seat belts at all times unless actively performing essential patient care in the patient compartment.
* **Scene & Environmental Hazards:**
  * Avoid high-traffic zones, downed power lines, fuel/chemical spills, fires, and radiation hazards.
  * **Never enter volatile scenes** (riots, active crime scenes, hostage situations) until law enforcement has secured and cleared the area.
  * Exercise heightened caution around intoxicated, behavioral, or emotionally volatile individuals whose behavior may escalate suddenly.
* **Protective Measures & PPE:**
  * Follow police, fire, and utility directives; establish safe treatment zones and redirect traffic.
  * Wear high-visibility reflective gear at night and deploy adequate scene lighting.
  * Use trauma/extrication gear (helmet/hard hat, protective outerwear, eye protection, leather gloves) around glass/metal, and standard **infectious disease PPE** (gloves, eye protection, mask, gown) as indicated.

#### C. Clinical Care, Lifting, Transport & Transfer of Care
* **Assessment & Care:** Gain patient access, triage multi-patient scenes, perform a **primary assessment** first to resolve immediate life threats (airway, breathing, cardiac arrest, severe hemorrhage), and follow with a **secondary assessment** and continuous reassessment.
* **Safe Lifting & Moving:** Use proper body mechanics, sufficient personnel, and appropriate emergency, urgent, or nonurgent moves/devices to protect both patient and crew.
* **Transport & Pre-Arrival Notification:**
  * Select the appropriate destination facility per local protocol/medical direction.
  * Give early pre-arrival notification to the receiving facility (patient count, condition/injuries, and specialty team needs such as trauma or stroke alerts), and consult on-line medical direction en route if condition changes.
  * Use lights and sirens judiciously—only when clinically warranted—while obeying emergency vehicle laws.
* **Transfer of Care:** Assist moving the wheeled stretcher into the ED, deliver both a **verbal and written report** (injuries/findings, care rendered, patient response), and never depart until care is formally transferred to receiving medical personnel.

#### D. Documentation & Patient Advocacy
* **Record Keeping:** Maintain shift call logs and complete the written or electronic **Prehospital Care Report (PCR)** prior to leaving the hospital or as soon as possible, as it becomes part of the permanent hospital and EMS medical record.
* **Patient Advocacy:**
  * Safeguard and document personal valuables transported with the patient.
  * Protect patient privacy, shield patients (and deceased bodies) from onlookers, and uphold confidentiality.
  * Ensure family/loved ones know how to reach the receiving facility, honor reasonable patient requests (e.g., securing a home or notifying family), and relay vital scene context to hospital staff that they did not witness.

#### E. Professional Attributes, Physical Demands & Credential Maintenance
* **Professional Behavior:** Integrity, empathy, self-motivation, professional appearance and hygiene, self-confidence, effective time management, strong verbal/written communication, teamwork and diplomacy, respect for patients/coworkers, patient advocacy, careful service delivery, and lifelong learning.
* **Appearance:** Clean uniform and personal grooming prevent cross-contamination and inspire patient trust.
* **Knowledge & Skills:** Mastery of DOT EMT curriculum, equipment operation/maintenance/disinfection, approved medication assistance, service area geography, and emergency traffic statutes, supported by ongoing continuing education.
* **Physical & Sensory Demands:**
  * Ability to lift and carry up to **125 pounds**.
  * Good visual acuity (corrected or uncorrected) and **good color vision** (essential for driving and detecting subtle cyanosis, pallor, or flushing in skin, nail beds, lips, and mucosa).
  * Adequate hearing for radio traffic, scene communication, and auscultating blood pressures and breath/body sounds.
* **Personal Traits:** Calm/reassuring demeanor, leadership ability, sound judgment under stress, high moral character, emotional stability/adaptability (processing stress appropriately after the call), active listening, resourcefulness/improvisation, and interagency cooperativeness.
* **Certification & Licensure:** Each EMT is individually responsible for tracking continuing education, verifying skills, maintaining ethical/legal standing, and submitting renewal fees on time.

### 1.4: EMS System Organization, Medical Oversight, and Safety

#### A. State EMS Agency Role (NASEMSO)
* Every U.S. state and territory designates a **lead EMS agency** responsible for:
  1. Statewide EMS system **planning**
  2. Statewide EMS system **coordination**
  3. Statewide EMS system **regulation**
  4. **Licensing** of local EMS agencies and individual personnel (distinct from national NREMT certification)

#### B. Medical Oversight, Medical Direction, Protocols & Standing Orders
* **EMT as Designated Agent:** An EMT cannot practice independently; all clinical care is rendered as the designated agent of the physician **medical director** under state law.
* **Medical Director Duties:** Holds ultimate clinical and legal accountability for patient care; establishes protocols, oversees education/refreshers, credentials system providers, and directs Quality Improvement.
* **Forms of Medical Oversight:**
  * **Indirect (Off-Line) Medical Oversight:** Routine administrative and clinical governance (majority of the medical director's role), including training, QI audits, and written guidelines.
    * **Protocols:** Comprehensive written guidelines defining the full scope of triage, treatment, transport, and destination decisions.
    * **Standing Orders (Off-Line Medical Direction):** Predetermined written subsets of protocols authorizing EMTs to administer specific treatments *without* contacting a physician first (or when communication is lost).
  * **Direct Medical Oversight:** Real-time physician direction during active patient care:
    * **On-Line Medical Direction:** Immediate guidance via radio, phone, or video telemedicine.
    * **On-Scene Medical Direction:** Guidance from a medical director physically present on scene.
* **Chest Pain Protocol Breakdown:**
  * *Off-Line / Standing Orders:* (1) Oxygen via NRB at 15 L/min; (2) position of comfort; (3) 1 patient-prescribed nitroglycerin if SBP > 90 mmHg; (4) 160–325 mg aspirin; (5) request ALS.
  * *On-Line Medical Direction:* (6) Contacting medical control by radio/phone to request authorization for additional nitroglycerin doses if pain persists.

#### C. Quality Improvement (QI / CQI)
* **Purpose:** Continuous internal and external reviews/audits designed to identify and fix *system-level* weaknesses and elevate patient care—not to punish individual providers.
* **Six EMT Roles in QI:**
  1. **Documentation:** Thorough PCRs reveal system trends (e.g., response-time bottlenecks or low-frequency skills needing retraining).
  2. **Reviews and Audits:** Participating in QI committees or peer case reviews.
  3. **Feedback:** Gathering formal patient/hospital surveys and informal post-call feedback from ED physicians and nurses.
  4. **Continuing Education:** Attending refreshers and training updates.
  5. **Preventive Maintenance:** Inspecting and maintaining ambulances and medical gear.
  6. **Regular Skill Maintenance:** Practicing clinical skills continuously toward mastery.

#### D. Patient Safety & Error Prevention
* **High-Risk Prehospital Activities:**
  * Patient hand-offs/transfers of care (scene-to-ambulance or EMS-to-ED)
  * Communication breakdowns causing clinical misunderstandings
  * Lifting, carrying, and moving patients (drop risk)
  * Ambulance collisions during transport
  * Omitting or improperly applying spinal motion restriction/immobilization
* **Root Causes of Errors:** Skill execution failure, rule/protocol violation, or knowledge gaps.
* **Error-Prevention Strategies:** Use clear protocols, ensure adequate scene lighting, minimize assessment interruptions, clearly label/separate medications, reflect on actions, question assumptions, use decision aids, ask for help early, and practice **closed-loop communication** (receiver repeats the order back; sender confirms "yes").

### 1.5: EMS Research

#### A. Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM) & Decision-Making
* **Shift from Tradition to Science:** Modern EMS replaces anecdotal habit with **evidence-based medicine (EBM)**—proving via rigorous research that specific interventions improve patient survival and recovery.
* **Four Steps of Evidence-Based Decision Making:**
  1. Formulate a specific clinical question from field practice.
  2. Search peer-reviewed medical literature for relevant research data.
  3. Appraise the evidence for scientific validity and reliability.
  4. Update protocols and implement validated changes into prehospital care.
* **Prehospital Research Challenges:** Time-critical emergencies, informed consent limitations (or exception-from-informed-consent rules), uncontrolled scene environments, and linking EMS records to hospital outcome data.

#### B. National Research Infrastructure & Data Initiatives
* **2001 *National EMS Research Agenda*:** Commissioned by NHTSA and MCHB alongside federal partners (AHRQ, CDC, HRSA, National Fire Academy, DoD, NIH) to elevate prehospital science.
* **2014 FICEMS Strategic Goal:** Prioritized data-driven, evidence-based EMS systems.
* **NEMSIS (National EMS Information System):** NHTSA-supported national database standardizing prehospital electronic data collection.
* **National Institute of General Medical Sciences (NIGMS):** Coordinates EMS research funding and interagency collaboration.
* **CARES (Cardiac Arrest Registry to Enhance Survival):** Largest U.S. out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) registry; links 911, police, fire, EMS, and hospital records to benchmark and improve cardiac arrest survival.
* ***Safety in Numbers: EMS Data Is Important* (NHTSA):** Uses EMS data to analyze motor vehicle crash injury patterns and improve crash prevention and trauma care.

#### C. The Scientific Method & EMT Responsibility
* **Steps of the Scientific Method:** Pose a question → conduct literature review → formulate hypothesis → run study → collect/analyze data → support or reject hypothesis → identify study limitations → publish peer-reviewed findings.
* **Four Uses of Scientific Evidence in EMS:** Ensure safest/most effective patient care; demonstrate EMS value via outcomes; improve responder safety in uncontrolled scenes; and reinforce thorough PCR documentation.
* **EMT Role:** Participate actively in data collection and approved clinical studies to advance the profession.

### 1.6: Evidence-Based Guidelines (EBGs)

#### A. Concept & Strength of Evidence
* **Definition:** Systematically developed clinical practice guidelines derived from strong, valid peer-reviewed research to standardize field care and reduce unwarranted treatment variation.
* **Recommendation Grading:** Guidelines reflect the quality of available studies, resulting in strong recommendations where evidence is robust and weaker recommendations where evidence is limited.

#### B. National Prehospital EBG Model Process (FICEMS & NEMSAC)
* **8-Step Cyclical Process:**
  1. **System inputs** (existing protocols and AHA guidelines)
  2. **Guideline initiation:** EMS evidence accumulation and evaluation
  3. **Establish priorities** for guideline development
  4. **Guideline development**
  5. **EMS protocol development**
  6. **Dissemination** of guidelines and protocols
  7. **Implementation** in field practice
  8. **Evaluation** of effectiveness, patient outcomes, clinical research, and QI *(loops directly back to Step 2)*
* **Key National Initiatives:**
  * *2015 NAEMSP Report:* Established 7 objectives and 7 action items for sustainable EBG development, implementation, and evaluation.
  * *NASEMSO Pediatric Pain Management Project:* NHTSA/HRSA (EMSC)-funded initiative refining the National EBG Model Process for pediatric analgesia.

### 1.7: Mobile Integrated Healthcare and Community Paramedicine (MIH-CP)

* **Mobile Integrated Healthcare (MIH):** Patient-centered out-of-hospital health care delivered using mobile technology and resources by various health care practitioners administratively or clinically integrated with the EMS system.
* **Community Paramedicine (CP):** Out-of-hospital patient-centered care delivered directly by an EMS agency and its EMS providers integrated with broader health care entities.
* **NAEMT Vision Services:**
  * Expanding care access in underserved communities.
  * Providing telephone triage/advice to appropriate 911 callers instead of automatic ambulance dispatch.
  * Deploying specially trained EMS clinicians to manage high-utilizer/high-readmission patients, support chronic disease management, and conduct preventive or post-discharge follow-up visits.
* **Strategic Value:** Shifts EMS from an exclusively emergency-response/transport model toward proactive community health integration.

## Part II: Public Health and EMS

### 1.8: Introduction to Public Health & EMS
* **Population Focus:** While acute clinical care treats one ill or injured individual at a time, public health protects entire populations (defined by age, condition, neighborhood, city, state, or nation).
* **Frontline EMT Role:** EMTs serve as public health eyes and ears—identifying community hazards (e.g., lack of child safety seats), reporting trends, and educating patients and families.
* **American Public Health Association (APHA):**
  * *Vision:* “Healthy people in healthy communities.”
  * *Mission:* “To promote physical and mental health and prevent disease, injury, and disability.”

---

### 1.9: Public Health Mission, Goals, and Core Functions

#### A. Foundational Definitions & Missions
* **C.E.A. Winslow (1920):** Defined public health as *"the science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life, and promoting health and efficiency through organized community effort..."* (spanning environmental sanitation, communicable disease control, personal hygiene education, early diagnosis/preventive treatment, and social machinery ensuring an adequate standard of living).
* **Institute of Medicine (IOM, 1988 — *The Future of Public Health*):** *"Fulfilling society’s interests in assuring conditions in which people can be healthy."*
* **U.S. Public Health Service (1994 — *Public Health in America*):** Focused on preventing epidemics, protecting against environmental hazards, preventing injuries, promoting healthy behaviors, disaster response/recovery, and ensuring health service quality and accessibility.

#### B. Core Functions, Arms & Essential Services
* **Five Arms of Public Health:** Epidemiology; biostatistics; health policy and management/administration; behavioral/social science and health education; and environmental health.
* **Three Core Functions & 10 Essential Public Health Services (CDC):**
  1. **Assessment** *(led by epidemiology & biostatistics)*:
     * Monitor health status to identify community health problems.
     * Diagnose and investigate community health problems and hazards.
     * Inform, educate, and empower people about health issues.
     * Research for new insights and innovative solutions.
  2. **Policy Development** *(all public health & government arms)*:
     * Mobilize community partnerships to identify and solve health problems.
     * Develop policies and plans supporting individual and community health efforts.
  3. **Assurance** *(led by health policy & administration)*:
     * Enforce laws and regulations protecting health and safety.
     * Link people to needed personal health services and ensure care access.
     * Ensure a competent public health and personal health care workforce.
     * Evaluate effectiveness, accessibility, and quality of personal and population-based services.

---

### 1.10: Public Health Versus Individual Patient Care

| Dimension | Public Health Approach | Individual Medical Care Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Focus** | Population health | Individual patient health |
| **Timing of Intervention** | **Before** onset/diagnosis (primary prevention) | **After** onset of symptoms or injury (acute care) |
| **View of Disease/Disability** | Consequence of multiple factors (behavioral, biological, social, economic, cultural, environmental, psychological) | Problem within the body or mind of the individual |
| **Primary Emphasis** | Prevention via health education and health promotion | Diagnosis and clinical treatment of the patient |
| **Workforce & Settings** | Multidisciplinary professionals working across diverse community settings | Physicians and clinicians working primarily in health care facilities/units |

* **Convergence:** Modern medicine increasingly integrates preventive care, while public health relies heavily on clinical medicine during epidemics and chronic disease crises (e.g., COVID-19, influenza, heart disease, cancer, obesity).

---

### 1.11: Achievements of Public Health

#### A. CDC's 10 Greatest Public Health Achievements
1. **Vaccination** (eradication of smallpox/polio; control of measles, rubella, tetanus, diphtheria, varicella, HPV)
2. **Motor vehicle safety** (seat belt, child restraint, and impaired-driving laws; vehicle engineering)
3. **Safer workplaces** (occupational safety standards, protective equipment, ventilation, hazard controls)
4. **Control of infectious diseases** (clean water, sanitation, antimicrobials, scientific advances)
5. **Decline in deaths from coronary heart disease and stroke** (risk-factor education and stronger EMS/emergency care)
6. **Safer and healthier foods** (refrigeration, pasteurization, sanitation, reduced microbial contamination)
7. **Healthier mothers and babies** (prenatal care, hygiene, nutrition, immunizations)
8. **Family planning** (contraception and reproductive health options)
9. **Fluoridation of drinking water** (major reduction in dental caries and tooth loss)
10. **Recognition of tobacco use as a health hazard** (public education and smoke-free legislation)

#### B. U.S. Life Expectancy & Mortality Shifts
* **1900:** Life expectancy at birth was **47.3 years**; top 3 causes of death were infectious diseases (**pneumonia/influenza, tuberculosis, and diarrheal diseases**).
* **2018 (Pre-COVID-19):** Life expectancy rose by >30 years to **78.4 years**; top 3 causes of death shifted to **heart disease, cancer, and unintentional injuries**.
* **2020 & 2022 (COVID-19 Era):** Life expectancy dipped to **77.0 years** in 2020; by 2022, the top 3 causes of death were **heart disease, cancer, and COVID-19**.

### 1.12: Governmental and Nongovernmental Roles and Resources
* **Constitutional Authority:** Because health is not explicitly delegated to the federal government in the U.S. Constitution, primary legal responsibility for public health rests with **state and local governments**, supported by federal agencies (e.g., CDC, NHTSA, HRSA) and nongovernmental organizations.
* **Core State & Local Mechanisms:** Disease/injury surveillance, health screenings and testing, health education/promotion, ensuring health care access/quality, vaccination programs, and enforcing environmental sanitation standards.

---

### 1.13: EMS Roles in Public Health

#### A. Intersectoral Collaboration & Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)
* **Intersectoral System:** EMS operates within a collaborative local public health network alongside hospitals, health departments, schools, and community groups (e.g., public CPR training, blood pressure screenings, ambulance open houses).
* **Social Determinants of Health (SDOH):** Non-medical environmental conditions shaping health outcomes across **five domains**:
  1. Economic stability
  2. Education access and quality
  3. Health care access and quality
  4. Neighborhood and built environment
  5. Social and community context
* **EHR Surveillance & Referrals:** EMS electronic health records (EHRs) capture frontline community injury/illness trends for public health policy, while EMTs connect underserved or vulnerable patients (including victims of human trafficking, child abuse, elder abuse, or untreated mental illness) to social and primary care services.

#### B. EMS Prevention Levels (Primary, Secondary, Tertiary)
* **Primary Prevention (Preventing Occurrence Before Onset):**
  * Public safety campaigns (DUI prevention, fall prevention, fire safety, seat belts, car seats, helmets).
  * Community health education and administering vaccinations/immunizations (e.g., influenza and COVID-19 vaccination/testing initiatives).
* **Secondary Prevention (Early Detection & Limiting Progression):**
  * Community health screenings (e.g., blood pressure checks) and reporting unsafe home environments or abuse/neglect.
  * Supporting public-access AED placement and participating in local/state pandemic and disaster surveillance/mitigation plans.
* **Tertiary Prevention (Acute Treatment & Limiting Disability):**
  * Delivering immediate emergency medical care, stabilization, and transport to minimize long-term morbidity and disability.
