## 3. READ (Core Concepts & Connections)

### Part I: EMS Systems, Roles & Science
* **Evolution & Governance:**
  * Early funeral-home ambulances only provided transport. Wartime trauma care (Korea, Vietnam, Iraq, Afghanistan) proved that stabilizing patients *before* transport saves lives.
  * **1966** was the turning point: the NAS/NRC *"White Paper"* exposed poor prehospital care, and the **Highway Safety Act** placed DOT/NHTSA in charge of EMS curriculum and standards, followed by the **1973 EMS System Act** (funding) and **2006 National EMS Scope of Practice Model**.
  * **States** hold primary regulatory power, guided by NHTSA's **10 Technical Assistance Program Assessment Standards** and a lead state EMS agency (planning, coordination, regulation, licensing).
* **911 Access & Models:**
  * Calls route to a **PSAP**. Landline **E-911** automatically displays phone number (**ANI**) and address (**ALI**); **EMDs** give pre-arrival CPR/bleeding instructions.
  * Wireless calls (~70% of volume) use **Phase I** (cell tower) or **Phase II** (GPS/triangulation within **50–300 meters**).
  * Delivery models include **Fire-based**, **Municipal ("Third Service")**, **Private**, **Hospital-based**, rare **Law Enforcement**, and cross-trained **Public Safety Officers**.
* **The 4 Provider Tiers:**
  * **EMR:** First-on-scene basic care (airway/O2, bleeding control, splinting, vitals, CPR/AED, emergency childbirth).
  * **EMT:** Basic care + transport (advanced O2/ventilation adjuncts, pulse oximetry, automated BP, limited meds).
  * **AEMT:** EMT + advanced airways, blood glucose checks, IV/IO access, select meds.
  * **Paramedic:** Highest prehospital tier (advanced assessments, field impressions, invasive procedures, broad pharmacology).
* **EMT Priorities & Medical Oversight:**
  * **Safety Hierarchy:** Self $\rightarrow$ Crew $\rightarrow$ Patient $\rightarrow$ Bystanders. Never enter violent/crime scenes until law enforcement clears them. Wear seat belts unless actively treating in the patient compartment.
  * **Physical/Sensory:** Lift/carry up to **125 lbs**; good visual acuity and **color vision** (to spot cyanosis, pallor, or flushing). Under the **ADA (1990)**, employers cannot discriminate against qualified individuals with documented disabilities and must provide reasonable accommodations.
  * **Medical Direction:** EMTs practice strictly as the designated agent of the physician **Medical Director**.
    * *Indirect (Off-Line):* Written **Protocols** and **Standing Orders** (preauthorized treatments done *before* calling a doctor, e.g., giving aspirin or 1 prescribed nitroglycerin for chest pain).
    * *Direct:* Real-time orders via radio/phone (**On-Line**) or physically on scene (**On-Scene**).
* **QI, Research & MIH-CP:**
  * **CQI** fixes *system* flaws through thorough PCRs, audits, hospital/patient feedback, continuing education, preventive gear maintenance, and skill practice.
  * **Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM)** replaces habit with science using databases like **NEMSIS** (national EMS data) and **CARES** (cardiac arrest survival), feeding into the **8-step cyclical EBG model**.
  * **MIH-CP** expands EMS beyond 911 transport into community health, chronic disease follow-ups, and telephone triage for underserved or high-utilizer patients.

### Part II: Public Health & EMS
* **Public Health vs. Clinical Medicine:** Clinical medicine treats *one sick/injured person after onset*; public health protects *entire populations before onset* (APHA vision: *"Healthy people in healthy communities"*).
* **CDC Framework & Impact:**
  * Driven by **5 arms** (epidemiology, biostatistics, policy/management, behavioral/social science, environmental health) and **3 core functions** (**Assessment**, **Policy Development**, **Assurance**).
  * Public health measures (vaccinations, motor vehicle safety, sanitation, safer workplaces/foods, heart disease/stroke reduction) pushed U.S. life expectancy from **47.3 years (1900)** to **78.4 years (2018)**, shifting top killers from infectious diseases to chronic conditions and injuries.
* **EMS on the Frontlines of Public Health:**
  * EMTs monitor **Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)** (economic stability, education, healthcare access, neighborhood/built environment, social/community context) and log surveillance data in EHRs.
  * **3 Prevention Tiers:**
    * *Primary (Prevent before it happens):* Seatbelt/helmet/DUI campaigns, flu/COVID-19 vaccinations.
    * *Secondary (Detect early / limit spread):* Blood pressure checks, reporting child/elder abuse or unsafe homes, public-access AEDs.
    * *Tertiary (Treat acute event / limit disability):* Emergency care and transport.

## 4. REFLECT (Connect & Apply)
Pause and test your understanding by connecting the concepts to real-world scenarios:
* *Scenario 1 (Medical Direction):* You arrive on scene of a patient with chest pain. Your written protocol lets you administer oxygen and aspirin immediately, and gives you standing orders for one dose of the patient's nitroglycerin if systolic BP > 90 mmHg. Before giving a *second* dose, you must radio the ED physician. **Which part is off-line medical direction, and which is on-line?**
  * *Connection:* Oxygen, aspirin, and the 1st nitro are **off-line (standing orders)**; calling for the 2nd nitro is **on-line (direct) medical direction**.
* *Scenario 2 (Public Health Prevention):* On a non-urgent lift assist for an elderly patient, you notice throw rugs everywhere, expired medications, and no grab bars in the bathroom. How does your role bridge clinical care and public health?
  * *Connection:* Helping them up safely is acute care, while recognizing fall hazards (**SDOH: neighborhood/built environment**) and connecting them with community fall-prevention resources is **primary/secondary public health prevention**.
* *Scenario 3 (QI & Safety):* During a busy shift, an EMT forgets to give a verbal report at handoff, leading to a delayed medication in the ED. Why does CQI look at handoff protocols and **closed-loop communication** instead of simply firing the EMT?
  * *Connection:* CQI targets *system-level vulnerabilities* (handoffs are a top prehospital error risk) to prevent future mistakes across the entire agency.

## 6. REVIEW (Rapid Consolidation)
Use this quick-hit checklist before your quiz or exam:
* **Safety First:** Personal safety $\rightarrow$ Crew $\rightarrow$ Patient $\rightarrow$ Bystanders. Stage for police on violent scenes.
* **Chain of Authority:** State Lead Agency licenses you $\rightarrow$ NREMT certifies you $\rightarrow$ Physician Medical Director authorizes you to practice.
* **Documentation & Handoff:** Always give *both* a verbal and written report (PCR); never leave until care is formally transferred.
* **Data Saves Lives:** **NEMSIS** = general EMS data; **CARES** = cardiac arrest data; **EBM/EBGs** = science-backed protocols.
* **Prevention Pyramid:** **Primary** = stop it before it starts (vaccines, seatbelts); **Secondary** = catch it early (BP checks, reporting abuse); **Tertiary** = treat the emergency (911 response).
