# Chapter 1: EMS Systems, Research, and Public Health

## 1. Foundations & Historical Evolution of EMS
- **Core Purpose**
  - Bridges sudden illness/injury onset with hospital stabilization, rehab, or discharge (Health Care Continuum)
  - Blends immediate field clinical care with system design, science, and public health
- **Historical Roots**
  - Early ambulances operated out of funeral homes (transport-only, no field care)
  - Military trauma lessons (Korea/Vietnam = field stabilization; Iraq/Afghanistan = resuscitation & hemorrhage control)
- **Key Legislative & Policy Timeline**
  - **1960s–1970s**: CPR developed; AHA begins public BLS training (now EMT prerequisite)
  - **1966**: *Accidental Death and Disability* ("White Paper") exposes prehospital care gaps
  - **1966**: Highway Safety Act puts DOT & NHTSA in charge of EMS standards and curriculum
  - **1973**: Emergency Medical Services System Act funds regional EMS planning and staffing
  - **1993**: NREMT Education and Practice Blueprint standardizes training frameworks
  - **1996 & 2000**: NHTSA *EMS Agendas for the Future* push healthcare integration and national education/licensure consistency
  - **2005–2006**: *National EMS Core Content*, *Scope of Practice Model* (4 levels), and IOM *EMS at the Crossroads* (reciprocity, paramedic accreditation, national certification)
  - **2014–2018**: *EMS Agenda 2050* establishes a 30-year people-centered vision

## 2. EMS System Architecture, Access & Delivery
- **State Governance & Benchmarks**
  - States independently regulate EMS via a **Lead State EMS Agency** (planning, coordination, regulation, licensing agencies/personnel)
  - **NHTSA 10 Technical Assistance Standards**: Regulation/policy, resource management, human resources/training, transportation, facilities, communications, public info/education, medical direction, trauma systems, evaluation (QI)
  - **6 System Design Goals**: Clinical quality, service quality, economic efficiency, accountability, improvement, resilience
- **Accessing EMS (911 Systems)**
  - **PSAP (Public Service Answering Point)**: Receives calls, selects agencies, dispatches units
  - **E-911 (Landlines)**: Displays ANI (phone number) and ALI (physical address) automatically
  - **EMDs (Emergency Medical Dispatchers)**: Give pre-arrival instructions (CPR, bleeding control)
  - **Wireless (~70% of calls) & VoIP**:
    - *Basic 911*: Transmits call regardless of carrier plan
    - *Phase I E-911*: Callback number + cell tower location
    - *Phase II E-911*: Latitude/longitude within 50–300 meters
    - *VoIP*: Transmits callback number + registered physical address
- **Service Delivery Models**
  - **Fire Department EMS**: Integrated with fire suppression (transport or non-transport first response)
  - **Municipal EMS ("Third Service")**: Standalone local government agency separate from fire/police
  - **Private EMS**: Private companies under municipal contract (911 + inter-facility transfers)
  - **Hospital-Based EMS**: Operated by for-profit or non-profit hospitals
  - **Law Enforcement EMS**: Rare; patrol officers cross-trained in smaller jurisdictions
  - **Public Safety Officer**: Single personnel cross-trained in fire, police, and EMS
- **Provider Levels (*National EMS Scope of Practice Model*)**
  - **EMR (Emergency Medical Responder)**: Immediate lifesaving care before ambulance arrives (basic airway/O2, vitals, bleeding control, splinting, CPR/AED, emergency childbirth)
  - **EMT (Emergency Medical Technician)**: Basic care + transport (advanced O2/ventilation devices, pulse ox, auto BP, limited meds)
  - **AEMT (Advanced EMT)**: EMT skills + advanced airways, blood glucose monitoring, IV/IO fluids, select meds
  - **Paramedic**: Highest prehospital care (advanced assessments, field impressions, invasive procedures, broad pharmacology)
- **Care Settings & Specialty Centers**
  - **Prehospital Care** (before ED arrival) vs. **Out-of-Hospital Care** (scene care with/without transport + inter-facility transfers)
  - **Specialty Referral Centers**: Trauma, Burn, Obstetrical, Pediatric, Poison, Stroke, Cardiac, Hyperbaric (CO/diving), Spine Injury, Psychiatric
  - **Modern Expansion (MIH-CP)**:
    - *Mobile Integrated Healthcare (MIH)*: Out-of-hospital patient-centered care using mobile tech integrated with EMS
    - *Community Paramedicine (CP)*: EMS agency clinicians providing preventive, chronic-disease, post-discharge, and telephone-triage care to underserved/high-utilizer patients

## 3. The EMT: Roles, Safety & Clinical Oversight
- **Safety Priority #1 (Self → Crew → Patient → Bystanders)**
  - **Vehicle Safety**: Drive with due regard; wear seat belts unless actively treating in back
  - **Scene Hazards**: Avoid traffic, downed wires, hazmat/fire/radiation; **stage until law enforcement clears violent/crime scenes**
  - **PPE**: High-vis reflective gear at night; trauma/extrication gear (helmet, eye protection, leather gloves); infectious PPE (gloves, mask, eye protection, gown)
- **Core Clinical & Operational Duties**
  - **Assessment & Care**: Triage → Primary assessment (fix immediate life threats first) → Secondary assessment & reassessment
  - **Lifting & Moving**: Proper body mechanics (lift up to **125 lbs**), sufficient crew, appropriate move/device
  - **Transport & Handoff**: Select appropriate facility; give early pre-arrival notification; deliver **both verbal and written reports**; never leave until care is formally transferred
  - **Documentation & Advocacy**: Complete PCR promptly; safeguard patient valuables; protect privacy; relay scene context to hospital staff
- **Professional & Physical Standards**
  - **Traits**: Integrity, empathy, calm demeanor, active listening, teamwork, lifelong learning, personal CE/licensure tracking
  - **Sensory**: Good visual acuity, **good color vision** (detecting cyanosis/pallor/flushing), hearing for radios and auscultation
  - **ADA (1990)**: Protects individuals with documented disabilities from job discrimination; requires reasonable accommodations
- **Medical Direction & Oversight**
  - EMT practices strictly as the **designated agent of the physician Medical Director**
  - **Indirect (Off-Line) Oversight**: Routine governance (training, QI audits, written guidelines)
    - *Protocols*: Comprehensive written rules for triage, treatment, transport, destination
    - *Standing Orders (Off-Line Direction)*: Preauthorized treatments EMTs give *before* calling a doctor (e.g., O2, comfort position, 1 prescribed nitro if SBP > 90, aspirin 160–325 mg, request ALS for chest pain)
  - **Direct Oversight**: Real-time physician guidance during care
    - *On-Line Medical Direction*: Via radio, phone, or telemedicine (e.g., requesting authorization for repeat nitro)
    - *On-Scene Medical Direction*: Medical director physically present on scene

## 4. Quality Improvement (QI), Patient Safety & Research
- **Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI)**
  - Audits system-level processes to elevate care (non-punitive)
  - **6 EMT Roles in QI**: Thorough PCR documentation, peer reviews/audits, gathering patient/ED feedback, continuing education, preventive equipment maintenance, regular skill practice
- **Error Prevention & Patient Safety**
  - **High-Risk Areas**: Care hand-offs, miscommunication, lifting/dropping patients, ambulance crashes, improper spinal restriction
  - **Root Causes**: Skill execution failure, protocol violation, knowledge gap
  - **Prevention Tools**: Clear protocols, good lighting, minimal interruptions, clear med labeling, decision aids, **closed-loop communication** (repeat back + confirm)
- **EMS Research & Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM)**
  - **EBM**: Replaces tradition with clinical decisions proven by scientific research
  - **4 Steps of EBM Decision-Making**: Formulate question → Search literature → Appraise validity → Implement protocol changes
  - **Scientific Method**: Question → Literature review → Hypothesis → Study → Data analysis → Support/reject → Identify limitations → Peer-reviewed publication
  - **National Data Infrastructure**:
    - *NEMSIS*: Standardizes national electronic prehospital data
    - *CARES*: Largest U.S. out-of-hospital cardiac arrest registry linking 911, EMS, and hospital survival data
    - *NIGMS*: Coordinates EMS research funding; *NHTSA "Safety in Numbers"*: Analyzes MVC injury patterns
- **Evidence-Based Guidelines (EBGs)**
  - Standardized clinical guidelines graded by strength of underlying scientific evidence
  - **8-Step National EBG Cyclical Process**: (1) System inputs → (2) Evidence accumulation → (3) Set priorities → (4) Develop guidelines → (5) Develop EMS protocols → (6) Disseminate → (7) Implement → (8) Evaluate outcomes/QI *(loops back to Step 2)*

## 5. Public Health & EMS Integration
- **Public Health vs. Individual Care**
  - **Public Health**: Focuses on **populations**; intervenes **before** onset (prevention); views disease as multi-factorial (social, economic, environmental, behavioral); APHA mission = *"Healthy people in healthy communities"*
  - **Individual Medicine**: Focuses on **one patient**; intervenes **after** onset (diagnosis/treatment)
- **Core Public Health Framework (CDC)**
  - **5 Arms**: Epidemiology, biostatistics, health policy/management, behavioral/social science, environmental health
  - **3 Core Functions & 10 Essential Services**:
    1. *Assessment*: Monitor health status, investigate hazards, educate/empower, research solutions
    2. *Policy Development*: Mobilize community partnerships, develop health policies/plans
    3. *Assurance*: Enforce safety laws, link people to care, ensure competent workforce, evaluate service quality
- **Historical Impact & Top 10 CDC Achievements**
  - **Life Expectancy Shift**: 1900 (**47.3 yrs**; top killers = pneumonia/flu, TB, diarrhea) → 2018 (**78.4 yrs**; heart disease, cancer, unintentional injuries) → 2020/2022 (**77.0 yrs**; heart disease, cancer, COVID-19)
  - **CDC 10 Greatest Achievements**: Vaccinations, motor vehicle safety, safer workplaces, infectious disease control, reduced heart disease/stroke deaths, safer foods, healthier mothers/babies, family planning, water fluoridation, tobacco hazard recognition
- **EMS Roles in Public Health**
  - **Legal Authority**: Primary public health power rests with **state and local governments** (supported by CDC, NHTSA, HRSA)
  - **Social Determinants of Health (SDOH — 5 Domains)**: Economic stability, education access/quality, healthcare access/quality, neighborhood/built environment, social/community context
  - **Surveillance & Referrals**: EHRs track injury/illness trends; EMTs connect vulnerable patients (trafficking, abuse, untreated mental illness) to resources
  - **3 Levels of EMS Prevention**:
    - *Primary (Prevent before onset)*: DUI/fall/fire/seatbelt/helmet campaigns, vaccinations (flu/COVID-19)
    - *Secondary (Early detection / limit spread)*: BP screenings, reporting unsafe homes/abuse, public-access AEDs, disaster/pandemic planning
    - *Tertiary (Acute care / limit disability)*: Immediate emergency treatment, stabilization, and transport
