# Chapter 1: EMS Systems, Research, and Public Health
- **1. Key Terms & Foundations**
  - **Key Terms (Grouped by Concept):**
    - *Provider Levels:* Emergency Medical Responder (EMR), Emergency Medical Technician (EMT), Advanced Emergency Medical Technician (AEMT), Paramedic
    - *System & Delivery Models:* EMS system, prehospital care, Mobile Integrated Healthcare (MIH), Community Paramedicine (CP), quality improvement (QI)
    - *Medical Oversight & Direction:* medical oversight, direct medical oversight, indirect medical oversight, medical director, medical direction, off-line medical direction, on-line medical direction, on-scene medical direction, protocols, standing orders
    - *Research & Legal:* evidence-based medicine, evidence-based guidelines (EBG), Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)
  - **Core Purpose:**
    - Bridges sudden illness/injury onset with hospital stabilization, rehabilitation, or discharge (**Health Care Continuum**)
    - Combines immediate out-of-hospital clinical care with system organization, evidence-based guidelines, quality improvement, and population-level public health integration
    - Addresses preventable deaths and permanent disabilities from catastrophic accidents and sudden illnesses
  - **Historical Roots:**
    - Early ambulances operated out of local funeral homes (rapid transport over active field medical treatment)
    - Military trauma lessons: Korean & Vietnam conflicts (immediate field stabilization improves survival); Iraq & Afghanistan conflicts (battlefield resuscitation & hemorrhage control)
  - **Key Legislative, Policy & Educational Timeline:**
    - **1960s–1970s:** CPR developed (1960s); AHA begins public CPR/BLS training in 1970s (now standard EMT prerequisite)
    - **1966:** *Accidental Death and Disability: The Neglected Disease of Modern Society* (NAS/NRC "White Paper") exposes prehospital deficiencies and spurs reforms
    - **1966:** Highway Safety Act requires state highway safety standards and puts DOT & NHTSA in charge of EMS systems and national curriculum
    - **1973:** Emergency Medical Services System Act provides major federal funding for regional EMS planning, staffing, and training
    - **1993:** NREMT Education and Practice Blueprint standardizes training/education frameworks
    - **1996 & 2000:** NHTSA *EMS Agendas for the Future* push health care integration (1996) and national education/certification/licensure consistency (2000)
    - **2005–2006:** NHTSA/HRSA *National EMS Core Content* (2005) & *Scope of Practice Model* (2006, 4 provider levels); IOM *EMS at the Crossroads* (2006: state reciprocity, paramedic program accreditation, national certification for licensure)
    - **2014–2018:** *EMS Agenda 2050* (via NEMSAC) establishes a 30-year people-centered vision for EMS

- **2. EMS System Architecture, Access & Delivery**
  - **State Governance & Benchmarks:**
    - Individual states design and regulate EMS independently via a **Lead State EMS Agency** (statewide planning, coordination, regulation, and licensing of agencies/personnel—distinct from national NREMT certification)
    - **NHTSA 10 Technical Assistance Program Assessment Standards:** (1) Regulation & policy, (2) resource management, (3) human resources & training (at least EMT level), (4) transportation (ground/air), (5) facilities, (6) communications, (7) public information & education, (8) medical direction, (9) trauma systems, (10) evaluation (QI)
    - **Six System Design Goals (Gunderson, 2015):** Clinical quality, service quality, economic efficiency, accountability, improvement, and resilience
    - **12 Modern EMS Service Lines (Gunderson, 2015):** Prevention & public education; triage; medical first response; ambulance response & transport; medical transportation; event coverage; disaster services; critical care transport; air medical transport; hazmat medical support; tactical medical support; community paramedicine
  - **Accessing EMS (911 vs. Non-911):**
    - Universal 911 access avoids looking up local 10-digit numbers and enables simultaneous police/fire/EMS dispatch
    - **PSAP (Public Service Answering Point):** Receives, verifies, and records calls; selects agencies; dispatches responders
    - **E-911 (Landlines):** Automatically displays **ANI** (phone number) and **ALI** (physical address)—vital if caller hangs up, is a child, or is incapacitated
    - **EMDs (Emergency Medical Dispatchers):** Provide pre-arrival care instructions (bleeding control, CPR)
    - **Wireless (~70% of calls, rising) & VoIP:**
      - *Basic 911:* Transmits all 911 calls regardless of carrier subscription
      - *Phase I E-911:* Provides callback number and cell tower/base station location
      - *Phase II E-911:* Provides caller latitude/longitude within **50 to 300 meters** (many systems use GPS)
      - *VoIP:* FCC requires 911 as standard feature transmitting callback number and registered physical address
  - **Six EMS Service Delivery Models:**
    - **Fire Department EMS:** Integrated into fire suppression (paid, part-paid, volunteer; transport or non-transport first response)
    - **Municipal EMS ("Third Service"):** Standalone local government agency separate from fire and police (911 + occasional inter-facility/non-emergency transport)
    - **Private EMS:** Private companies under municipal contract/regulation (911, inter-facility transfers, non-emergency transport)
    - **Hospital-Based EMS:** Operated by for-profit or not-for-profit hospitals (911 + inter-facility transport)
    - **Law Enforcement EMS:** Rare model in smaller, lower-volume jurisdictions leveraging patrol readiness
    - **Public Safety Officer:** Personnel cross-trained in fire, police, and EMS within one unified agency
  - **Four Provider Levels (*National EMS Scope of Practice Model*):**
    - **EMR (Emergency Medical Responder):** Immediate lifesaving care before ambulance arrival (basic airway/ventilation/O2 devices, vital signs, spinal/extremity stabilization, eye irrigation, bleeding control, emergency moves, CPR, AED, emergency childbirth)
    - **EMT (Emergency Medical Technician):** Basic emergency care and ambulance transport (EMR skills + advanced O2/ventilation equipment, pulse oximetry, automated BP monitors, limited medication administration/assistance)
    - **AEMT (Advanced EMT):** Basic + limited advanced care and transport (EMT skills + advanced airway devices, blood glucose monitoring, IV/IO fluid therapy, select medications)
    - **Paramedic:** Highest prehospital tier (EMT/AEMT skills + advanced assessments, field impressions, invasive procedures, expanded pharmacology)
  - **Care Settings & Specialty Referral Centers:**
    - *Prehospital Care* (emergency care before hospital arrival) vs. *Out-of-Hospital Care* (scene care with/without transport + inter-facility transfers)
    - **10 Specialty Referral Centers:** Trauma center, Burn center, Obstetrical center, Pediatric center, Poison center, Stroke center, Cardiac center, Hyperbaric center (diving, CO/toxic exposures), Spine injury center, Psychiatric center
    - Interagency coordination: EMTs serve as the medical liaison alongside fire, rescue, and law enforcement
  - **Mobile Integrated Healthcare & Community Paramedicine (MIH-CP):**
    - **MIH:** Patient-centered out-of-hospital health care using mobile tech/resources by various clinicians integrated with EMS
    - **CP:** Out-of-hospital care delivered directly by an EMS agency and its providers integrated with broader health care entities
    - **NAEMT Vision Services:** Expanding underserved access; telephone triage/advice instead of automatic ambulance dispatch; managing high-utilizer/high-readmission patients, chronic disease management, and preventive/post-discharge visits

- **3. The EMT: Roles, Safety & Clinical Oversight**
  - **Safety Priority #1 (Self → Crew → Patient → Bystanders):**
    - An injured EMT cannot help patients and diverts rescue resources
    - **Vehicle Safety:** Drive defensively with due regard; wear seat belts at all times unless actively performing essential patient care in back
    - **Scene Hazards:** Avoid traffic, downed power lines, fuel/chemical spills, fires, radiation; **never enter volatile scenes** (riots, active crime scenes, hostage situations) until law enforcement clears area; exercise caution with intoxicated/behavioral/volatile patients
    - **Protective Measures & PPE:** High-vis reflective gear at night + adequate lighting; trauma/extrication gear (helmet/hard hat, protective outerwear, eye protection, leather gloves); standard infectious disease PPE (gloves, eye protection, mask, gown)
  - **Core Clinical & Operational Duties:**
    - Maintain vehicle/equipment readiness and operate emergency vehicles safely
    - Triage multi-patient scenes → **Primary assessment** first (resolve airway, breathing, cardiac arrest, severe hemorrhage) → **Secondary assessment** & continuous reassessment
    - Safe lifting/moving (lift/carry up to **125 lbs**) using proper body mechanics, sufficient crew, and emergency/urgent/nonurgent moves
    - Select appropriate destination facility; give early pre-arrival notification (patient count, condition, trauma/stroke alerts); use lights/sirens judiciously
    - **Transfer of Care:** Assist wheeled stretcher into ED, give **both verbal and written reports**, and never depart until care is formally transferred
    - **Documentation & Advocacy:** Complete PCR promptly (permanent medical record); safeguard/document valuables; protect privacy/shield bodies; inform family of destination; honor reasonable requests; relay scene context to ED staff
  - **Professional & Physical Standards:**
    - **Traits:** Integrity, empathy, self-motivation, clean uniform/hygiene, self-confidence, time management, communication, diplomacy, teamwork, calm demeanor, emotional stability, lifelong learning, personal tracking of CE/skills/renewal fees
    - **Sensory:** Good visual acuity (corrected/uncorrected), **good color vision** (driving + spotting cyanosis/pallor/flushing in skin/nail beds/lips/mucosa), adequate hearing (radios, scene, auscultation)
    - **ADA (1990):** Prohibits job discrimination against individuals with documented disabilities; requires reasonable accommodations
  - **Medical Direction & Oversight:**
    - EMT practices strictly as the **designated agent of the physician Medical Director** (cannot practice independently)
    - **Indirect (Off-Line) Medical Oversight:** Routine governance (training, QI audits, written guidelines)
      - *Protocols:* Comprehensive written guidelines for triage, treatment, transport, destination
      - *Standing Orders (Off-Line Medical Direction):* Preauthorized written subsets allowing EMTs to treat *without* calling a physician first (e.g., Chest Pain: O2 via NRB at 15 L/min, position of comfort, 1 prescribed nitroglycerin if SBP > 90 mmHg, 160–325 mg aspirin, request ALS)
    - **Direct Medical Oversight:** Real-time physician guidance during care
      - *On-Line Medical Direction:* Immediate guidance via radio, phone, or telemedicine (e.g., requesting authorization for repeat nitroglycerin)
      - *On-Scene Medical Direction:* Medical director physically present on scene

- **4. Quality Improvement (QI), Patient Safety & Research**
  - **Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI):**
    - Non-punitive internal/external reviews and audits fixing *system-level* weaknesses
    - **Six EMT Roles in QI:** (1) Thorough PCR documentation, (2) peer reviews and audits, (3) gathering patient/ED feedback, (4) continuing education, (5) preventive vehicle/gear maintenance, (6) regular skill practice toward mastery
  - **Patient Safety & Error Prevention:**
    - **5 High-Risk Prehospital Activities:** Care hand-offs, communication breakdowns, lifting/moving/dropping patients, ambulance collisions, omitting/improperly applying spinal motion restriction
    - **3 Root Causes of Errors:** Skill execution failure, rule/protocol violation, knowledge gaps
    - **Error-Prevention Strategies:** Clear protocols, adequate lighting, minimal assessment interruptions, clear med labeling/separation, reflection, questioning assumptions, decision aids, asking for help early, **closed-loop communication** (repeat order back + sender confirms "yes")
  - **EMS Research & Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM):**
    - Replaces anecdotal habit with scientific evidence proving interventions improve survival/recovery
    - **4 Steps of EBM Decision-Making:** (1) Formulate clinical question → (2) search peer-reviewed literature → (3) appraise scientific validity/reliability → (4) update protocols and implement
    - **Challenges:** Time-critical emergencies, informed consent limits, uncontrolled scenes, linking EMS to hospital outcome records
    - **8 Steps of the Scientific Method:** Question → literature review → hypothesis → run study → collect/analyze data → support/reject hypothesis → identify limitations → publish peer-reviewed findings
    - **National Infrastructure:** 2001 *National EMS Research Agenda*; 2014 FICEMS Strategic Goal; **NEMSIS** (national standardized EMS database); **NIGMS** (research funding/collaboration); **CARES** (largest U.S. out-of-hospital cardiac arrest registry linking 911/EMS/hospital survival); NHTSA *Safety in Numbers* (MVC injury analysis)
  - **Evidence-Based Guidelines (EBGs):**
    - Standardized clinical guidelines graded by strength of underlying scientific evidence
    - **8-Step National Prehospital EBG Cyclical Process (FICEMS & NEMSAC):** (1) System inputs (protocols/AHA) → (2) Guideline initiation (evidence accumulation/evaluation) → (3) Establish priorities → (4) Guideline development → (5) EMS protocol development → (6) Dissemination → (7) Implementation → (8) Evaluation of effectiveness/outcomes/QI *(loops back to Step 2)*
    - Key initiatives: 2015 NAEMSP Report (7 objectives/7 action items); NASEMSO Pediatric Pain Management Project

- **5. Public Health & EMS Integration**
  - **Public Health vs. Individual Medical Care:**
    - **Public Health:** Focuses on **populations**; intervenes **before** onset (primary prevention); views disease as multi-factorial (behavioral, biological, social, economic, cultural, environmental, psychological); emphasizes prevention/education/promotion across multidisciplinary community settings
    - **Individual Care:** Focuses on **one patient**; intervenes **after** onset (acute diagnosis/treatment) inside health care facilities/units
    - **APHA:** Vision = *"Healthy people in healthy communities"*; Mission = *"To promote physical and mental health and prevent disease, injury, and disability"*
    - Foundational definitions: C.E.A. Winslow (1920), IOM *The Future of Public Health* (1988), U.S. Public Health Service *Public Health in America* (1994)
  - **Core Public Health Framework (CDC):**
    - **Five Arms:** Epidemiology; biostatistics; health policy and management/administration; behavioral/social science and health education; environmental health
    - **Three Core Functions & 10 Essential Services:**
      - *1. Assessment (epidemiology & biostatistics):* Monitor health status; diagnose/investigate hazards; inform/educate/empower; research innovative solutions
      - *2. Policy Development (all arms):* Mobilize community partnerships; develop supportive policies and plans
      - *3. Assurance (health policy & administration):* Enforce health/safety laws; link people to personal health services; ensure competent workforce; evaluate effectiveness/accessibility/quality
  - **Achievements & Life Expectancy Shifts:**
    - **CDC 10 Greatest Public Health Achievements:** (1) Vaccination, (2) motor vehicle safety, (3) safer workplaces, (4) control of infectious diseases, (5) decline in coronary heart disease/stroke deaths, (6) safer/healthier foods, (7) healthier mothers/babies, (8) family planning, (9) drinking water fluoridation, (10) tobacco hazard recognition
    - **Life Expectancy & Top Killers:** **1900:** **47.3 yrs** (pneumonia/flu, TB, diarrheal diseases) → **2018:** **78.4 yrs** (heart disease, cancer, unintentional injuries) → **2020/2022:** **77.0 yrs** (heart disease, cancer, COVID-19)
  - **Governmental Authority & EMS Public Health Roles:**
    - **Constitutional Authority:** Primary legal responsibility rests with **state and local governments**, supported by federal agencies (CDC, NHTSA, HRSA) and NGOs
    - **Social Determinants of Health (SDOH — 5 Domains):** (1) Economic stability, (2) education access & quality, (3) health care access & quality, (4) neighborhood & built environment, (5) social & community context
    - **Surveillance & Referrals:** EHRs track injury/illness trends; EMTs connect vulnerable patients (human trafficking, child/elder abuse, untreated mental illness) to social/primary care
    - **Three Levels of EMS Prevention:**
      - *Primary (Prevent before onset):* DUI/fall/fire/seatbelt/car-seat/helmet campaigns, community education, flu/COVID-19 vaccinations
      - *Secondary (Early detection / limit progression):* Blood pressure screenings, reporting unsafe homes or abuse/neglect, public-access AEDs, disaster/pandemic planning
      - *Tertiary (Acute treatment / limit disability):* Immediate emergency care, stabilization, and transport
