# Chapter 6: Lifting and Moving Patients
- **1. Body Mechanics, Posture & Teamwork**
  - **Key Terms:**
    - *Mechanics & Posture:* body mechanics, kyphosis, lordosis, power grip, power lift
    - *Move Categories:* emergency move, urgent move, nonurgent move
  - **Golden Priorities:**
    - Choose easiest safe method; let equipment do the work; get maximum help; never risk falling or injuring yourself
  - **Four Basic Principles of Body Mechanics:**
    - **1. Keep weight close to the body:** Reaching far for a light object causes more back injuries than lifting a heavy object held close
    - **2. Engage large lower-body & core muscles:** Drive with legs, hips, glutes, and contracted abs—never back muscles
    - **3. "Stack" and align as a unit:** Keep shoulders stacked over hips and hips over feet; never lift and twist simultaneously
    - **4. Reduce lift height or travel distance:** Move closer, reposition first, or lift in stages
    - *Additional Practices:* Maintain normal inward lumbar curve; keep wrists/knees aligned; substitute equipment for manual force
  - **Posture & Fitness:**
    - **Excessive Lordosis (Swayback):** Stomach too far anterior, buttocks too far posterior → lumbar spine stress
    - **Excessive Kyphosis (Slouch):** Shoulders roll forward → lower-back fatigue and whole-spine pressure
    - **Standing Posture:** Ears, shoulders, and hips vertically aligned; knees slightly bent; pelvis slightly tucked
    - **Sitting Posture:** Weight even across both ischia; ears, shoulders, and hips aligned; feet flat or crossed at ankles; lower back supported
    - **Fitness:** Combine flexibility, cardiovascular conditioning, strength training, and nutrition
  - **Communication & Teamwork:**
    - Match partners of comparable, adequate strength and height; coordinate every lift verbally from start to finish; explain plans to alert patients beforehand so they do not shift mid-lift

- **2. Lifting, Reaching, Pushing & Pulling Techniques**
  - **General Carrying Limits:**
    - Check weight limits; use even number of rescuers; limit two-rescuer heavy carries to **<=1 minute**; use wheeled devices whenever possible
  - **Power Lift & Power Grip:**
    - **Power Grip:** Full palm and finger contact with all fingers bent at the same angle
    - **Power Lift Steps:** Feet shoulder-width apart and slightly outward → bend knees (sit down, don't lean forward) → tighten abs/back with neutral head → straddle object with weight forward of heels → hands **>=10 inches** apart with power grip → drive through heels and arches, raising upper body before hips
  - **Squat Lift & One-Handed Carry:**
    - **Squat Lift (for one weak leg/ankle):** Weaker leg slightly forward with foot flat → squat with power grip → push up with stronger leg, upper body rising before hips
    - **One-Handed Carry:** Stagger feet (one knee up, one down) → bend at hips with trunk tilt **<=45 degrees** → drive up simultaneously through front heel/arch and back ball
  - **Reaching, Pushing & Pulling:**
    - **Reaching Limits:** Effort caps at **100% for 6 seconds** and **50% for 1 minute**; limit forward reach (e.g., log roll) to **15–20 inches** (move closer if `>20 inches`); support weight with free arm; never hyperextend overhead
    - **Pushing vs. Pulling:** Push rather than pull whenever possible; pull with load between shoulders/hips and knees slightly bent; push between waist and shoulder height (kneel for low loads) with elbows bent and arms close; never push/pull overhead

- **3. Patient Moves: Emergency, Urgent & Nonurgent**
  - **Emergency Moves (Immediate Danger to Patient or Rescuer):**
    - *Indications:* Fire/danger of fire, explosives/hazmat, uncontrolled traffic, unstable structures, extreme weather, hostile crowds, or inability to access patient for lifesaving care (CPR, defibrillation, hemorrhage control)
    - *Spinal Rule:* Keep head/neck neutral, align nose with umbilicus, and pull along the **long axis** of the body
    - **3 Techniques:**
      - *Armpit-Forearm Drag:* Reach under armpits from behind, grasp opposite forearms, drag along long axis
      - *Shirt Drag:* Never use on T-shirt alone; fasten wrists/belt if possible, grasp collar/shoulders with head resting on fists, pull engaging armpits without choking neck
      - *Blanket Drag:* Pleat half of blanket/coat lengthwise → roll patient away and tuck under → roll back centered → wrap securely → drag from beneath head
  - **Urgent Moves (Immediate Threat to Life Requiring Rapid Care/Transport):**
    - **Rapid Extrication (MVC):** Indicated for airway/breathing/circulation abnormalities, AMS, shock, head/neck/chest/abdomen/pelvis trauma, bilateral femur fractures, or major bleeding → manual in-line stabilization + C-collar + rotate patient in short coordinated moves onto long backboard → move off backboard onto stretcher/vacuum mattress per protocol
    - **Self-Extrication:** Used to reach a trapped critical patient behind a non-critical patient → apply C-collar, coach neutral head/neck alignment, turn as a unit, and exit
  - **Nonurgent Moves (No Immediate Threat to Life):**
    - *Walking Assist:* Never walk patients who are light-headed/sweaty, or have chest pain, dyspnea, lower-extremity injury, or suspected spinal injury
    - **Direct Ground Lift:** 2–3 rescuers on same side on one knee → arms under head/back, knees/buttocks (and waist) → lift to knees and curl to chests → stand and carry (not recommended for heavy patients)
    - **Extremity Lift:** Contraindicated in spinal or extremity injuries → one rescuer grasps wrists under armpits, second grasps under knees → rise together from crouch
    - **Direct Carry (Bed to Stretcher):** Stretcher perpendicular at foot of bed → lift/curl supine patient to chests → rotate onto stretcher
    - **Draw Sheet Method (Bed to Stretcher):** Loosen bottom sheet, place stretcher alongside bed, brace hips, grasp sheet at head/chest/hips/knees, and slide across

- **4. Packaging, Carrying Devices & Special Transport**
  - **General Packaging Precautions:**
    - Confirm device is locked open; place sheet/blanket first and cover patient; secure straps and tuck loose ends; confirm ambulance floor/wall locks; maintain **Spine Motion Restriction (SMR)** when indicated
  - **Carrying Devices & Weight Ratings:**
    - **Wheeled Stretchers:** Most common/safest; up to **650 lbs**; manual roll-in/self-loading (**~70–95 lbs**) vs. power stretchers (up to **140 lbs**, including "no-lift" horizontal loaders); never leave patient unattended
    - **Bariatric Stretchers:** Wide-deck heavy-duty frames rated up to **850–1,600 lbs** (wheels down) with optional ramp and winch
    - **Portable Stretchers (Soft / Pole Litters):** Up to **350 lbs**; compact for tight spaces; lock pole crosspieces, pad head, avoid rocky ground, no SMR without backboard; `>=4` rescuers preferred
    - **Stair Chairs:** Contraindicated in **AMS, suspected spinal injury, or lower-extremity injury**; head rescuer tilts back, foot rescuer grasps legs, **spotter** counts steps (above going up, below going down); powered tracked models up to **500 lbs**
    - **Backboards & Vacuum Mattresses:** Plastic/composite boards (*Farrington* rounded, *Ohio* tapered); full-body vacuum mattress conforms around patient when air is withdrawn to eliminate voids and restrict spine motion
    - **Scoop Stretchers:** Up to **300 lbs**; splits into halves for confined spaces, pelvic, or bilateral femur fractures; **not recommended for suspected spinal injury**; prewarm metal in cold weather; pad head/bony prominences; `>=3` straps
    - **Basket Stretchers (Stokes) & Flexible Stretchers (Reeves):** Basket accepts backboard/scoop for rough terrain or snow; Reeves has 6 handles for tight hallways/stairs
  - **Carrying on Flat Ground & Stairs:**
    - Minimum **3 straps**; secure patient's hands inside device; **feet first** going **down** stairs/hills; **head first** going **up** stairs/hills
    - **Two-Person Carry:** Stronger rescuer at **head**; foot EMT walks backward (use **spotter** behind foot EMT when going downstairs, ahead of head EMT when going upstairs)
    - **Four-Person Carry:** **Diamond** (1 head, 1 foot facing forward, 2 sides) or **Rectangular** (2 at head sides, 2 at foot sides)
  - **Clinical Positioning, Special Populations & Air Transport:**
    - **Positioning:** Unresponsive non-trauma = **left lateral recumbent**; chest pain/dyspnea = sitting (if normotensive); shock = supine; **pregnancy >20 weeks** = left side or right hip elevated (prevents **supine hypotensive syndrome**); prolapsed cord = supine with hips elevated on pillow
    - **Special Needs:** Infants/toddlers = undamaged car seat padded with rolled towels; older adults = gentle handling for osteoporosis; physical disabilities = pad fused/twisted limbs and ask patient comfort; **Neonatal Isolette** = prevents hypothermia during interfacility transport (locks to ambulance stretcher mount)
    - **Air Medical Packaging (13 Rules):** Full hazmat decon first; intubate before arrival if indicated and keep chest accessible; confirm backboard fits aircraft; secure all loose gear/sheets; cover eyes/ears/wounds; wet dusty LZ; remove loose hats/clothing; wait for pilot/crew signal; stay clear of tail rotor; lay IV bag on chest; minimize personnel under rotors
