# Chapter 3: Medical, Legal, and Ethical Issues — TL;DR

## Professional authority and standards of care

EMTs are accountable to patients, medical direction, and the public. Competence requires both clinical proficiency and understanding the legal and ethical boundaries of care.

| Concept | Practical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Scope of practice** | What a clinician is legally authorized to do. State law, licensure, credentialing, and medical direction determine actual permissions. |
| **Standard of care** | What a reasonably prudent EMT with similar training would do under similar circumstances, including appropriate selection and performance of care. |
| **Medical direction** | Physician oversight of EMS practice through approved protocols, training, credentialing, quality review, and individual consultation. |

The [National EMS Scope of Practice Model][scope] describes EMR, EMT, AEMT, and paramedic levels; the [National EMS Education Standards][education] establish entry-level educational expectations. Neither independently authorizes practice.

**Education → certification → state licensure → system credentialing:** education develops competence, certification verifies specified competencies, licensure grants legal authority, and credentialing establishes individual permissions within a system.

- **Offline medical direction:** standing orders, protocols, education, equipment standards and quality improvement.
- **Online medical direction:** real-time physician consultation and orders for individual patients, especially when care, refusal, or treatment limitations are uncertain.

Follow approved protocols, communicate clearly, clarify unusual orders using read-back when appropriate, and document consultations and resulting care. If communication fails, follow contingency protocols. Interagency cooperation and recordings must follow agency policy. [Physician oversight statement (2017)][oversight].

> [!CAUTION]
> **ALS connection:** EMTs provide foundational assessment, ventilation, hemorrhage control, CPR/AED and selected medications. AEMTs have limited advanced capabilities, such as specified vascular access and medications; paramedics have broader advanced assessment, ECG interpretation, electrical therapy and pharmacology. Assisting an ALS clinician does not authorize performing their skills. A physician's order or partner's request does not expand legal scope.

## Professional duties and ethics

**Duty to act** is a legal obligation to provide service. On-duty EMTs must provide necessary, authorized care to patients who require and consent to it. Off-duty duties vary by state; voluntarily responding or beginning care may establish a duty that continues until lawful release or appropriate transfer. Protect patient property taken into your custody.

| Duty | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Patient | Advocate, protect dignity and confidentiality, relieve suffering, provide competent physical and emotional care, avoid harm. |
| Self | Maintain credentials, clinical proficiency, and physical and mental fitness. |
| Partner | Recognize unsafe impairment and report a partner who is unfit for duty. |
| Equipment | Check readiness at the start of each shift; report failures and use authorized backups. |

Ethics concerns right conduct; the NAEMT Code of Ethics (1978) emphasizes patient welfare, equitable care, honesty, confidentiality, continuous improvement and professional cooperation.

- **Autonomy:** respect informed choices.
- **Beneficence:** act for the patient's benefit.
- **Nonmaleficence:** avoid unnecessary harm.
- **Justice:** allocate care fairly.

Legal, ethical and personal moral obligations can conflict, particularly during end-of-life care or resource-limited triage. Follow applicable law and protocols; seek medical direction when uncertain.

> [!NOTE]
> **ALS connection:** More intervention is not necessarily better care. An advanced airway that unnecessarily interrupts effective CPR may undermine benefit. Mixed-level crews share responsibility for equipment readiness, communication and assigned care; ALS presence does not excuse missed BLS assessments. [AHA ethics and adult ALS (2025)][ahaethics]; [ambulance equipment statement (2021)][equipment]; [AHA adult ALS][ahaals].

## Liability and professional protections

**Criminal liability** involves government prosecution and possible fines or incarceration. **Civil liability** involves claims against clinicians, agencies or other responsible parties, generally seeking compensation. A tort is a civil wrong; negligence involves failure to meet the standard of care rather than deliberate wrongdoing.

### Negligence

A successful civil negligence claim requires all four elements:

1. **Duty:** a legal obligation to provide care.
2. **Breach:** failure to meet the applicable standard of care.
3. **Damages:** actual, legally compensable harm.
4. **Causation:** the breach caused the harm rather than merely occurring alongside the underlying illness or injury.

Simple negligence involves an error or omission; gross negligence involves markedly reckless conduct. Intentional wrongdoing is legally distinct. *Res ipsa loquitur* describes circumstances in which the nature of an injury supports an inference of negligence; *negligence per se* concerns violation of a relevant statute or regulation. Their application varies by jurisdiction.

A poor outcome, complication or protocol deviation alone does not establish all four elements. Document individual observations, interventions, communication and timing, including changes after a partner's treatment.

### Intentional torts

| Tort | Distinguishing conduct |
| --- | --- |
| Abandonment | Improperly ending care without appropriate transfer or lawful release. Moving a patient onto a hospital bed is not a completed handoff. |
| Assault | Threatening bodily harm in circumstances creating apprehension of harm; touching is unnecessary. |
| Battery | Unlawful touching, including unauthorized treatment. |
| False imprisonment | Unlawfully restricting a patient's freedom, including forced transport without consent or legal authority. Physical restraints are not required. |
| Defamation | Statements unlawfully damaging reputation; slander is spoken, libel written or published. |

### Legal protections

Good Samaritan laws may protect good-faith emergency assistance, commonly when unpaid, subject to exceptions such as gross negligence. Sovereign immunity may protect governmental providers subject to statutory exceptions and waivers. Statutes of limitations establish filing deadlines; contributory or comparative fault concerns the patient's contribution to harm.

> [!CAUTION]
> These protections are jurisdiction-specific, not blanket immunity or guarantees against lawsuits. Neither ALS licensure nor medical orders eliminate liability. The strongest practical safeguards are authorized, competent care, accurate documentation, professional conduct and appropriate liability protection.

## Consent, capacity and refusal

A patient with decision-making capacity and legal authority may accept, decline or withdraw consent to individual interventions or transport, even when refusal carries serious risk. Calling 911 is not consent to every aspect of care. Explain proposed assessment, treatment, risks and alternatives in understandable terms, invite questions and obtain permission.

| Form | Application |
| --- | --- |
| Expressed | A legally authorized, capable patient communicates agreement verbally or nonverbally; document it. |
| Implied (emergency doctrine) | Necessary emergency care when a patient cannot decide and delay risks death, disability or deterioration, subject to applicable legal conditions. |
| Minor consent | Generally requires a parent, guardian or legally authorized decision-maker; emergency and emancipation exceptions vary by state. |
| Involuntary / surrogate authorization | Requires actual legal authority when the patient cannot legally determine care; custody alone does not remove medical decision-making rights. |

For a minor whose guardian is unavailable, the [NASEMSO 2022 model][model] supports emergency care when life or health is endangered, consent cannot be obtained in time, and care cannot safely wait. Limit treatment to emergency needs, document circumstances and consult medical direction when authority is uncertain or parental contact is impossible. Pregnancy, marriage or other circumstances do not universally establish emancipation. [Custody guidance (2024; ED-focused)][custody].

### Decision-making capacity

**Capacity** is clinically assessed, specific to the decision, and may fluctuate. **Competence** is a legal determination by a court. Orientation to person, place, time and event helps assess cognition but does not establish capacity.

Four abilities support an informed decision:

1. **Understanding:** explain the problem, proposed care, alternatives and risks.
2. **Appreciation:** recognize how those risks apply personally.
3. **Reasoning:** compare alternatives and consequences.
4. **Communicating a choice:** express a sufficiently clear and consistent decision.

Hypoglycemia, hypoxia, hypoperfusion, head injury and intoxication can impair cognition. Assess mental status, history, vital signs and glucose when authorized; address reversible causes with appropriate care and reassess capacity as the condition changes.

> [!CAUTION]
> An unconventional decision, mental illness, dementia, intoxication or disability does not automatically establish incapacity. Suicidal intent requires urgent safety evaluation, medical direction and applicable behavioral-emergency or involuntary-transport rules. The categorical refusal language in the 2022 national model must be interpreted alongside decision-specific capacity and lawful authority. [High-risk refusal review (2025)][refusal]; [NASEMSO model][model].

### Informed refusal

Obtain the assessment and vital signs the patient permits, explain specific risks (including death when relevant), alternatives and benefits of evaluation, and use teach-back to assess understanding. Address communication barriers and seek medical direction for uncertain or high-risk decisions as required. If capacity is absent, determine the applicable emergency or other legal authority rather than assuming blanket permission.

Document findings, capacity reasoning, care refused, discussions, patient response, consultation, and signatures or witnessed refusal to sign according to policy. The chapter recommends an independent witness other than the partner. Give specific warning signs and encourage the patient to seek help or call EMS again if needed.

**A signature alone does not establish capacity, informed refusal or immunity.** Neither symptom improvement nor a single reassuring set of vital signs excludes a time-sensitive emergency.

> [!NOTE]
> **ALS connection:** Additional assessment (e.g., ECG or authorized metabolic testing) may identify a reversible problem or refine risk, but a normal ECG does not exclude serious disease. Patients can consent to BLS assessment while declining an IV, medication or transport. Reassess consent before additional nonemergent care. [Refusal review][refusal]; [statewide protocol study (2025)][refusalstudy].

> [!WARNING]
> **Restraint:** Use de-escalation and authorized assistance when feasible; restraint or sedation requires lawful authority and clinical indication, never punishment or police convenience. Avoid positions impairing breathing, maintain airway access and close observation, reassess vital signs, and never leave a restrained patient unattended. Pulse oximetry measures oxygen saturation, not ventilation; authorized ALS capnography provides exhaled CO₂ trends. Report deterioration immediately. Do not attempt forced transport alone or outside policy. [Joint restraint statement (2021)][restraint].

Altered cognition in a vulnerable adult also raises safeguarding concerns: obtain objective findings and collateral history where possible, assess the reliability and authority of potential surrogates, consider reversible causes and report suspected neglect as required without assuming intentional abuse.

## Advance directives and end-of-life care

The Patient Self-Determination Act forms part of the legal background for patient autonomy. Advance directives communicate future health care preferences or designate decision-makers when a patient loses capacity.

| Document or order | Primary function |
| --- | --- |
| Living will | Records preferences for future treatment under specified circumstances; may not be readily actionable in an emergency. |
| Durable health care power of attorney | Names a proxy to make decisions when legally authorized; the patient resumes decision-making when capacity returns. |
| DNR | Limits resuscitation under the circumstances specified by the order. |
| DNI | Limits a specified intubation intervention. |
| POLST/MOLST (and regional equivalents) | Portable medical orders for serious illness or advanced frailty; may specify treatment limits before arrest as well as resuscitation preferences. |

Verification, accepted forms, signatures, activation and surrogate authority depend on state law. Do not infer universal physician-count, physical-original or expiration requirements or a fixed one-year POLST eligibility rule. Ohio illustrates the distinction: DNR Comfort Care activates immediately; DNR Comfort Care–Arrest activates at cardiac or respiratory arrest. [Ohio DNR program][ohio]; [National POLST][polst]; [advance care planning][polstacp].

### Applying treatment limitations in the field

- Verify patient identity, accepted documentation, order validity, applicability and specific prohibited interventions.
- If a valid order excludes resuscitation, follow local withholding or discontinuation procedures. If CPR has begun, it may be stopped after appropriate verification under protocol.
- If a patient has signs of life, provide appropriate care consistent with the actual order, including permitted comfort measures.
- If status or authority is unclear, initiate indicated emergency care while promptly seeking clarification and medical direction. Family distress alone does not override a valid order.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **DNR is not do not treat.** Comfort-focused care is not abandonment. Oxygen is not automatically necessary; symptoms, oxygenation, orders and protocol guide use. Authorized ALS analgesia may relieve severe distress but can cause respiratory depression, sedation or hypotension. BLS supports positioning, symptom assessment, airway care and reassessment within the patient's goals and treatment limits. [NASEMSO 2022][model]; [AHA ethics 2025][ahaethics]; [prehospital pain recommendations][pain].

## Transport, transfer and continuity of care

EMTALA, enacted through COBRA, primarily requires covered hospitals to provide emergency screening, stabilization within capability and appropriate transfer, regardless of ability to pay. It addresses patient dumping; independent EMS clinicians are not automatically directly liable under EMTALA merely for accepting a transfer. State law, agency policy, contracts and professional obligations still govern EMS practice. Hospital-owned ambulance and destination rules have specific qualifications. [CMS][cms]; [42 USC §1395dd][emtala].

Choose appropriate destinations according to patient needs, authorized patient choice, medical direction and protocol—not insurance or finances. Document bypass reasons. Before interfacility transfer, obtain the clinical report, verify crew and equipment capability, clarify destination and obtain applicable consent and transfer documentation; paperwork requirements depend on the circumstances.

**Continuity of care** requires a qualified receiving clinician to accept responsibility. Give an oral/structured report covering identity, symptoms and onset, history, assessment and vital trends, interventions and responses, outstanding risks, consent/code status and ongoing devices or medications. Document the accepting clinician and time.

> [!CAUTION]
> **ALS-to-BLS transfer:** Certification level and stable vital signs alone do not determine suitability. Review recent advanced care, anticipated deterioration, ongoing monitoring/infusions, receiving clinician authorization and equipment, downgrade criteria, required consultation and contingency plans. Do not accept sole responsibility for care outside scope. Maintain authorized support during transfers and report interrupted monitoring or displaced devices; do not independently adjust advanced equipment. [Joint handoff statement (2014)][handoff]; [scope model][scope].

## Confidentiality and HIPAA

Confidentiality covers identifiable patient history, examination, treatment and sensitive circumstances. HIPAA (1996) establishes federal privacy protections; follow agency policies for secure records, approved communications, access, storage, and disclosure.

Permitted or required disclosures may include treatment handoffs, billing, legally required reports, and qualifying law-enforcement or court requests. Other releases require appropriate authorization or a valid legal basis. A police request alone is not blanket permission to release a PCR. Refer nonroutine requests and inadvertent disclosures to the privacy officer.

Provider-to-provider treatment disclosures generally do not require separate HIPAA authorization and are generally exempt from the minimum-necessary rule, but safeguards remain essential. Avoid public discussion, personal photographs and unapproved storage; handle recordings and monitor data under policy.

Privacy-notice acknowledgment is not consent to treatment. Emergency care must not be delayed for privacy paperwork; notice may be given once practical after the emergency, with the applicable exception to written acknowledgment efforts. [HHS Privacy Rule][hhsprivacy]; [law enforcement][hhslaw]; [ambulance handoffs][hhstransfer]; [emergency notices][hhsnotice].

## Special clinical and medicolegal situations

### Organ donation and medical identification

Treat the patient first. Donor registration may constitute legally effective authorization; notify the receiving facility or designated donation process, but do not start, prolong or withhold resuscitation solely for possible donation. Eligibility and authorization are handled by the appropriate hospital and organ procurement organization. [HRSA donation][donation]; [donor recovery ethics][donorethics].

Medical alert cards, jewelry and tattoos may suggest allergies, diabetes, epilepsy or medication risks. Report their exact wording and verify clinical findings independently. A tattoo alone may not satisfy legal requirements for a resuscitation-limitation order.

### Recognition of death and resuscitation decisions

Apnea, pulselessness and unresponsiveness indicate cardiac arrest, not necessarily irreversible death. Other presumptive findings include absent reflexes and pupil responses; these alone do not establish irreversibility. Obvious lethal injury, decomposition, rigor mortis or dependent lividity may support withholding under applicable criteria. The national model includes additional specific exclusions and trauma criteria, which require local protocol application.

- **Withholding:** not initiating resuscitation when a recognized exclusion applies.
- **Termination (TOR):** stopping resuscitation already underway using authorized criteria and medical direction as required.
- **Pronouncement:** formal recognition of death by a legally authorized person; determining cause and manner of death is separate.

> [!WARNING]
> If arrest status or a withholding criterion is uncertain, initiate indicated resuscitation and consult medical direction. Hypothermia, drowning, lightning and other special circumstances may alter ordinary criteria. Rewarming guidance cautions against premature declaration, but is not an absolute requirement despite obvious lethal injury or valid care limitations. Field recognition of irreversible death is not formal determination of death by neurologic criteria.

**ALS connection:** ECG reflects electrical activity, not necessarily perfusion; capnography reflects exhaled CO₂ and helps assess ventilation, airway placement and blood-flow trends during CPR. Neither a rhythm nor a single ETCO₂ value establishes futility. Continue quality CPR, ventilation and timely defibrillation until a valid decision is communicated. Document findings, response, times and decision-maker. [AHA adult ALS (2025)][ahaals]; [special circumstances (2025)][ahaspecial]; [NASEMSO model][model].

Notify the medical examiner or coroner under local rules for suspicious, violent, unexpected or otherwise reportable deaths, including suspected abuse, suicide, trauma or sudden infant death.

### Crime scenes and evidence

Ensure scene safety and coordinate with law enforcement before entering unsafe scenes. Minimize unnecessary alterations: use a consistent access route, touch or move only what care requires, avoid cutting through suspected weapon defects or knots, and document necessary changes and evidence transfer.

Patient care takes priority over evidence. Do not delay urgent ventilation, hemorrhage control, exposure or transport; preserve warmth and dignity. In sexual assault cases, explain evidence-preservation options without coercion; a capable patient may decline them. Necessary treatment and basic needs are not conditional on evidence preservation. [DOJ forensic examination guidance (2024)][safe].

### Mandatory reporting and safe-haven laws

Reporting obligations may involve suspected child or elder abuse, domestic violence, crime-related injuries, communicable diseases, restraint or other specified circumstances. Requirements depend on jurisdiction, age and type of harm. Report objective findings to the designated authority when legally required; notifying a partner or hospital alone may not satisfy the duty. Protect privacy and document reporting steps. [HHS Privacy Rule][hhsprivacy].

Baby safe-haven laws permit eligible infants to be surrendered at designated sites under specified conditions. Texas enacted the first law in 1999; the source describes adoption in all states and DC, with varying age limits, sites and procedures. Verify local rules rather than relying on historical examples.

Assess a surrendered infant's warmth, breathing, color, responsiveness and signs of illness or injury. Seek appropriate ALS/neonatal support when unstable; surrender alone does not establish illness or justify unnecessary invasive intervention. [NASEMSO model][model].
