## Read

### Chapter 1: EMS Systems, Research, and Public Health
- **Evolution & Governance:** Early funeral-home ambulances only transported; military trauma care (Korea/Vietnam = field stabilization; Iraq/Afghanistan = resuscitation/hemorrhage control) proved pre-transport stabilization saves lives. Milestones: **1960s–70s** CPR/AHA BLS; **1966** *Accidental Death and Disability* ("White Paper") & Highway Safety Act (DOT/NHTSA); **1973** EMS System Act; **1993** NREMT Blueprint; **1996/2000** *EMS Agendas*; **2005–2006** Core Content, Scope of Practice Model, IOM *EMS at the Crossroads*; **2014–2018** *EMS Agenda 2050*. States regulate EMS via a **lead state EMS agency** (planning, coordination, regulation, licensing) benchmarked by NHTSA's **10 Assessment Standards** and Gunderson's **6 Goals** & **12 Service Lines**.
- **911 Access & Delivery Models:** **PSAP** + **E-911** (**ANI** phone #, **ALI** address) + **EMDs**; Wireless (~70% of calls): Basic 911, Phase I (tower), Phase II (lat/long within **50–300 m**); **VoIP** (registered address). **6 Delivery Models:** Fire Department, Municipal ("Third Service"), Private, Hospital-Based, Law Enforcement, Public Safety Officer.
- **4 Provider Tiers, 10 Specialty Centers & MIH-CP:** **EMR** (first-on-scene basic care) → **EMT** (basic care/transport, pulse ox, auto BP, limited meds) → **AEMT** (advanced airways, BGL, IV/IO, select meds) → **Paramedic** (highest prehospital care). **10 Specialty Centers:** Trauma, Burn, Obstetrical, Pediatric, Poison, Stroke, Cardiac, Hyperbaric, Spine Injury, Psychiatric. **MIH-CP** expands EMS into underserved access, tele-triage, and chronic/post-discharge care.
- **EMT Roles, Medical Oversight, QI & Research:** Safety Priority #1 (Self → Crew → Patient → Bystanders); seat belts unless treating in back; stage until police clear violent scenes; lift/carry up to **125 lbs**; good color vision; **ADA (1990)** reasonable accommodations; deliver verbal + written report before leaving ED. Practice as designated agent of **Medical Director**: **Indirect (Off-Line)** = Protocols & Standing Orders (chest pain: O2 via NRB 15 L/min, comfort position, 1 prescribed nitro if SBP > 90, 160–325 mg aspirin, ALS) vs. **Direct** = On-Line (radio/phone/telemedicine) & On-Scene. **CQI** audits systems (6 EMT roles); **closed-loop communication** prevents errors; **4-step EBM**, **8-step Scientific Method**, **NEMSIS**, **CARES**, **NIGMS**, and **8-step cyclical EBG process**.
- **Public Health & EMS:** Population focus *before* onset (APHA: *"Healthy people in healthy communities"*); CDC **5 arms** & **3 Core Functions / 10 Essential Services** (Assessment, Policy Development, Assurance). Life expectancy: **47.3 yrs (1900)** → **78.4 yrs (2018)** → **77.0 yrs (2020/2022)**; state/local constitutional authority; **5 SDOH domains** (economic stability, education, health care access, neighborhood/built environment, social/community context); **Primary** (vaccines, seatbelts/helmets/DUI), **Secondary** (BP checks, reporting abuse, AEDs), **Tertiary** (acute emergency care).

### Chapter 2: Workforce Safety and Wellness of the EMT
- **Emotional Care & Grief:** Distress exceeding coping capacity = emotional crisis. **Kübler-Ross's 5 Stages:** Denial (*"Not me."*), Anger (*"Why me?"*), Bargaining (*"Okay, but first..."*), Depression (*"Okay, but I haven't..."*), Acceptance (*"Okay, I am not afraid."*). Follow **9 interaction principles** (dignity, respect, clear facts, safe venting, no false assurances; clean postmortem blood/secretions & elevate head briefly if allowed—**except in possible crime scenes**, where body/blood/access must remain undisturbed until cleared by police/coroner/ME).
- **Stress & CISM:** **Acute** (immediate), **Delayed** (days–years; **PTSD**), **Cumulative** (drives **burnout**); 10 warning signs across 5 domains (Thinking, Psychological, Physical, Behavioral, Social). **Defusing** (`<=8 hrs`, `30–45 min`, informal venting) vs. **CISD** (**24–72 hrs**, **7 phases**, confidential/non-punitive) + 10 CISM components.
- **Scene Safety & Infection Control:** Never act outside medical responder role unless trained and authorized. **5 Pathogen Groups:** Bacteria (antibiotic-treatable), Viruses (not antibiotic-treatable; EIDs = COVID-19, Ebola, Zika), Fungi, Protozoa (malaria), Helminths (hookworm).

| Pathogen | Mode of Transmission | Incubation Period | Required Protection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HIV/AIDS** | Blood, semen, vaginal fluid, transfusion, needlestick, transplacental, breastfeeding | Months | Gloves, eyewear, hand washing |
| **Hepatitis B and C** | Blood, semen, vaginal fluid, needlestick, transplacental, human bite, sexual contact | Weeks or months | Gloves, eyewear, hand washing |
| **Tuberculosis** | Respiratory secretions, airborne or direct contact | 2–6 weeks | Gloves, eyewear, HEPA or N-95 respirator, hand washing |
| **Influenza** | Airborne droplets, direct contact with body fluids | 1–3 days | Gloves, surgical mask, hand washing |
| **Chicken pox (varicella)** | Airborne droplets, direct contact with open sores | 11–21 days | Gloves, surgical mask, hand washing |
| **Bacterial meningitis** | Oral and nasal secretions | 2–10 days | Gloves, surgical mask, hand washing |
| **Pneumonia** | Respiratory secretions and droplets | 1–3 days | Gloves, surgical mask, hand washing |
| **German measles (rubella)** | Airborne droplets, transplacental | 10–12 days | Gloves, surgical mask, hand washing |
| **Whooping cough (pertussis)** | Respiratory secretions, airborne droplets | 6–20 days | Gloves, surgical mask, hand washing |
| **Staphylococcal skin infection** | Direct contact with infected lesion or contaminated object | 1–3 days | Gloves, hand washing |
| **SARS coronavirus / COVID-19** | Respiratory airborne, direct contact | SARS: 10 days; COVID-19: 3–7 days | Gloves, N-95 or N100 respirator, hand washing, eye protection |

| **Zika virus disease (ZVD)** | *Aedes* mosquito bite, pregnant woman to fetus, sexual contact | 3–12 days | Avoiding mosquito bites, abstaining from sex with infected/potentially infected partner (condoms reduce risk), preventing pregnancy |

- **Standard Precautions, PPE, Decontamination & Injury Prevention:** Airborne exposure (TB) reported to DO within **24 hours**; bloodborne exposure (HIV/HBV) within **48 hours**. **6-step CDC hand wash** (`>=20 sec` scrub) or `>=60%` alcohol sanitizer; treat all used PPE as contaminated; HEPA/N-95 for TB/COVID-19 (clean-shaven, annual fit test); wash soiled uniforms `>=25 min` in hot soapy water; **Cleaning** → **Disinfecting** → **Sterilization** (autoclave); never recap sharps; PPD TB screen + COVID-19, Tdap q10y, Hep B (offered free within **10 days**), annual flu, MMR (born `>=1957`), varicella. Scout hazmat with binoculars + DOT *ERG*; **ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2/3** or **207 PSV** vests (`Class 1 <25 mph`, `Class 2 >=25 mph`); stage for police on violent/crime scenes.
- **Wellness & Glove Removal:** **6 Wellness Dimensions** & **5 Fitness Components** (cardio **30–40 min, >=3 days/wk**; `Target HR = [((220 - Age) - RHR) × 0.70] + RHR`; 28 y/o, RHR 62 = **153 bpm**); sleep **8–10 hrs/day** (half get `<6 hrs`; 5 NASEMSO guidelines, shifts **<24 hrs**); `>30%` first responders have mental health diagnosis; untreated **PTSD** = **6x** suicide risk (**1-800-273-8255**). **7-Step Glove Removal:** Contaminated touches contaminated only; clean touches clean only.

### Chapter 3: Medical, Legal, and Ethical Issues
- **Scope, Standards, Duties & Ethics:** **Scope of practice** (state law: legal actions) vs. **Standard of care** ("reasonable person standard": expected quality). **Duty to act** applies on duty and whenever stopping/responding off duty (cannot leave until equal/higher training takes over; responsible for picked-up property); duties to patient, self, partner (report impairment), and equipment. **Good Samaritan laws** (CA **1959**; unpaid good-faith care unless **gross negligence**); **Sovereign immunity** (government EMS only); Statute of limitations; Contributory negligence; NAEMT Code of Ethics (**1978**).
- **Consent, Directives & Refusals:** **5-step consent approach**; **4 Consent Types:** **Expressed** (rational adult), **Implied** (unresponsive/irrational emergency doctrine), **Minor** (`<18/21`, 6 emergent conditions, emancipated minor criteria), **Involuntary** (mentally incompetent or law enforcement custody). **Advance Directives:** **Living Will** (**2 physicians** confirm terminal/comatose), **Durable Power of Attorney** (**1 physician** certifies incapacity), **DNR / DNRCC** (valid physical document required; act only in cardiac arrest), **POLST/MOLST** (`<=1 yr` prognosis, pre-arrest treatment limits). **Refusals:** Medical **capacity** vs. legal **competence**; **7-step refusal protection** (assess, re-persuade, verify capacity, consult medical direction, stay safe, warning signs, thorough documentation + signed release/witness).
- **Torts, Privacy, Federal Laws & Special Situations:** Criminal vs. Civil (**plaintiff** sues **defendants**); **Negligence (4 elements):** Duty to act + Breach (*simple* vs. *gross*) + Damages + Proximate cause (*res ipsa loquitur*, *negligence per se*); **5 Intentional Torts:** Abandonment, Assault, Battery, False Imprisonment, Defamation (**Slander** = spoken, **Libel** = written). **HIPAA (1996)** (5 disclosures without consent: continuing care, mandatory reporting, police investigation, billing, subpoena); **COBRA/EMTALA** ("anti-patient-dumping") + **5 interfacility transfer safeguards**. Organ donors (patient first, donor last); **accidental hypothermia** exception; **5 presumptive** vs. **4 obvious** signs of death; **2022 NASEMSO** 8 biological exclusions; crime-scene evidence rules; **Baby Safe-Haven laws** (TX **1999**, 3 days to 1 yr).

### Chapter 4: Documentation
- **Goals, Minimum Data Set & NEMSIS:** **3 Goals** (continuity of care, legal protection, reimbursement) & **5 Functions**; *"If it wasn’t written down, it wasn’t done"* & *"If it wasn’t done, don’t write it down."* **U.S. DOT Minimum Data Set:** **8 Patient Elements** (`BP >3 yrs`, `cap refill <6 yrs`) + **6 Administrative Elements** + **synchronous timekeeping**. **5 Core PCR Sections:** Admin info, demographics, vitals (**>=2 complete sets** with position/times), narrative (chief complaint in quotes, objective vs. pertinent subjective, **pertinent negatives**), and chronological treatments/responses. **2022 NASEMSO / NEMSIS v3.4:** Mandatory timestamps (**unit notified** & **back in service**); **Primary impression** = most acute problem; **Tiered vitals** (Routine → Initial → Full); aspirin **324 mg** PTA = *"medication already taken"*; procedure attempts = **"1"**; **Mandatory** vs. **Required** fields (`d` and `e` datasets).
- **Narratives, Abbreviations, Legal & Reimbursement:** **Medical Narrative**, **SOAP/SOAPIE**, **CHART/DCHART**, **CHEATED**. Never invent abbreviations; writing terms out is preferred (`PE` = physical exam or pulmonary embolism). **8-Step Refusal**; **Zero Falsification** (**False Claims Act**); paper before = single line + initials; paper after = single line + note + initials + date/time + notify; ePCR = timestamped amendment; **Drop reports**, **MCI triage tags**, **Special reports**. **Medical Necessity:** Commercial (**prudent layperson**), Medicare (**42 CFR § 410.40(d)**), Medicaid (state rules), **ET3** (CMS/HHS 2019, launched Jan 5, 2020).

### Chapter 5: Communication
- **Hardware, FCC & Radio Rules:** **1973 EMS Act** (1 of 15 components) & **2012 FirstNet**; **Base Station** (80–150 W), **LMRS** (700–800 MHz), **Mobile** (20–50 W, 10–15 mi), **Portable** (1–5 W), **Repeaters**, **Encoder/Decoder**, **MDT**, **Cellular** (2–40 sq mi), **Telemetry**, **Telemedicine**, **Satellite**; **FCC** licensing/rules + daily maintenance. **11-Step Progression & 16 Radio Rules:** Wait **1 sec** after PTT, mic **2–3 in** from lips, pause at **30 sec**, plain English, no patient names, **closed-loop echo** repeat-back, *"we"*, *"affirmative/negative"*, *"Over"*. **AACN**; **9 dispatch points**; **24-hr military time**; **SBAR**; pre-transport radio report vs. in-person oral handoff; scene authority transfers.
- **Therapeutic & Nonverbal Communication:** **Sender → Encoding → Receiver → Decoding → Feedback** loop + **9 therapeutic responses** (Facilitation, Clarification, Summary, Explanation, Silence, Reflection, Empathy, Confrontation, Facilitated Communication). **Three Cs** (Competence, Confidence, Compassion); open posture, eye level, **Intimate (`<1½ ft`) / Personal (`1½–4 ft`) / Social (`4–12 ft`) / Public (`>=12 ft`)**, gestures, eye contact, **haptics**. **Open-ended** vs. **Closed-ended** (avoid **leading questions**); overcome **ethnocentrism**; elderly, pediatric (truth + eye level), hostile patients, **Family-Centered Care (1987)**.

### Chapter 6: Lifting and Moving Patients
- **Body Mechanics, Lifting & Moves:** **4 Principles:** Keep weight close; engage legs/hips/glutes/abs; stack shoulders/hips/feet (never lift & twist); reduce lift height/distance; avoid **lordosis** & **kyphosis**. **Power Lift & Power Grip** (hands `>=10 in` apart, drive through heels/arches, upper body before hips); **Squat Lift** (weak leg forward); **1-Hand Carry** (`<=45°` tilt); heavy 2-rescuer carry `<=1 min`; 100% effort `6 sec`, 50% effort `1 min`; forward reach `15–20 in`; push over pull. **3 Move Categories:** **Emergency** (Armpit-Forearm, Shirt, Blanket drags along long axis), **Urgent** (Rapid & Self-Extrication), **Nonurgent** (Walking assist, Direct Ground Lift, Extremity Lift, Direct Carry, Draw Sheet).

| Carrying Device | Weight Rating / Spec | Key Uses, Advantages & Contraindications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wheeled Stretcher** | Up to **650 lbs** | Primary transfer device; manual roll-in (**~70–95 lbs**) vs. power (**up to 140 lbs**, including "no-lift" horizontal loaders); load hanging stretchers first; never leave patient unattended |
| **Bariatric Stretcher** | **850–1,600 lbs** | Wide deck, heavy-duty frame, stability wheels, optional ramp and winch |
| **Portable Stretcher** | Up to **350 lbs** | Soft/pole litter for confined spaces; lock crosspieces, pad head, avoid rocky ground, no SMR without backboard; `>=4` rescuers preferred |
| **Stair Chair** | Up to **500 lbs** *(powered)* | Best for stairs/narrow hallways; **contraindicated** in AMS, suspected spinal injury, or lower-extremity injury; spotter counts steps (above going up, below going down) |
| **Backboard / Vacuum Mattress** | Varies | Boards (*Farrington* rounded, *Ohio* tapered) aid extrication; full-body vacuum mattress conforms rigid around body when air is withdrawn to eliminate voids and restrict spine motion |
| **Scoop Stretcher** | Up to **300 lbs** | Splits into halves for tight spaces/pelvic/bilateral femur fractures; **not recommended for suspected spinal injury**; prewarm metal in cold weather; pad head/bony prominences; `>=3` straps |
| **Stokes Basket / Reeves** | Varies | Stokes (wire/polyethylene) accepts backboard/scoop for rough terrain/snow; Reeves flexible stretcher (6 handles) for tight hallways/stairs |

- **Carrying, Stairs, Positioning & Air Transport:** `>=3` straps; hands secured inside; **feet first down / head first up**; stronger EMT at head + **spotter**; Diamond vs. Rectangular 4-person carry. Unresponsive non-trauma = **left lateral recumbent**; shock = supine; **pregnancy `>20 wks`** = left side or right hip elevated (**supine hypotensive syndrome**); prolapsed cord = hips elevated; **Neonatal Isolette**; **13 Air-Medical Packaging Rules** (hazmat decon first, avoid tail rotor, IV bag on chest).
